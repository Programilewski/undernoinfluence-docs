V1 · Pre-launch hardening

Under No Influence (UNI) is a hand-curated map and directory of venues with genuine alcohol-free menus, launching in Warsaw. This document is the single source of truth — it describes what is actually built and locked for V1 (not an aspirational design), and why those choices fit the alcohol-free on-premise opportunity. The summary below is the 10-second version; everything else is detail.

What UNI is

  • Curated, anti-scraping venue directory + map (/mapa) for alcohol-free options — quality over coverage.
  • V1 is browse-only: no public auth, no accounts. Admin runs in Filament (/admin); the owner panel exists but is gated.
  • Stack: PHP 8.4, Laravel 13, Livewire 4, Filament 5, PostgreSQL, Tailwind 4.
  • City-agnostic in code (multi-city flag), Warsaw first; programmatic SEO by city/district/category.
  • GDPR-careful: EU-resident services, genuinely anonymous server-side analytics, consent-gated PostHog.

  • Tailscale + Nginx admin gate — /admin still publicly reachable.

Terminology Glossary — Canonical Term Usage

Multiple terms exist for alcohol-free drinks across research sources, public UI, and internal code. This document uses the following canonical mapping:

  • bezalkoholowe — canonical Polish UI term for all alcohol-free options (public-facing; used on all pages, filters, labels)
  • alcohol-free / NA — canonical internal/B2B English term; used in code, this spec, and B2B outreach
  • no/low, NoLo — market research citation term only (IWSR, NIQ); never used in public UI copy
  • 0.0% — strict-zero products only (abv_status = verified_zero AND abv = 0.0); see Market Evidence → ABV Trust Model and Filters → Tylko 0.0%
  • bezalkoholowe, ABV do potwierdzenia — public label for products with abv_status = unknown
  • Owner — a venue owner with a claimed and admin-approved listing; has role='owner' and access to /panel
  • Admin — UNI team member with role='admin'; accesses /admin via Filament
  • Venue — a public listing of type restauracja, pub, or bar; see Market Evidence → Venue Type Scope

Market Evidence & Positioning
What the NIQ, IWSR, and OPUS Poland sources imply for UNI
Positioning thesis

UNI is not a sober directory and not a generic beverage map. It is a trust-based discovery product for real venues where alcohol-free beer, wine, cocktails, spirits alternatives, cider, and sparkling drinks are credible enough for a social outing. The strongest audience is not only abstainers; it is moderators, substituters, blenders, strict-zero users, and mixed social groups.

Source Signal UNI Decision
NIQ US non-alcohol reports, 2022-2025 Non-alcohol beer, wine, and spirits grew from roughly $395M in 2022 to $925M in 2025 in tracked US off-premise data; the category remains small but fast-growing. Treat alcohol-free as a serious category, but keep V1 focused on discovery and trust rather than broad lifestyle content.
NIQ EMEA On-Premise, 2025 34% of EMEA consumers report drinking less alcohol out-of-home; 63% of global consumers fall into a moderator bracket. Most are reducing or switching by occasion, not fully abstaining. Position UNI around moderation, social participation, and mixed groups, not anti-alcohol messaging.
NIQ Poland On-Premise, 2025 Polish restaurants, cafes, QSR, cinemas, bars/pubs, festivals, and special occasions appear in broad on-premise behavior, but beer remains the top drink and 72% of pub/bar consumers are open to influence. Use the broad channel data as market context, but keep V1 supply limited to restaurants, pubs, and bars. UNI should influence choice before arrival through SEO, categories, freshness, and venue pages.
OPUS Poland two-pager OPUS tracks Polish on-premise visit frequency, spend, reasons for visit, need states, path to purchase, brands, flavours, serves, and occasions across 5,000+ consumers. Validate the future B2B analytics layer: gap reports, category demand, competitor context, and path-to-purchase insights are coherent owner-facing products.
IWSR no/low research, 2023-2024 Substituters and blenders switch between full-strength, no-alcohol, and low-alcohol depending on occasion. Availability remains a major barrier to more frequent no/low consumption. Fresh, precise availability is UNI's moat. V1 stays alcohol-free / under 0.5% and defers broader low-alcohol above 0.5%.
What This Means for B2C
SSOT
Users need to know where they can go, what category is available, whether the data is fresh, and whether a product is verified zero, under 0.5%, exact non-zero, or still unconfirmed. Venue type is identity/context metadata; drink categories and freshness remain the main discovery axes.
What This Means for B2B
SSOT
The owner pitch is not moral. It is commercial: guests are moderating, mixed groups still go out, and venues with credible alcohol-free options can capture demand. UNI's future paid layer should turn first-party search and engagement data into category gaps, demand rankings, competitor context, and timely update nudges.
Venue Type Scope for V1
LOCKED
The reports mention cafes, QSR, cinemas, festivals, bars/pubs, restaurants, and special occasions as broad on-premise channels. UNI should not copy that whole channel list into V1. The launch product needs venue types where alcohol-free options affect a social outing decision and where product-level menu verification is practical.
  • Public V1 venue types: restauracja, pub, bar.
  • Restauracja: food-led sit-down venue where alcohol-free products or house drinks matter for meals, dates, and group occasions.
  • Pub: pub identity, beer-forward or neighborhood drinking culture, or venue explicitly brands itself as a pub. This is not merged with bar because Warsaw search behavior and real venue identity distinguish pubs from cocktail/sky/wine bars.
  • Bar: cocktail bar, sky bar, wine bar, hotel bar open to outside guests, or other drink-led venue where "bar" is the natural public label.
  • Hotels: not a public venue type. A hotel bar or hotel restaurant can be listed as bar or restauracja only when outside guests can visit without booking a room.
  • Excluded from V1 public taxonomy: cafes, cinemas, festivals/events, QSR/fast food, markets/street food, nightclubs, generic hotels. These can be reconsidered only after core venue discovery proves demand.
  • inne: internal admin/import fallback only. It is not a public trust label; any public venue must be reclassified before publication.
ABV Trust Model for V1
LOCKED
Warsaw venue reality includes both true 0.0% products and products marketed as alcohol-free up to 0.5%. Excluding the latter would make venue pages visibly incomplete; pretending unknown products are zero would destroy trust. V1 therefore supports both alcohol-free tiers plus an honest unknown state.
  • Platform language: bezalkoholowe remains the umbrella label.
  • Schema contract: products.abv must be nullable and paired with abv_status: verified_zero, verified_under_0_5, verified_exact, unknown.
  • Public labels: 0.0%, <=0.5%, exact {n}%, or bezalkoholowe, ABV do potwierdzenia.
  • Strict-zero eligibility: only abv_status === verified_zero and abv === 0.0. Unknown and up-to-0.5 products are excluded from Tylko 0.0%. Implemented in discovery as Filters → Tylko 0.0% →. See also D-04: ABV Trust Model.
  • Import/create rule: abv=0.0 is invalid unless abv_status=verified_zero. Unverified alcohol-free products use abv=null and abv_status=unknown.
  • Out of scope: low-alcohol products above 0.5%.

Scoring Logic
How venues are ranked — the algorithm behind the default sort in Discovery /mapa →. Breadth as the primary signal is justified by Market Evidence → IWSR: availability is the key barrier →. See also D-05: Breadth Score Decision.
Breadth Score
P0
Primary ranking signal. Count of total distinct products (catalog products + custom house drinks). Stored as a denormalized integer column venues.breadth_score. Recalculated by VenueScoring PHP service class triggered via VenueProductObserver and VenueDrinkObserver — NOT a DB trigger.
Rationale — Why Breadth?

IWSR research identifies availability as the main barrier to more frequent no/low consumption. Breadth (number of distinct options) is the best available proxy for "this venue takes alcohol-free seriously." A venue with 12 alcohol-free options across 4 categories is more likely to serve a mixed group than one with 2 options. Rarity-weighted scoring (rewarding distinctive over commodity products) is deferred to V3 when stable product distribution data exists. See Market Evidence → IWSR signal and D-05.

  • 5 beers + 3 wines + 2 cocktails + 1 spirit = breadth_score: 11
  • VenueScoring::calculate() returns: breadth by category, total breadth, categories used
  • VenueScoring::recalculate() persists to DB
  • Indexed column → fast ORDER BY without live count query
Category-Aware Sort
P0
When a category filter is active (e.g. "piwo"), venues are sorted first by how many products they carry in that category (category_breadth, a SQL subquery computed by VenueQuery::categoryBreadthExpression()). Overall breadth_score is the second sort key. Handles multi-category selection (sums across selected slugs) and the special drinki category (counts custom drinks from venue_drinks). Without category filter, falls through to overall breadth as primary sort.
Category Diversity Tiebreaker
P0
When breadth scores are equal, venues with more distinct categories rank higher. Computed by VenueScoring::sort(): sorts by total breadth DESC, then categories used DESC. Implemented as P0 (spec said P1).
Freshness Modifier
P0
Computed via freshness_state accessor on Venue model. Fresh = offer_updated_at within 90 days. Stale = beyond 90 days. Unknown = null. Visual indicator on venue cards and profile pages. Freshness filter on discovery page lets users filter by recency (7/30/90/180 days).
Admin Verified Flag
P0
Manual admin toggle: venues.is_verified. UNI team has independently verified this venue. Separate from freshness (automated) and claimed status (owner action). Shown as the "Sprawdzona karta" badge (renamed 15.08). The badge expires: it only renders while last_menu_check_at is inside uni.verification_valid_days (180 days) — is_verified alone never expires, so on its own it would keep promising a check nobody redid. Discovery page has a filter toggle (sprawdzone) for these venues. Implemented as P0 (spec said P1).

Interactive Map
P0
Leaflet.js map with multi-city support. Venue markers use MarkerCluster for grouping nearby pins (maxClusterRadius: 50). Click a marker to open venue detail popup (name, type, verified badge, address, category breakdown, freshness label, link to profile). Popup and tooltip text is escaped before insertion into Leaflet HTML; category colors/SVGs are constrained before rendering. Map bounds filtering supported.
  • Rich pin markers: show top category icon + count, verified indicator; selected pin highlighted. Filter-aware: when category filters are active, the marker icon reflects the filtered category (or the most popular among selected categories), not the globally most popular one
  • Category breakdown per venue visible in marker popup with icons and colors
  • Map state syncs with active filters — filtered-out venues disappear from map. Active category slugs dispatched to JS via venues-updated event for filter-aware marker rendering
  • Geographic bounds search: drag map to filter venues within visible area ("Szukaj w tym obszarze" button)
  • User geolocation supported for "Nearest" sort mode
  • Mobile: list/map toggle (not split view), desktop: 3/5 list + 2/5 map side-by-side
  • Desktop hover tooltip: venue name with white text on dark background, elevated above marker to avoid blending
  • Dark/light tile switching: CARTO dark tiles in dark mode, OSM standard in light mode
  • Map bounds constrained to Poland (lat 49–54.9, lng 14.1–24.2)
  • Mobile drag optimization: marker cluster loading is chunked, latest Livewire venue payload is cached client-side, map auto-fit is skipped while the active mobile map is open, and drag-time CSS suppresses expensive shadows/filters without removing valued animations
  • Unloaded tile background is theme-aware: black in dark mode, neutral blue-grey in light mode, so tile loading during drag no longer flashes white/blank
  • Accessibility: map container is a labelled role="region" with a skip link to the venue list; role="application" was intentionally avoided because UNI does not implement a full custom application keyboard model

Admin Access Security — Tailscale VPN
Decision: DECIDED — pending implementation. The /admin panel will be restricted to devices on the founder's private Tailscale network. Not yet deployed.
Why this decision was made

The /admin Filament panel manages all platform data — venues, claims, users, products. Leaving it reachable on the public internet means it relies solely on Filament's login form as the only barrier. A Tailscale + Nginx layer adds infrastructure-level access control that is cryptographically enforced and entirely invisible to regular app users. Evaluated and rejected: a separate admin application (overkill for a solo founder pre-launch — same database, double the deployment surface, no structural security benefit over VPN + Nginx).

How It Works
Architecture Overview
Two completely separate traffic paths coexist on the same server. Regular users never interact with Tailscale at any point.
  • Public path: 10,000 users → public internet → Hetzner public IP → Nginx → Laravel app. Tailscale is invisible to this traffic.
  • Admin path: founder's laptop/phone → Tailscale encrypted tunnel → Hetzner Tailscale IP (100.x.x.x) → Nginx checks source IP → allow → /admin → Filament login.
  • The Hetzner production server joins the founder's Tailscale tailnet, giving it a second private network interface (tailscale0) alongside the public one (eth0). Public traffic uses eth0 as always — no performance overhead, no bottleneck.
Trust Chain — Three Independent Layers
Access to /admin requires passing all three independently:
  1. Tailscale device authentication — only devices explicitly approved by the founder into the tailnet can establish a WireGuard tunnel. Tailscale uses WireGuard, a modern audited VPN protocol. Device approval happens via Tailscale's control plane (Google/GitHub SSO or email).
  2. Nginx IP allowlist — source IP must be in the Tailscale CGNAT range 100.64.0.0/10. This range is not routable on the public internet — it cannot be spoofed from outside the encrypted tunnel. Any request arriving with a public source IP receives 403 Forbidden before reaching PHP.
  3. Filament authentication — standard username + password login form on /admin. Only users with UserRole::Admin can pass canAccessPanel().
Why Tailscale Membership = Trusted IP
Tailscale is a mesh VPN — it only tunnels traffic between devices that are both on the tailnet. When the founder's laptop connects to the production server via Tailscale, the request travels through a WireGuard-encrypted tunnel and arrives at Nginx with source IP 100.x.x.x. A random person on the internet cannot fake this source IP because their request never goes through the Tailscale tunnel. The 100.x.x.x source IP is a cryptographic guarantee, not just an IP claim.
Implementation Plan
Production Server Setup
The Hetzner VPS provisioned via Laravel Forge needs to join the tailnet once. This is a one-time operation:
  • Install Tailscale on the server: curl -fsSL https://tailscale.com/install.sh | sh
  • Authenticate and join tailnet: tailscale up
  • Server receives a permanent 100.x.x.x Tailscale IP and a MagicDNS hostname
  • tailscaled runs as a system daemon — always on, zero overhead to public app traffic
Nginx Configuration via Laravel Forge
Forge exposes the site Nginx config directly in its UI. No SSH or manual file editing required. Add an IP allowlist scoped to the /admin location block inside the existing server {} block. Forge reloads Nginx automatically on save.
location /admin {
    allow 100.64.0.0/10;  # Tailscale CGNAT range
    deny all;
    try_files $uri $uri/ /index.php?$query_string;
    # ... rest of existing proxy/fastcgi config
}
Tailnet Devices (Free Tier)
Tailscale's free tier limits apply to devices on the tailnet, not to app traffic or end users. Regular app users never touch Tailscale. The tailnet will contain:
  • Founder's laptop
  • Founder's phone (optional)
  • Hetzner production server

That is 2–3 devices — well within the free tier (100 devices). App users do not appear on the tailnet at any point.

Privacy Policy Consideration
Tailscale running on the server is an infrastructure/ops tool. It does not inspect, process, or store any user data — it only handles the encrypted admin→server tunnel between the founder's devices. Under GDPR, Tailscale would fall under the "infrastructure and hosting providers" catch-all rather than as a user data processor. Most existing privacy policies already cover this. A note under infrastructure providers is sufficient if desired — a dedicated disclosure is not required.
Known Limitations
  • Stolen device risk: if a device connected to Tailscale is stolen while authenticated, the attacker gains network access. Mitigated by removing the device from the tailnet via the Tailscale admin console immediately.
  • Tailscale control plane dependency: Tailscale's coordination servers are required for new device authentication. If they go down, existing connected devices continue to work but new devices cannot join. Admin access remains available via any already-authenticated device.
  • Not a substitute for Filament auth: Tailscale + Nginx is a network-level gate, not application-level auth. Filament's own login must remain enabled and use a strong password.

  • Admin UI: "Analytics plan" dropdown on venue edit form. "Feature flags (overrides)" tab on venue edit page — shows active overrides, allows adding comps/beta/partner access with optional expiry date and notes, records which admin set each override.

Transition path to automated plans: When paid tiers launch, add a payment/subscription event that calls $venue->update(['plan' => VenuePlan::Basic->value]). Progressive Disclosure Upsell — Reveal Signal, Gate Resolution

Each paid plan should deliver real value while exposing the next useful business question. The lower tier shows that a signal exists; the next tier reveals enough detail to make a better decision. This is not arbitrary paywalling. It is a product ladder: signal → cause → action + timing → automation.

Why: owners are more likely to upgrade when they understand the decision they cannot make yet. "Search spike detected" is weak by itself; "Search spike detected, upgrade to see which category and how to respond" connects the locked data to revenue action. The tease must be based on a real computed signal, never fabricated sample demand.

  • Free → Basic: Free shows profile performance and catalog health only. It may show at most 1–2 compact paid teasers per page, e.g. "Demand signals exist in your city" without category names, volumes, or trend details. Free must not become a locked-card nag wall.
  • Basic → Pro: Basic can show the category/demand gap for the owner's venue. Pro unlocks competitive context: which nearby venues are capturing that demand, what they added, and whether the district supply is changing.
  • Pro → Premium: Pro shows competitive/demand context. Premium unlocks proactive monitoring, delivery timing, engagement deep dives, alerts, and shareable proof assets.
  • Premium: Do not show a fake "higher analytics tier" above Premium. Upsell service add-ons only: exports, automated alerts, account review, distributor/reporting products, or custom analysis.

Guardrail: teasers must name the blocked decision, not only blur curiosity numbers. Prefer "Upgrade to see which category and how to respond" over "Upgrade to see 47%." If there is no real signal over the volume threshold, show no teaser.

Disclosure mechanics: it is acceptable to blur, mask, or truncate a specific value only after the real signal exists. Examples: "Demand for cocktails in Żoliborz is up this week" with the percentage/chart blurred; "3 competitors added new items nearby" with competitor names hidden; "Your profile appeared in searches this month" with the exact count masked. The masked value must be real and available immediately after upgrade.

Conversion copy rule: every teaser must explain the business decision the owner cannot make yet. "We detected a demand gap in your district. Upgrade to see which category and how to respond" is stronger than a blurred percentage because it connects the locked detail to an operational action.

All first-party data collection prerequisites met as of 2026-05-14

discovery_empty_results event
LIVE
Fires in DiscoveryPage::render() when $venues->isEmpty() AND at least one filter is active. Properties: { query, categories[], cities[], districts[], sort, freshness, has_bounds }. Written to the local events table through AnalyticsGateway; optional PostHog sink is config-gated.

LIVE
Fires server-side in DiscoveryPage through AnalyticsGateway when categories change. Includes result_count, selected categories, and selected city state. Captures category demand whether results were found or not.

LIVE
Per-venue daily aggregated counters in venue_stats: views, direction_clicks, website_clicks, instagram_clicks, card_clicks. All server-side, rate-limited (10/min per IP+venue+type), tamper-proof. Incremented via VenueStat::track(venueId, column).

LIVE
First-party duration events sent from resources/js/venue-analytics.js to POST /analytics/events. Tracks visible menu time and open category accordion dwell time. Pauses when the tab is hidden, flushes on visibility/pagehide, ignores sub-2-second noise, caps at 900 seconds, and validates venue/category server-side.

venue_card_impressed + venue_card_clicked events
LIVE
Client-side PostHog only. venue_card_impressed via IntersectionObserver (50% threshold, 500ms dwell). venue_card_clicked on card click. Gives impression-to-click conversion rate per venue.

venue_products + venue_offer_logs tables
LIVE
Current catalog state (venue_products) and immutable change history (venue_offer_logs: polymorphic, actions: product_added / product_removed / drink_added / drink_removed). Together these record what each venue carries and when it changed.

BASIC ANCHOR

Powered by discovery_empty_results events cross-referenced with the venue's current product catalog. Loss-framed, locally specific, with a clear action (add a product from the catalog). The best-performing insight type in B2B SaaS: quantified lost revenue + specific fix.

Volume Thresholds — When to Surface Each Report

Early-stage low user volume means small numbers. "3 people searched for Wino 0% in Mokotów" doesn't justify a paid tier. Do not surface a district-level gap report until the threshold is met — use the citywide proxy instead.

  • Gap report — district level: ≥ 10 searches for that category in the venue's district in trailing 30 days
  • Gap report — citywide fallback: below 10 in district → aggregate citywide, show if ≥ 25 city-level searches. Frame as "your district contributed to this demand."
  • Gap report — suppress entirely: below 25 citywide for that category that month. Silence is better than an unimpressive number.
  • Competitor alerts: minimum 3 other claimed venues in the proximity radius before firing (avoids alerting on the venue themselves or a single outlier)
  • Trend reports: minimum 60 days of data in both comparison periods before computing month-over-month. No trend in month 1.
  • Search spike alerts: minimum 15 absolute searches in the spike week regardless of percentage increase (prevents triggering on 2 → 4 = "100% spike" noise)
  • Breadth percentile: fall back to city-wide if fewer than 5 venues in district — district ranking with 2 venues is meaningless

Target locale — Polish (single locale)

UNI is a single-locale Polish application; there is no language switcher and no per-user locale. The app locale is set in .env: APP_LOCALE=pl and APP_FALLBACK_LOCALE=pl. This was previously en, which silently left every framework-generated string (Filament UI chrome, validation, pagination, e-mail boilerplate) in English even though our own labels were Polish. Flipping it to pl is what makes Filament render its built-in UI in Polish.

Convention for new strings

Follow the existing pattern: inline Polish string literals in Filament resources, Blade views, notifications, and enum labels — not translation keys. There is no __('key') indirection layer for our own copy. Translation files (lang/pl/*, lang/pl.json) exist only to translate framework / vendor strings that we don't author. When you add a Filament field, always set an explicit Polish ->label(); never rely on Filament's auto-generated label (it humanizes the English column name).

What is in Polish

Coverage

Public site (Blade views), the venue-owner panel (labels + Filament chrome), all admin moderation queues (claims, inaccuracy reports, product proposals, erasure requests), the entire admin catalog (Venues, Products, Brands, Producers, Categories, Users + their forms, tables, filters, actions, importer), the admin dashboard widgets, and the venue-analytics screens (admin diagnostics page, owner-analytics preview, and the analytics widgets). The VenueAnalyticsReportService emits Polish user-facing text too (day-of-week abbreviations, seasonal-forecast notes). Notification e-mails are fully Polish end to end.

Display-only translation of enum/value codes

String codes that are used in conditional logic (e.g. catalog_completeness.level = high/medium/low) are kept as their backing values and mapped to Polish at the view layer only — translating the stored value would break comparisons. Enums that surface directly in the UI implement Filament's HasLabel (e.g. UserRole → "Administrator / Właściciel lokalu / Użytkownik").

Manual checks (not unit-testable as chrome)

  • A real password-reset / verification e-mail: salutation, button helper text, and footer are Polish.
  • Validation errors on public forms read as Polish ("Pole … jest wymagane").
