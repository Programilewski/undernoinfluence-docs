## Important findings already established

**Recognition is not universally benign.** A 2012 randomised Wikipedia experiment involving 200 highly productive editors reported 60% greater median productivity after a barnstar, but the population came from the top 1% of contributors, limiting relevance to UNI's occasional anonymous contributors. Later Stack Overflow work found that much apparent badge steering could be a statistical artefact; only a minority appeared materially steered, and some reduced activity after crossing the threshold.

**Task clarity can improve quality while reducing quantity.** A 2019 Zooniverse field experiment found accuracy of 49% under an outcome-information treatment versus 35% in the control, while treated participants completed fewer classifications. That supports precise prompts such as "confirm this exact drink from the current menu," not points for making more taps.

**Gamification can damage measurement.** A preregistered experiment and replication found that a game produced more enjoyment but less accurate data than the equivalent non-game task, with accuracy effects of d = −0.68 and d = −0.40. UNI should therefore avoid streaks, leaderboards and volume badges on factual reports.

**The 90–9–1 split is a heuristic, not a dependable law.** Nielsen presented it in 2006 as an approximate pattern. Zooniverse instead found that the top 10% supplied 71–88% of contributions across seven projects—strong inequality, but not the canonical ratio.

**Early rejection predicts attrition.** Across a population of wikis, newcomers reverted in their first session had 0.49 times the odds of continuing compared with unreverted newcomers. A controlled Wikipedia interface intervention that warned reviewers they were reverting a newcomer reduced the decline in newcomer activity by 20.2%.

**Feedback helps, but effects are modest and uneven.** A multilingual Wikipedia experiment including Polish Wikipedia found about a 2% increase in retention after thanks, no significant increase in contributed hours, and 1.6 times as many subsequent thanks. An earlier post-edit confirmation experiment increased new-editor contribution volume without a detected quality penalty.

**Anonymity has trade-offs.** Research does not support the simple claim that real names always improve quality: a 45-million-comment study found the highest cognitive complexity under durable pseudonyms, followed by a decline after mandatory real names. Relative anonymity also enables participation around stigmatised health topics.

**Trust should follow confirmed accuracy, not engagement.** WikiTrust increases an editor's reputation when later editors preserve the contribution and decreases it when they undo it; high reputation predicts lower revert risk, although many newcomers also make good contributions. UNI's proposed weighting by views, searches or time spent would measure attention, not truth.

**Emptying an e-mail column is not necessarily anonymisation.** The Article 29 Working Party's 2014 opinion requires assessment of singling out, linkability and inference, and explicitly says removing direct identifiers alone is insufficient. Free text that contains a name, workplace, health disclosure or uniquely identifying incident remains personal data even after the separate e-mail field is cleared.

**Non-alcoholic searches can become inferred health data.** EDPB guidance treats assumptions concerning Article 9 characteristics as special-category data when the controller intentionally makes or uses that inference. Keeping a per-account history and using it to infer pregnancy, medication, recovery or another health condition would therefore be materially riskier than storing isolated contribution records.

**Small counts need suppression, not merely removal of names.** GUS prohibits certain business aggregates involving fewer than three entities or one entity contributing over three quarters of the total. Health-publication guidance commonly suppresses cells below five, while Eurostat describes primary and secondary suppression to prevent reconstruction.

**UNI should retain accountless contribution.** Wikimedia's temporary-account system preserves logged-out editing while hiding IP addresses from the public; temporary accounts expire after 90 days, and IP access is restricted to abuse investigation. UNI can adopt the accessibility principle without adopting Wikimedia's IP retention.

**A treatment-search safety response is appropriate.** KCPU provides the free, anonymous 800 199 990 helpline daily from 16:00 to 21:00 and maintains treatment information and a facility map. UNI should show this only for explicit help-related searches, calmly and without diagnosing, retargeting or recording the trigger.
