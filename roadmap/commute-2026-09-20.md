## 3. Findings raised unprompted

Ranked by consequence.

### 3.1 The "cleanup session" and the migration's own steps 2 and 3 are the same work, described twice — and removing the duplicate takes a whole session off the critical path

The forward plan in [`next-session-2026-09-20.md`](next-session-2026-09-20.md) §3 lists **a cleanup session, then the repository migration**, in that order, as two separate sittings. But the migration plan's step 2 is *"delete what is dead"* and its step 3 is *"move what leaves git"* — which is the cleanup, inside the migration, where it has to be anyway because the squash must not carry 51 MB of research into a new history.

What is genuinely left over from the cleanup session is documentation housekeeping: closing the ADR-006 and ADR-002 records, the P43 systems map, moving plan history into `v2.md`, and the Ploi page. **None of that gates anything.** It does not block the migration, the home server, production or the catalogue.

**So the plan should read: your answers, then the migration, then the home server.** The documentation chores become a session that can happen any evening, and the critical path loses a step it never needed. That is worth saying plainly because five of the last ten planning documents have listed the cleanup as the next thing to do, and it has been the reason the migration has not started.

### 3.2 The one irreversible step grew by 23 citations in three days, and it is the only backlog item that gets worse by waiting

Measured today: **311 hex candidates across the documentation, of which 76 resolve to real commits, spread over 34 files.** Of those 76, **eighteen were written on 18.09 and five on 19.09** — 23 in three working days, or roughly seven per session that ships code.

Every one of them becomes a dead reference the instant the history is squashed, and step 5 of the migration exists to rewrite them into dates and descriptions before that happens. It is the step most likely to be skipped, because it is tedious and it stands between you and the interesting part.

Two consequences, and both are decisions rather than work: **the migration should stop waiting** ([[#3. Findings raised unprompted\|§3.1]] removes its excuse), and **journals should stop minting new citations until it is done** (D9). Holding D9 costs nothing — a date and a description carry the same information and survive the squash.

### 3.3 Nothing scheduled or queued has ever run on any machine this project has lived on

Carried from 19.09 and verified again today: `crontab -l` returns *no crontab for bub*. Nothing has changed, but the stakes have.

As of 19.09 the following all depend on cron and a queue worker: the 08:00 reminder for requests waiting over 14 days, the registry lookup's retries, every claim e-mail, the nightly backup, and the daily anonymisation that four retention windows rely on. **On a server without them the owner check sends nothing and looks up nothing, and fails silently while appearing to work.** It is the first thing the home-server session must prove, and it is the concrete reason `UNI_OWNER_ACCESS` cannot open before that session happens.

Answer: do we have a file listing all crontabs needed and where it's explained what these do? If yes, show me, if not, create it 

### 3.4 Two documents give two different sizes for the same reference material, neither is wrong, and the decision they feed is the poorer for it

`docs/reference/` is **93 MB on disk**: 53 MB tracked across 56 files, plus **41 MB of gitignored Syncthing conflict copies**. The migration plan's "52.2 MB" is the tracked figure; the undone inventory's "91 MB" is the on-disk one. Neither says which it is measuring, and the two have been quoted against each other for a fortnight.

It matters because the decision drawn from them is wrong at the edges. Three things follow once the number is split:

- **The 41 MB never enters git.** Deleting the conflict copies frees disk and Syncthing bandwidth and shrinks no repository by a byte.
- **The tracked 53 MB is not one kind of thing.** 51 MB is `research/` — NIQ and IWSR market reports, four of the five largest written for operators and brands, which is the class of evidence you ruled out on 10.09 as not grounding UNI decisions. The remaining 1.7 MB is `gus-api/`, `nominatim/` and `legal/`, which is API documentation and the GDPR and ePrivacy source texts.
- **The plan's "20 files and 52.2 MB" describes two different scopes in one row.** Twenty files is `research/` alone; 52.2 MB is the whole directory, which is 56 files.

Hence D3: the research leaves git, the 1.7 MB stays. Also worth stating so nobody sweeps them out by mistake — `legal/` is the GDPR and ePrivacy regulation texts, **not** the withdrawn alcohol-law analysis, and it is not covered by the 07.09 decision.

### 3.5 The ten conflict copies are five files saved twice, and the pairs are byte-identical

18.09's open question 3 asked whether they are duplicates and deferred the comparison to the home server. It can be answered here: `md5sum` across the ten returns **five distinct hashes, each appearing exactly twice**. Every one is a second capture of a page whose first capture is tracked, and they differ from the tracked original only in the way two MHTML saves of the same page always differ. There is nothing in them that is not somewhere else. Hence D8.

The live schema has 33 documented tables and **342** columns — `migrations` is the 34th table and is deliberately not mapped. `docs/tech/schema.md` says 342 and is correct; the two migrations on 19.09 updated the map as your rule requires, but nobody updated the sentence in the instruction file that points at it. **Your local `.env` has `SESSION_DRIVER=database` while `.env.example` and the record say `file`.** Production built from the example is correct, so this is laptop-only drift — but it means the local database is holding **25 session rows, every one with an `ip_address`**, against a record titled `sessions-hold-no-ip-address`. Your rule from 19.09 is that `.env.example` is production; the fix therefore belongs on the laptop, not in the example.

## 7. This document's name

On 16.09 four commute documents were written in one evening, each named for a future day: 19.09, 20.09, 21.09 and 22.09. The calendar has since caught up and passed them. **Tomorrow is Monday 21.09, and `commute-2026-09-16_6.md` already exists** — it is v8.0, it was written on 16.09, and it describes a project of 774 tests and a switch called `UNI_OWNER_PANEL` that no longer exists.

So this one takes the next free filename rather than overwriting a real document, which puts it last in the folder where you will look for it, and says the real day in its title. That is a patch, not a convention.

**The convention worth having is D13: name the file for the day it was written.** A document written on 20.09 is `commute-2026-09-16_5.md` whatever morning it is read on, the name is never wrong, and writing four ahead stops being able to lie. Say the word and the two misleading names get a line at the top saying when they were actually written — renaming them would break the links from the journals, which is a worse trade.
