## 1. Your café question found a hole, and the rule shipped narrower because of it

The fifth version's test 3 read:

> *"A non-alcoholic member of a drinking category — beer, wine, sparkling, cider, spirits, mixed drinks — **or a drink the venue makes and names itself**."*

You wrote: *"I feel like it might clutter the app since each cafe will offer stuff like coffee etc."*

**You were right, and about my rule rather than about the enum.** "A drink the venue makes and names itself" describes a café's signature latte exactly. Under the rule as written, any café with a named house drink on its menu passed all three tests — the place (you drink there), the offer (named, priced, on the menu), the drink (made and named by the venue). Every specialty coffee shop in Warsaw would have qualified, on coffee.

**The fix was already in the database.** `venue_drinks.base_category` is an enum with exactly four values — `mocktail`, `koktajl_nolo`, `drink_spirytus_0`, `virgin_classic`. There is no coffee, no tea, no lemonade, and there never was. The schema has been narrower than my prose since June; I wrote the rule loosely and the code would have refused what the rule admitted.

**Test 3, as shipped:**

> Either a **non-alcoholic member of a drinking category** — beer, wine, sparkling, cider, spirits, mixed drinks — **or a house drink in one of the four categories the schema already enforces**: `mocktail`, `koktajl_nolo`, `drink_spirytus_0`, `virgin_classic`.

A coffee is not what somebody orders instead of a beer, and now the rule says so rather than relying on a column to say it.

**One consequence, applied today.** The "Szampan 0%" category described itself as *"Kombucha, toniki, wody smakowe i napoje musujące"* — and three of those four are out at test 3. Corrected to *"Bezalkoholowe wina musujące, prosecco i szampany"*, which is what the category actually is. That description was the case you raised on 16.09 as the example of the scope problem; it is now the first thing the rule fixed.

## 2. `kawiarnia` — the research, and why the enum is not the thing you are worried about

You asked to research the validity and viability of adding the category. Here is what the numbers say, and then a structural point that I think matters more.

### The numbers

Warsaw has **over 7,500 gastronomy venues**. In the counted subset, bars lead with **905 (34%)**, street-food points **735 (28%)**, full restaurants **472 (18%)**, canteens **460 (17%)**. **Cafés are not broken out as a category at all** in that data — which is itself a hint about how the trade classifies them. Śródmieście alone holds **2,153** venues, Mokotów 995, Wola 927.

So your instinct about volume is right: cafés are numerous, and "five per street in Śródmieście" is not an exaggeration. I could find no Poland-specific data on what share of cafés carry a non-alcoholic *drinks* offer as opposed to coffee — the 2025 trade coverage is global and describes NA menus spreading through bars and restaurants, with coffee shops appearing mainly as a place where coffee-based mocktails are being invented.

### The structural point, which I think is the actual answer

**Adding `kawiarnia` to the enum admits nothing.** `venue_type` is a label and a filter. It is not an admission gate anywhere in the code — what admits a venue is the activation gate, which requires a point on the map and something on the menu that passes the scope rule.

So the two questions are independent:

| Question | Answered by |
|---|---|
| *Does this café belong in UNI?* | **The scope rule** — test 3, which now excludes coffee |
| *What do we call it once it is in?* | **`venue_type`** |

A coffee-only café fails test 3 and never enters, whether or not the enum has a word for it. A café that makes four named mocktails passes — and **that one is in the catalogue today**, labelled `inne`, because the enum has no better word.

**So the clutter risk you are sensing is real and it lives in test 3, which is now closed.** The enum question is only about whether the venues that already qualify are labelled honestly.

### And a better framing for the type list

Re-reading `venue-type-filtering` (22.04), its whole argument is capacity: *"a cocktail bar can have 15 items, a restaurant realistically 3–5"*, and types exist so a filter can compare like with like rather than letting restaurants sink permanently. **That means the type list should track NoLo capacity, not cuisine.**

Which explains something in the record I had read past: it does not say *"kawiarnia"*. It says **"kawiarnia/koktajlbar"** — one category, hyphenated. That is not "coffee shop". It is the drinks-led small venue class, the places whose offer is the drink itself. A café-bar with eight mocktails sits there naturally; a specialty roaster with no NoLo offer never arrives at all.

**Revised recommendation — smaller than the one you were asked to approve:**

1. **Add one value, `kawiarnia_koktajlbar`**, matching the record's own wording rather than inventing "café". It labels the venues that already qualify and are currently mislabelled `inne`.
2. **Add `hotel`**, which the same record names and which is genuinely a different capacity class.
3. **Leave `klub` out** for V1 — rare enough, and `inne` is an honest answer for them.
4. **Make `venue_type` required on the form**, so unclassified and deliberately-other stop being the same value.

That is four existing values plus two, and it is a fifteen-minute change while production is empty. **Still your call**, because it is taxonomy — but the decision is much smaller than it looked, since none of it changes who gets in.

**One thing worth knowing while you decide:** `venue-type-filtering`'s `Executed:` line already reads *"no — there is no venue-type filter, and the types in `VenueType` differ from the four named here"*. The gap has been recorded since 14.09; the June journal put a deadline on it — *"decide before importing, not after"* — and that deadline is the one you are about to reach.

## 3. Yes — there is a third badge, and it is already built

You asked: *"Do we have a badge for saying that the venue is claimed and run by an owner? Like a third one apart from these two?"*

**Yes. "Zarządza właściciel."** It exists, it is in `venue-tile.blade.php`, and it is documented in `what-the-badges-claim` alongside the other two.

| Badge | Condition | Claims | Live? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freshness pill | `breadth_score > 0`, `offer_updated_at` set, wording changes past **90 days** | When the offer was last confirmed | **Yes** |
| "Sprawdzona karta" | `is_verified` **and** `last_menu_check_at` within **180 days** | A person checked this menu within six months | **Yes** |
| **"Zarządza właściciel"** | `is_claimed` **and** `config('uni.owner_managed_badge')` | **A named, verified person is answerable for this offer** | **No — flag off** |

**Why it is dark.** Fourteen venues carried `is_claimed = true` with **zero claims in the database** — an import had set the column. The badge was making a promise about every one of them that nothing could honour, which is exactly what `v1-copy-truth` exists to prevent. It was gated in the view rather than cleaned in the data on purpose: `is_claimed` is a column an import can set again, so cleaning the rows would have hidden the problem until the next spreadsheet.

**When it comes on.** Its config comment says *"the day the owner panel opens"* — written when the panel's status was ambiguous. Now that the panel has a flag of its own (`UNI_OWNER_ACCESS`, O-2, built 16.09), I would make the badge wait one step longer: **it turns on when the first real claim is approved**, not when the panel opens. An open panel with no owners in it asserts nothing, and the badge asserts that somebody specific is answerable.

So your V2 sequence is three switches, in this order: `UNI_OWNER_ACCESS=true` → first claim recorded and approved → `UNI_OWNER_MANAGED_BADGE=true`.

**It is also the badge with the most commercial weight**, which is worth saying while it is still off. The other two describe our work; this one describes a relationship. It is the visible half of what a venue gets for engaging at all, and `analytics-three-tiers` puts the free owner dashboard in the same role — *"a weekly reason to open the dashboard, which is also the primary freshness maintenance touchpoint"*. Turning it on the day the first owner is approved is what makes claiming worth doing.
