# V3 — Expansion & Monetization

**Theme:** Prove the model scales beyond Warsaw, then monetize the B2B side.
**Target:** When V2 engagement metrics prove the flywheel works.

---

## V3 Entry Gate

ALL of these must be true before starting V3 work. No exceptions, no "close enough."

| # | Metric | Threshold | Why this number |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly unique visitors (organic) | 500+ | Proves SEO and word-of-mouth work without paid acquisition |
| 2 | Claimed venues with fresh data | 30+ (offer_updated_at < 90 days) | Proves owners find enough value in the free tier to stay engaged |
| 3 | Post-visit confirmation response rate | > 15% | Proves users engage beyond passive browsing |
| 4 | Venue owners asking about premium features | 5+ unprompted inquiries | Proves willingness to pay without you selling |
| 5 | Revenue model validated | Pricing conversations (V1 BV-1) show > 0 PLN willingness | Proves there's a business here, not just a hobby project |

**Why these numbers matter:** They represent the minimum viable proof that the flywheel works. 500 visitors with 30 active venues means ~17 visitors per venue per month — enough to show owners real engagement data. 15% confirmation rate means users feel invested enough to respond. 5 premium inquiries means demand exists organically.

---

## Kill Conditions

Rethink the entire model if, after 6 months post-V1 launch, ANY of these are true:

| Condition | What it means | Pivot options |
|---|---|---|
| < 10 venues claimed despite active outreach | Owners don't see value in managing their listing | Pivot to pure B2C (no owner panel), or find a different owner incentive |
| < 5% of directions clicks lead to confirmed visits | Users browse but don't actually go to venues | UNI may be a research tool, not a decision tool — rethink the value prop |
| 0 owners express interest in paying for anything | The B2B monetization thesis is wrong | Pivot to affiliate (link to NA drink shops), sponsored content, or accept it's a free tool and find alternative funding |
| < 100 monthly visitors after 6 months of SEO + Instagram | The market is too small or the content doesn't rank | Either the NoLo niche in Poland isn't big enough, or the SEO strategy needs fundamental rethinking |

These conditions are uncomfortable to define. That's the point. They prevent you from building for 18 months toward something the market doesn't want. If you hit a kill condition, it doesn't mean "shut down" — it means "stop building the next version and rethink the current one."

## Features

### V3-F1: Multi-City Expansion

**What:** Launch UNI in a second Polish city (Gdansk or Krakow), then a third.

**Why it matters:**
- Proves the model isn't Warsaw-specific
- Multiplies total addressable market
- Creates competitive moat — first mover in each city owns the data

**City selection criteria:**
- Venue density of NoLo-friendly establishments
- Founder access (can seed initial venues personally or remotely)
- Instagram reach in target city
- Distance from Warsaw (for field verification if needed)

**Prerequisites from V1/V2:**
- All queries are city-scoped (V2 priming PR-V3-1)
- Districts table handles multiple cities (already done — `city_id` FK)
- "Warszawie" hardcoded strings removed (V1 TODO #8)
- City selector in UI is prominent (V2 priming)

**Implementation outline:**
1. Seed districts for new city
2. Research and seed 30-40 venues with real data
3. Launch city landing page and district pages
4. Local Instagram content campaign
5. B2B outreach to seeded venue owners

**Estimated scope:** 3-5 days of seeding per city, 1 day of code (mostly configuration)

### V3-F2: i18n / Locale Infrastructure

**What:** Support non-Polish languages when expanding to non-Polish-speaking cities.

**Why it matters:**
- Revisit trigger from V1 D-12: "First non-Polish city is added"
- Berlin, Prague, Vienna are potential expansion targets

**Implementation outline:**
1. Add `hreflang` middleware (as planned in D-12)
2. Route segment translations (e.g., `/miejsce/` -> `/venue/` or `/lokal/`)
3. Content translation for static pages (manual, not automated)
4. Category names in target language
5. City slug = implicit locale anchor (already designed this way)

**Design constraint:** Don't build a full translation management system. Start with 2 languages (PL + EN or DE), hardcoded translation files. Graduate to a CMS-driven approach only if 3+ languages are needed.

**Estimated scope:** 3-5 days for first non-Polish city

**English for tourists in Polish cities (parked 14.09.2026, not before launch):** a second trigger for the same infrastructure — visitors in Warsaw who cannot read "bezalkoholowe" on a Polish menu. Researched in `commute-2026-09-14.md`: a real audience (GUS: 15.2 million foreign tourists in Polish accommodation in 2025) that passes the mission test, but English means extracting every inline Polish string, reciprocal hreflang, doubled noindex gates and legal pages kept true in two languages, and nothing can rank before launch. First step when revisited: one hand-written English page with a glossary of Polish menu words, linking into the catalogue, measured in Search Console for 8–12 weeks against a threshold set in advance; the full translation only if that page earns it. An `/en/` prefix adds addresses without touching any Polish URL, so nothing has to be decided earlier.

### V3-F3: B2B Premium Features (Monetization)

**What:** Paid tier for venue owners with enhanced analytics, priority placement, and marketing tools.

**Why it matters:**
- First revenue stream
- Validates the B2B business model
- Funds continued development and expansion

**Prerequisites:**
- 30+ claimed venues (enough to create a market)
- Owner analytics dashboard live and proven valuable (V2-F3)
- At least 3 owners asking for more than the free tier provides

**Possible premium features (validate before building):**
- **Enhanced analytics:** Competitor comparison, trend analysis, demographic insights
- **Priority placement:** Highlighted listing in discovery results (clearly labeled as promoted)
- **Monthly PDF report:** Automated report emailed to owner — profile views, visits, competitive ranking
- **Custom branding:** Upload logo, customize profile page accent color
- **Multi-location management:** Dashboard for owners with 2+ venues
- **Promotional banners:** "Happy hour on NA cocktails this Friday" — time-limited promotional content on venue page

**Pricing strategy:**
- Freemium model: free tier (current features) + premium tier
- Price research happens in V1 (see `v1.md` BV-1) — by the time you reach V3, you should already have 5+ data points on willingness to pay from real owner conversations during the seeding phase
- If V1 pricing conversations reveal zero willingness to pay, revisit the entire monetization model before building V3-F3. Alternative revenue: affiliate links to NA drink e-shops, sponsored category placements, or accept this is a free tool funded differently.
- Consider: flat monthly fee vs. performance-based pricing

**Implementation outline:**
1. Stripe/Paddle integration for subscription billing
2. `subscription` column or `subscriptions` table on venues
3. Feature gates in Filament panel based on subscription tier
4. Premium analytics widgets
5. "Upgrade" CTA in free-tier dashboard with preview of premium data

**Estimated scope:** 2-3 weeks for MVP billing + first 2-3 premium features

### V3-F4: Public API

**What:** RESTful API for third-party integrations — food apps, travel apps, local guides.

**Why it matters:**
- Distribution channel — your data in other apps drives traffic back
- Partnership enabler — "integrate UNI's NoLo venue data into your app"
- Data authority signal — if you're the API source, you're the authority

**Prerequisites:**
- Data quality proven (high accuracy rates from V2-F1 confirmations)
- Enough data volume to be useful (200+ venues across multiple cities)
- Legal review of data licensing terms

**Implementation outline:**
1. Laravel API resources (already exist in patterns)
2. API versioning (v1 prefix)
3. API key authentication (free tier with rate limits)
4. Endpoints: venues (list, show, search), categories, cities, districts
5. Documentation (auto-generated from OpenAPI spec)

**Estimated scope:** 1-2 weeks

---

### V3-F5: AI-Powered Recommendations

**What:** "Venues like this", "Based on your saves", "Popular in your area" — personalized venue suggestions.

**Why it matters:**
- Engagement driver — surfaces venues users wouldn't find through search
- Retention — personalized content keeps users coming back
- Differentiator — no competitor does this for NoLo venues

**Prerequisites:**
- User saves data (V2-F2) with enough volume to find patterns
- Visit confirmation data (V2-F1) to validate recommendations
- 200+ venues to have meaningful variety

**Implementation approaches (evaluate based on data volume):**
- **Simple:** Content-based filtering — "venues with similar category breadth in your district"
- **Medium:** Collaborative filtering — "users who saved X also saved Y"
- **Advanced:** Claude API-powered — natural language venue matching based on description + menu

**Estimated scope:** 1-2 weeks depending on approach

---

### V3-F6: Brand Diversity in Scoring

**What:** Evolve the flat category count scoring (ADR-008) to reward brand diversity within categories.

**Why it matters:**
- Revisit trigger from V1 D-08: "Post-launch analytics show users value specific categories differently"
- A venue with 5 different NA beer brands is more interesting than one with 5 SKUs from the same brand
- Creates a stronger incentive for venues to diversify their offering

**Prerequisites:**
- Enough data to calibrate (100+ venues with 10+ products each)
- User behavior data showing that diverse venues get more engagement
- Careful design to avoid penalizing specialty venues (a cocktail bar shouldn't need 5 beer brands)

**Implementation outline:**
1. Add brand diversity factor to `VenueScoring::recalculate()`
2. Within each category: count unique brands, apply a diversity multiplier
3. Update `breadth_score` column to include the new factor
4. A/B test: do users engage more with diversity-ranked results?

**Estimated scope:** 1-2 days (once formula is designed and validated)

## Non-Goals for V3

- Native mobile app (unless PWA engagement data strongly argues for it)
- User-generated content beyond reports (reviews, photos, comments) — UNI is a data platform, not a review platform
- B2C monetization (charging end users) — the product is free for consumers, always
- Automated venue discovery (scraping) — data integrity requires manual curation
- Social features (following users, sharing lists publicly) — evaluate based on demand
