## 1. I had kebab-rule backwards, and it changes the catalogue rule

You wrote: *"Read the journals and other relevant files to see the decisions."* I did, and the first thing it turned up was my own error.

**What I wrote, twice** — in the 18.09 and 19.09 documents, as a worked case:

> *"Kebab shop with tables, one 0,0% beer in the fridge → **in**. Exactly `kebab-rule`."*

**What `kebab-rule` actually says**, from the record dated 22.04:

> *"Na V1 venue musi mieć produkt NoLo **w menu** lub w stałej, nazwanej ofercie napojowej. **«Widziałem coś w lodówce» to za mało.** Ta reguła naturalnie filtruje **kebaby, żabki i stacje benzynowe**, zostawiając restauracje, bary, kawiarnie i hotele."*

And its Rules section: *"Venue bez menu napojowego (tylko «napój» w lodówce) nie kwalifikuje się na V1."*

So the rule is named for the case it **excludes**, and the exact example I used — a beer in the fridge — is the disqualifying one, stated almost word for word. I inverted it, built a worked example on the inversion, and repeated it. You were right to send me to the record.

### What this adds to the rule

My fourth version had two tests: **the place** (can you drink it there?) and **the drink** (is it what somebody orders instead of alcohol?). `kebab-rule` adds a third that is about neither — it is about whether the offer is **deliberate and documented**:

> **A named item, with a price, in the venue's drinks offer.** Not a bottle observed in a fridge. The venue has to have decided to sell it.

That test is doing more work than either of mine. It is what makes the data defensible — "I saw one once" cannot support a freshness date, a verified badge or a breadth score, because it changes with the next delivery. And it is why `kebab-rule` says *"this does not mean kebabs never"*: a kebab shop that prints a drinks menu with two 0,0% beers on it **qualifies**, and one with a fridge does not. The test is the menu, not the cuisine.

### The rule, fifth version — three tests, all required

> **1. The place.** A venue where drinks are consumed on the premises — not a shop, supermarket, petrol station, off-licence, or delivery-only kitchen. *Can you drink it there?*
>
> **2. The offer.** The drink is a **named item in the venue's drinks offer, with a price** — on a menu, a board, or a standing list. Not something seen in a fridge. *Did the venue decide to sell this?*
>
> **3. The drink.** A non-alcoholic member of a drinking category — beer, wine, sparkling, cider, spirits, mixed drinks — **or** a drink the venue makes and names itself. *Is this what somebody orders instead of alcohol?*

**All three, or it does not go in.** Test 2 is `kebab-rule` restored to what it says; tests 1 and 3 are yours from 18.09 and 19.09.

Worked cases, corrected:

- **Kebab shop, 0,0% beer in the fridge, no drinks menu** → **out at test 2.** *This is the case the rule is named after, and what I got wrong twice.*
- **Kebab shop with a printed drinks list including a 0,0% beer** → **in.** `kebab-rule`'s own "this does not mean kebabs never".
- Żabka, Biedronka, petrol station → **out at test 1**, whatever they stock.
- A café with four named house mocktails → **in**, on the house-drink route.
- A bar with a NoLo section on the menu → **in**, obviously.
- Kombucha, craft soda, tonic, flavoured water → **out at test 3.** Your 18.09 call, unchanged.
- Delivery-only → **out at test 1.**

**One thing worth your eye:** `kebab-rule`'s revisit trigger is *"post-V1, if the fast-casual / street-food segment starts deliberately building a NoLo offer with a named menu"* — which test 2 already admits case by case. That is the right shape: no segment is banned, a menu is required.

**Say yes and this goes into the record in the cleanup session**, superseding the scope half of `catalogue-excludes-actual-alcohol` and amending `kebab-rule` with the test written explicitly rather than implied by a Polish sentence I misread.
Answer: yes, do as you just said here

## 2. `venue_type` — you were right to ask

You wrote: *"Why is default of `inne` bad? We do not accept cafes etc. Read the journals and other relevant files."*

**On "we do not accept cafés" — the record says the opposite, twice.** `kebab-rule` lists what survives its filter as *"restauracje, bary, **kawiarnie** i hotele"*. And `venue-type-filtering.md` sets V1's types as *"restauracja, bar/pub, **kawiarnia/koktajlbar**, hotel"*. So cafés are in scope by decision, and a café with named house mocktails is one of the clearest qualifying cases there is.

**What is actually true is that the enum does not have them.** `App\Enums\VenueType` holds exactly four cases: `restauracja`, `pub`, `bar`, `inne`. No `kawiarnia`, no `hotel`, no `klub`. So every café and hotel in the catalogue lands in `inne` — not because anybody decided that, but because the enum was never widened to match the record.

**And that is precisely the open item from June.** The 17.06 journal, item 6 of its backlog:

> *"Consider `venue_type` expanding to `kawiarnia`/`klub` — if the Warsaw venue data includes cafes or clubs, `inne` is the only bucket. **Decide before importing, not after.**"*

Three months old, still open, and the deadline in it is the one you are about to hit.

**So: `inne` as a bucket is fine. `inne` as a default is the problem**, and for a reason that comes from `venue-type-filtering` rather than from tidiness. That record's whole argument is that venue types exist because different types have different natural capacity for a NoLo offer — *"a cocktail bar can have 15 items, a restaurant realistically 3–5"* — so that a filter can compare like with like and restaurants do not permanently sink to the bottom of one shared list. **That mechanism stops working if a third of the catalogue is `inne`**, and a default means unclassified and deliberately-other are indistinguishable.

**Recommendation — three small things, before the first venue:**

1. **Add `kawiarnia` and `hotel`** to the enum and the CHECK constraint, matching `venue-type-filtering`. Free now, a migration after the first deploy. `klub` too if you expect clubs; my read is that they are rare enough for V1 to leave out.
2. **Make `venue_type` required on the venue form** rather than defaulting. Four or six choices is not a burden while cataloguing, and it is the column carrying test 1 of the scope rule.
3. **Keep `inne`** as a deliberate choice for genuinely odd places — a cinema bar, a bowling alley, a theatre foyer — and keep it out of `publicOptions()`, which it already is.

**Not a code change I would make without your yes**, because widening the enum is a catalogue taxonomy decision, not a refactor.

Answer: can we research the validity and viability of the categories like kawiarnia? I feel like it might clutter the app since each cafe will offer stuff like coffee etc, we need to think about it thoroughly not to add too much noises, there's like 5 cafes for each street in Warsaw alone.

## 3. Converging the badge windows — what that would mean

You asked: *"What does it mean we can converge windows?"*

Two badges on a tile count to different numbers:

| Badge | Window | Config | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freshness pill | **90 days** | `uni.freshness_days` | When the offer was last confirmed |
| "Sprawdzona karta" | **180 days** | `uni.verification_valid_days` | A person checked the menu within six months |

"Converging" would mean making both the same number — say both 90, or both 180 — so a visitor never sees one badge saying the listing is old while the other still says it is checked. It is one config change and nothing else.

**I do not recommend it, and your instinct to explain is the right one.** The two windows measure genuinely different things and the difference is meaningful: the freshness pill is about **the data** — when did anybody last confirm this menu — while "Sprawdzona karta" is about **an act of verification**, which is a heavier thing done deliberately and which reasonably stays valid longer. Forcing them together would either make the verification badge expire faster than the work behind it deserves, or make the freshness date stop meaning "recent".

**So: explain, do not converge.** One sentence on the venue page next to the badges — *"Data pokazuje, kiedy ostatnio potwierdziliśmy ofertę. «Sprawdzona karta» oznacza, że ktoś z nas przejrzał kartę lokalu w ciągu ostatnich sześciu miesięcy."* That is a copy change, it belongs with the `/kontakt` work before launch, and I have added it to `what-the-badges-claim` as the resolution of its open question rather than leaving both options live.
Answer: I agree, but do we have a badge (for V2 since owners panels will be in v2) for saying that the venue is claimed and run by an owner?
Like a third one apart from these two discussed here?
