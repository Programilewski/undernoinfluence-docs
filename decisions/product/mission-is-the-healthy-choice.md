# The mission is the healthy choice — everything else is an implementation detail

**Date:** 2026-09-04
**Status:** Decided — foundational
**Executed:** standing rule — foundational
**Area:** Brand | Strategy

---

## Problem

UNI has a brand book, a scoring model, a category taxonomy, a venue database and a set of monetisation ideas, and it had no written statement of what any of it is *for*. That is not a philosophical gap, it is an operational one, and it showed up as decisions being reasoned backwards from the artifact. Questions arrived as "should brand pages list venues or products", "should producers be public", "how do we rank venues" — all answerable only against a purpose, and all being answered against precedent, taste and SEO instead. Where the artifact is the premise, the roadmap drifts toward whatever the current build makes easy.

## Options considered

**No stated mission, decide case by case.** What we had. Fast, and it produced the failure above.

**Mission as a marketing line.** "Odkryj bezalkoholowe" on the homepage and nowhere else. Cheap, and worthless the moment a decision needs arbitrating — a slogan cannot settle whether producers get public pages.

**Mission as a constitution — stated, load-bearing, and above the artifact.** Every product decision derives from it; it never derives from a product decision. Costs the discipline of actually applying it.

## Decision

**UNI exists to promote the healthy choice. Everything else — venues, products, brands, producers, the map, the scoring, the reports — is a consequence of that mission, chosen because it currently serves it, and replaceable when it does not.**

Three parts, all load-bearing:

**1. We do not promote products, brands, producers, or even venues.** We promote the availability of a healthier option. A venue appears because it helps somebody make that choice; a product appears because it is what they would choose. Neither is the point. The point is that the choice exists and can be found.

**2. Harm reduction, not abstinence.** Helping somebody find an alternative asks far less of them than asking them to stop, and **partial substitution is a recognised harm-reduction outcome, not a consolation prize** — supported by RCTs on provision of non-alcoholic drinks, and consistent with how harm reduction is measured in tobacco and opioids. **If somebody has two non-alcoholic drinks and two alcoholic ones instead of four, that is a success.** The product is not measured against sobriety and never sets that as its target.

**UNI takes no public position on alcohol policy.** Not that restriction works, not that it has failed. This is a rule, and it was arrived at by getting it wrong twice on 04.09.2026.

The record first said *"telling people to stop drinking has failed repeatedly and at scale"*. That is factually wrong — modern restriction is well evidenced, and it conflates targeted regulation with Prohibition — **and it is the alcohol industry's characteristic sentence.** *"Restriction doesn't work, education and choice do"* is the industry's line, and repeating it makes a harm-reduction product sound like a lobby.

It was then corrected to *"restriction works and we complement it"* — **and Paweł rejected that, correctly.** People do not want to be told their freedom should be limited; they want a choice. A product whose whole method is being useful rather than preachy cannot open by endorsing constraints on its own users. That framing would lose the person who was going to have four beers, which is the only person UNI exists for.

**Both were the same mistake: answering a question that is not ours.** Whether taxes, availability limits or advertising bans work is a question for public health policy. **UNI's domain is what a person can order tonight, and that is the only subject on which it speaks publicly.**

**3. The mission does not come from what we do.** If promoting the healthy choice one day means running an e-commerce catalogue, or talking to people on the street, or something not yet imagined, then that is what UNI does. The current form is a hypothesis about the best available method, not the identity of the organisation.

**And we monetise — honestly when asked, never as a boast.** The mission requires the project to survive, and survival requires revenue. We do not hide that behind non-profit framing: **asked directly, we answer directly.** But we do not advertise it, and we make no forward-looking public statements about how we will monetise.

Two reasons, and they are different. **A published commitment about revenue can harden into an expectation somebody later treats as an obligation** — a stated model is much harder to change than an unstated one. And **volunteering it reads as greedy**, which damages the mission it is supposed to fund: a product whose stated purpose is public-health-shaped and whose loudest sentence is about money invites exactly the cynicism that makes people ignore it.

**The constraint is that revenue may never come from compromising the choice** — no paid ranking, no paid inclusion, no brand-funded placement that makes an option look better than it is. Revenue comes from selling venues honest information about their own visibility: a service that helps them and misleads nobody.

## Rules

Every product decision is tested against the mission first, and the test is: *does this help somebody find a healthier option they would otherwise have missed?* A feature that helps a brand more than a person is not a UNI feature.

**Ranking is never for sale.** See [[decisions/product/ranking-is-never-for-sale]]. Neither is inclusion, position on a brand page, or the outcome of any filter.

**The catalogue carries every non-alcoholic option a venue actually serves.** Completeness serves the person choosing; a directory that quietly omits categories sends them to a bar without telling them what is behind the counter. The boundary is the one in [[decisions/product/catalogue-excludes-actual-alcohol]] — nothing above 0,5% — and there is no second boundary inside it.

**We claim individual harm reduction and never population-level impact.** The evidence supports the first. **It does not support the second:** WHO judges the net population effect of no/low-alcohol products uncertain, since they create new drinking occasions as well as displacing existing ones, and **no study has ever tested whether a discovery layer like ours changes what anyone orders.** *"We help individuals substitute"* is defensible. *"We reduce alcohol consumption in Poland"* is not, and claiming it would invite exactly the discrediting this record exists to prevent.

**Tone stays non-preachy.** This does not contradict the mission — it *is* the mission's method. [[decisions/product/faceless-brand]] holds: we answer a question about what is on the menu rather than making a point about drinking, because the moment UNI reads as activism it stops reaching the person who was going to have four beers.

**Venues, brands and products are described as instruments throughout the documentation and the interface** — never as clients being promoted, even where they are also customers.

## What this prevents

**A roadmap that optimises the artifact.** Without this, "we have brand pages, so brands must matter" is a valid-sounding argument. With it, brand pages exist only while they help somebody choose, and the day they mostly help brands they are a candidate for deletion.

**A monetisation route that quietly inverts the product.** Paid ranking is the obvious way to make money here and it is available at any moment. Naming it as excluded now, while there is no revenue pressure, is much cheaper than resisting it later.

**A product that overclaims and gets discredited.** A mission asserting population-level impact is one study away from being embarrassing. One asserting that individuals can find and choose an alternative is a claim we can actually stand behind.

## Revisit when

The method stops serving the mission. Specifically: if the data shows people find options through UNI but do not choose them, the discovery model is wrong and should change. **The mission itself is not scheduled for revisiting — it is the thing everything else is revisited against.**
