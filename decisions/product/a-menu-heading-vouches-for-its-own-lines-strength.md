# A menu heading vouches for its own line's strength, and only that line's

**Date:** 2026-11-20
**Status:** Decided
**Executed:** 2026-11-20, `v0.1.20`
**Area:** Catalogue | Sourcing

---

## Problem

Paweł's first real use of "Zapytaj AI" was refused: "Fines Bulles Vintense", printed under "Wina bezalkoholowe", could not become the product "Vintense Fines Bulles Blanc 0.0%", because the guard compared strength only against the printed name, which says nothing about strength. The guard exists so that a menu's "Heineken" never becomes Heineken 0.0, the worst wrong fact UNI can publish. But here the menu does say the strength, one line higher, and refusing it sends a true alcohol-free wine back to a person for no reason.

## Options considered

Keep reading only the printed name and ask Paweł to strip "0.0%" from the product name. Let the heading count, and remember the bare spelling as the product's alias as usual. Let the heading count for this one line, and remember nothing.

## Decision

**A heading that claims alcohol-free counts as the menu stating the strength for the lines under it, but the bare printed spelling is not kept as an alias.** The same words on another menu, outside such a section, could be the alcoholic version, so that menu asks a person again. A heading that says nothing about strength ("Piwa butelkowe") vouches for nothing.

## Rules

A product's strength claim is accepted from a menu line when the printed name repeats it, or when the printed name together with its section heading does. Only the first case remembers the spelling as a confirmed one ([[decisions/product/menu-spellings-are-confirmed-not-guessed]]); in the second the offer is recorded under the product's own name. The refusal tells the person what to do when a brand makes only alcohol-free drinks: drop the strength from the product's name. The same rule holds for "Utwórz produkt" and for "Popraw".

## What this prevents

A real alcohol-free drink refused because the menu wrote its strength in the heading, which is how many menus write it. And, through the missing alias, the quieter failure: a spelling learned under an alcohol-free heading silently attaching an alcoholic drink of the same name elsewhere.

## Revisit when

Headings turn out to be wrong often enough (a "bezalkoholowe" section mixing in alcoholic drinks) to need a person's look, or bare spellings under such headings repeat across many menus and asking each time becomes the slow part of the review.
