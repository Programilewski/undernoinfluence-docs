# 2026-09-19 — Nobody is phoned, one page for owners, and the switch waits for a real check

## Session Overview

The day had two halves, split by a broken session. **Before the break**, the owner side was tidied and extended: the switch became `UNI_OWNER_ACCESS`, a venue page stopped naming other venues, "Nowe w karcie" arrived, owners got an approval e-mail that works without the panel, and a form for venues UNI does not list yet. **After the break**, you changed the ground under all of it: *"We are not phoning."* The phone call had been the only thing proving that the person asking really runs the venue, so it was taken out of every form and record, a research prompt was written, and two research reports came back and were assessed. The two owner forms became **one page, `/zglos-lokal`, with two tabs and a search**. The venue page now tells an owner, first and plainly, that the profile can be theirs to manage. A chain's owner can be given any number of venues. And you set the rule that ties it together: **owner access is switched on only once there is a way to check owners.** Nothing is committed yet. **1012 tests pass**, up from 971 on 18.09.

*The first half is reconstructed from the working tree and today's decision records, because the conversation that produced it broke. Everything described there is in the code and was re-tested after the break (1002 tests at that point).*

### The research — two reports, assessed

Both reports are in `docs/roadmap/research-results/`. **Neither gives sources**, although the prompt asked for links, so every specific number in them (Foursquare's $20, postage and SMS prices, rate limits, "20% of Google edits") is unverified. Where the two agree, that is still not independent confirmation: they likely draw on the same material.

**Where both agree — adopt:**
1. **Proof is control of a channel the venue itself publishes, and that UNI recorded before the request**: the venue's own e-mail, an e-mail on the venue's website domain, or a message from the venue's Instagram or Facebook. Both call this the strong signal.
2. **Registries (KRS, CEIDG, REGON) support a decision; they never make one.** A NIP and a board member's name are public.
3. **Skip bank transfers, e-ID, video and document uploads.** Postcards only as a last resort in a dispute. A website meta tag is too technical for a bar (the second report's view, which I share).
4. **UNI checks the owner; the owner adds staff and agencies**, as Google does. An agency is never the owner.
5. **A claim on a venue that already has an owner goes to that owner first.**

**What the second report adds that is useful:** concrete Polish API facts — the KRS API (`api-krs.ms.gov.pl`) is free, needs no registration, and lists board members and proxies; CEIDG's free key is obtained through Profil Zaufany; REGON's by e-mail; the VAT white list's API is rate-limited to the point of being marginal for us. A **chain onboarding path**: the chain writes from its own domain and you assign many venues at once — which is exactly your 80-venue worry. And **automating the Instagram step later** through Meta's official messaging webhooks.

**Where I disagree with one or both:**

| Recommendation | Which report | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| **Approve automatically, with no person, on a domain e-mail** | Second (first report: at scale) | Any employee with a mailbox on the venue's domain — or an ex-employee whose box was never closed — would become the owner and read the reports. It also breaks our rule that a request grants nothing until a person approves. At a few requests a week the win is having the checks done so approval takes a minute, not removing you |
| **Reject automatically when the name is not on the KRS board** | Second | The form rightly offers "Manager" and "Pracownik"; they are never on the board. Name matching breaks on diacritics and married names. And its fallback — ask for a utility bill — contradicts its own privacy argument |
| **Transfer ownership automatically after 72 hours of silence** | Second | This is the exact hijack both reports describe on Google: owners who ignore an e-mail lose their listing. Silence goes to you, never to a transfer |
| **"The phone field must be removed", citing the EU Court (C-394/23) and Polish anti-money-laundering practice** | Second (the first says keeping it is defensible) | Overstated. As I understand it, that ruling concerned a *mandatory* title field, not an optional one, and anti-money-laundering rules do not apply to a directory. The kernel is fair, though: a field we never use is hard to justify. See Open Question 2 |
| **Automated calls to the venue's public number** | First: later fallback. Second: advise against (landlines and VoIP fail) | Leave it out of the first build; revisit only if many venues have neither e-mail nor Instagram on record |
| Same check for the badge and the panel, with analytics protected by being owner-only | Second (the first wanted a stronger bar for the panel) | **Agree with the second.** One bar is simpler, and the owner role is what guards the reports |

**What I would build — waiting on your yes (Open Question 1):**
1. **On every request, a registry lookup by NIP** — KRS for companies, CEIDG for sole traders — shown on the request in the admin: company, status, address, and whether the claimant's name is among the people who can act for it. Supporting evidence only.
2. **A channel check, through a channel UNI recorded itself:**
   - if the claimant's e-mail is the venue's recorded e-mail, or on the venue's recorded website domain, they get a confirmation link at that address;
   - otherwise, after sending the form they see a short code to send as a message **from the venue's recorded Instagram or Facebook** to UNI's account, and you match it;
   - for a new venue, you first record its channels from its own public presence, never from the claimant, and the check goes there.
3. **You approve, with the signals on screen** — about a minute each. No automatic approval, no automatic rejection.
4. **A claim on an owned venue notifies the current owner.** An objection or silence comes to you.

Later, at about ten a day: the Instagram step automated, bulk onboarding for chains with one e-mail, owner-added managers. The form itself does not change, and `venue_claims` already has an unused column for a verification token.

**And the rule that makes this the gate for V2:** you — *"switch on is turned after having a way to check owners"* ([`owner-access-opens-only-once-owners-can-be-checked`](../decisions/product/owner-access-opens-only-once-owners-can-be-checked.md)). It is now the first step of the V2 opening list.

## What to Test in the Browser

Owner access is on in your local `.env`. Use `composer run dev` so e-mails land in the log.

1. **Open a venue page nobody manages.** Under the buttons: "To Twój lokal? **Zarządzaj nim samodzielnie w panelu lokalu**" first, "Coś się nie zgadza?" smaller below. Tap "Coś się nie zgadza?" — the correction form opens.
2. **Tap the owner link.** `/zglos-lokal` opens with that venue already picked. Tap "Zmień", type part of another name — results appear with addresses.
3. **Type a name that does not exist**, then "Dodaj nowy lokal" — the second tab opens with the name already filled in. Switch tabs: your own details below stay put.
4. **Send a new-venue request for a venue that is listed** (same name, city and street, even written "ul. …") — the page turns into a claim on the listed one, keeping everything you typed.
5. **Leave the phone empty** and send — it goes through.

### Checked against the real APIs first

Before building, both registries were asked directly rather than trusted from the research:
- **The VAT white list** answers without a key; an unknown NIP comes back as an empty result, not an error. **100 lookups a day**, per the Ministry's own page — the second report's "10 a day" was out of date.
- **KRS** answers without a key, and an unknown number is a 404. **It masks the board's names** — "I******* F*****", first letter and length. The second report said names were visible and only the PESEL was hidden; that was wrong. So KRS can only say "possible". ### What Was Built

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Automatic registry lookup** | In the background when a request arrives: white list by NIP, then KRS for a company.
