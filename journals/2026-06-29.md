# 2026-06-29 — External Services Audit & Transactional Email Decision

## Session Overview

Goal: figure out what's left for V1, then make a deliberate, cost-conscious decision about the external services the app depends on — with the explicit constraint that V1 is a *validation* phase, so no 12-month contracts and no recurring spend that drains the wallet if nobody shows up. The session started with a V1 status review, moved through a backups recommendation, then widened into a full external-services map. The centre of gravity became transactional email: we examined whether a US provider (Resend) is safe for an EU product, verified the legal facts against live sources, compared providers at scale, and landed on **Scaleway TEM** (French sovereign cloud) for transactional email. That decision was then wired into config and the privacy policy, recorded as a formal decision record, and reflected in the SSOT. No application logic or schema changed this session — the deliverables are a provider decision, its compliance plumbing, and documentation.

## What Was Built

### 1. `.env.example` — Production mail block switched to Scaleway TEM

**What changed:** The commented "Production Baseline" mail section was rewritten from a generic placeholder SMTP block (which also carried an outdated `MAIL_ENCRYPTION=tls` line, no longer used by Laravel 13's mailer config) into a Scaleway TEM-specific block: `MAIL_HOST=smtp.tem.scaleway.com`, port 587, username = Scaleway project ID, password = a TEM SMTP key, and a from-address on the dedicated `tx.undernoinfluence.pl` subdomain. A comment block documents the DNS setup (SPF/DKIM/DMARC on the subdomain) and an explicit warning that marketing email must use a separate provider on a separate subdomain.

**Why:** Until now `MAIL_MAILER=log` meant the app sent nothing — claim approvals, erasure responses, email verification and password resets were all silent. This is a pre-launch blocker. Documenting the exact production values means deployment is a copy-paste, not a research task.

**Why no change to `config/mail.php`:** Laravel's generic `smtp` mailer already drives Scaleway TEM with no code change — Scaleway is a standard SMTP relay. Adding a bespoke mailer would have been needless churn. The wiring is entirely `.env` + from-address + privacy disclosure.

**Current state:** Active in `.env.example`. Production `.env` still needs the real credentials (operator action).

### 2. `resources/views/privacy-policy.blade.php` — Two GDPR additions

**What changed:**
- **Section 3 (Cel i podstawa przetwarzania):** new bullet for *Powiadomienia transakcyjne (e-mail)* — names the transactional emails (claim confirmation, erasure response, email verification, password reset), states they are explicitly not marketing, and cites the legal basis: performance of contract / pre-contractual steps (Art. 6(1)(b)) and legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)).
- **Section 6 (Odbiorcy danych):** new paragraph naming **Scaleway SAS** (France) as an Article 28 sub-processor, EU-hosted infrastructure, processing only the address and message content for delivery, no transfer outside the EEA, no own-purpose use.

**Why:** Using a third-party to send email that contains a person's address and name makes that third-party a processor. GDPR requires both a lawful basis for the processing (Section 3) and disclosure of the recipient/sub-processor (Section 6). Doing this *now*, at the moment of choosing the provider, keeps the privacy policy truthful instead of trailing reality.

**Current state:** Active. Views compile cleanly (`view:cache` passed).

## Business Impact

**The email decision protects the compliance story you've been carefully building.** Every other part of the stack was deliberately kept on EU soil. Piping owner names and emails to a US service would have been the one inconsistent seam — and worse, a seam resting on a legal framework (the DPF) whose enforcement court is currently non-functional and which is being challenged in the same court that already killed its two predecessors. Choosing Scaleway means there is no transfer mechanism to maintain, nothing fragile to explain to UODO, and one fewer thing that can break out from under you.

**It also honours the validation-phase budget rule.** Scaleway TEM is pure pay-as-you-go: €0 at V1 volumes (you'll send dozens of emails a month against a 300/month free allowance), cents per thousand as you grow, and the only monthly-fee plan (€80) doesn't make financial sense until past ~1.2 million emails a month. No contract, nothing to cancel, nothing wasted if the idea doesn't take off.

**The transactional/marketing split is a quiet but important growth safeguard.** Your own launch plan includes cold B2B outreach and the possibility of owner-facing reports. The moment that kind of mail mixes with your password-reset channel, a single complaint spike can send critical emails to spam. Locking in the subdomain split now — before any marketing exists — means that failure mode is designed out, not discovered later.

## Learning Path

**Why an EU provider beats a DPF-certified US provider:** GDPR Chapter V restricts sending personal data outside the EU. A US provider needs a legal "bridge" — currently the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. That bridge's redress mechanism (the Data Protection Review Court) has been non-functional since the court's members were removed in January 2025, and a CJEU challenge is pending. Its two predecessors, Safe Harbor (2015) and Privacy Shield (2020), were both struck down. Picking an EU-resident provider removes the bridge from the equation entirely — there is no transfer, so there is nothing to invalidate.

**Transactional vs marketing email:** Transactional mail is triggered by one person's action (a receipt, a password reset, a claim approval) and is legally "necessary for the service," so it needs no separate marketing consent. Marketing mail is a broadcast and, in the EU/Poland, requires prior opt-in consent and a working unsubscribe. They also behave differently for deliverability — transactional has high engagement (good reputation), marketing has complaints and unsubscribes (reputation drag) — which is why best practice keeps them on separate sending domains.

**Why sender reputation is per-domain:** Gmail and Outlook decide whether your mail lands in the inbox partly by the reputation of the sending domain/IP. Splitting transactional onto `tx.` and marketing onto `news.` gives each its own reputation, so a bad newsletter campaign can't drag your password-reset emails into the spam folder.

**Useful references:** EDPB on international transfers (Chapter V); noyb.eu for the live DPF challenge status; Scaleway TEM docs for the SMTP endpoint and DNS records; SPF/DKIM/DMARC primers (the three DNS records every sending domain needs).

## Session 2 — SSOT Document Overhaul: Readable, Mobile-Safe, Dependency-Ordered

### Session Overview

Goal: turn the interactive SSOT (`inspo/interactive_v1_feature_spec_SSOT.html`) into the best possible version *to read* — not just a data dump you navigate by search. We started with a broad quality review (UX, mobile, information structure, freshness), then worked through every finding: a top-of-document summary panel, real mobile support, a big cleanup of repeated inline styling, and finally a full logical reorder so the document reads in dependency order (you never meet a concept before it's defined). Along the way the founder rightly challenged a premature "it's mobile-friendly" claim — rendering the page in a real browser exposed genuine horizontal overflow, which was then fixed and verified. The session closed by merging the redundant hero into the summary panel and marking database backups as an explicit, findable TODO. Net result: the SSOT now opens with a 10-second summary, flows Evidence → Decisions → Product → Future → Reference → Audits, works on a phone, and is ~1,300 inline-style attributes lighter.

### What Was Built

All changes are in `inspo/interactive_v1_feature_spec_SSOT.html` (plus two decision-vault files).

**1. "At a Glance" summary panel (new, top of document).** A gradient card that gives anyone the essence in ten seconds: a kicker identifying the doc ("UNI v1 · Single Source of Truth"), a canonical "Last updated 2026-06-29", a status pill, the four key metrics (63 active venues, 18 districts/1 city, 407 tests passing, 6 categories), a "What UNI is" column, and a "Where it stands — launch blockers" column. **Why:** a 6,800-line source-of-truth that can only be used by search is one nobody reads in full; the panel makes the document self-summarising and gives a single canonical home for current status.

**2. Freshness fixes.** The page `<title>` (stale at "2026-06-22") and the subtitle (stale at "2026-06-19") were corrected to 2026-06-29, and the canonical date now lives in the At a Glance panel. **Why:** the most visible freshness signal was a week out of date, which undermines trust in a document whose whole job is to be current.

**3. Real mobile support.** Previously, below 900px the entire sidebar (navigation *and* search) simply vanished with no replacement. Added a hamburger button that slides the sidebar in as a drawer over a dimmed backdrop, with full screen-reader attributes and close-on-tap/overlay/Escape. Added a back-to-top button and a proper label on the search box. **Why:** on a phone the document was previously an un-navigable 6,800-line scroll.

**4. Horizontal-overflow fix (the one the founder caught).** Rendering the page at phone width revealed the page was far wider than the screen — text clipped, stat cards pushed off-screen. Two root causes fixed: the mobile grid used `1fr` (which lets wide content stretch the page) instead of `minmax(0, 1fr)`, and 40 of 48 tables had no scroll container so they stretched the layout. Fixed the grid, constrained the main column, and wrapped every table in a border-free scroll container that scrolls internally on mobile and looks unchanged on desktop. **Why:** a document that overflows sideways on a phone is broken; verified fixed by rendering at 360px and 390px (no overflow) and confirming the drawer opens.

**5. Inline-style cleanup.** Extracted the dominant repeated inline styles (table cells and rows) into utility classes and converted **1,318 inline-style attributes** to classes (2,078 → 760, a 63% reduction), verified rendering-identical. **Why:** every future edit to this file (like today's) was fragile because table cells carried long repeated style strings; classes make edits safer and the file smaller.

**6. Full logical reorder.** Moved all 50 body sections and rebuilt the sidebar so the document reads in dependency order: Foundations now run **User Personas → Market Evidence → Decision Registry → Assumption Registry → Scoring** (evidence and personas before the decisions that rest on them; Scoring before the Discovery/Filters/SEO features that use it). FAQ and BRD moved out of the audit run back into Reference. The 21-section audit pile was regrouped from date-order into themed clusters: Status → Engineering → UX & Localization → Legal & Compliance, with the two accessibility audits now adjacent. Done with a validated script that asserts every section block survives exactly once. **Why:** the document previously presented decisions before the evidence behind them and defined Scoring eleven sections after it was first used — it contradicted its own logic.

**7. Hero merge.** The redundant "Corrected Spec / UNI v1 Feature Spec" hero was folded into the At a Glance panel (its identity became the kicker, its one real assurance — "actually built, not aspirational" — folded into the lede). The market-evidence stats it used to sit above now carry a clear "Why UNI exists — market signal" label. **Why:** two stacked intros doing the same job; one coherent opening reads better.

**8. Database-backups TODO (explicit).** The TODOs section's stale "All TODOs complete" meta was corrected, and a prominent red "OPEN PRE-LAUNCH TODO — Database Backups (added 2026-06-29)" callout was added with the decided approach (spatie + Backblaze B2) and the implementation plan. **Why:** backups are the top remaining launch blocker; the founder asked to make sure it's remembered and findable.

**9. Decision-vault files.** Created `decisions/product/ssot-document-structure.md` (the dependency-ordered, summary-first structure decision) and added it to the Decisions Log. The transactional-email decision record from Session 1 remains.

## Session 3 — SSOT: Version Ladder + Per-Version "Definition of Done"

### Session Overview

Goal: answer a sharp question the founder asked about the SSOT — *do the versions (V1, V2, V3) flow one after another, each built on the last, and does each version carry a checklist of exactly what must be done to call it complete?* The honest answer was "no, not clearly." The version order existed in the sidebar, but the document never made the **build-on-previous dependency** explicit, the gating logic (signal-triggered, not date-triggered) was scattered, and there was **no single per-version completion gate** — to know whether V1 was shippable you had to triangulate across three different sections. We agreed the signal-based gating is *correct* for a validation-stage product (a rigid waterfall would be wrong), so the fix was not to linearise the plan but to make the existing logic *visible*: a version ladder at the top and a Definition-of-Done block per version. Implemented all of it. Documentation-only — no app code or schema changed.

### What Was Built

All changes are in `inspo/interactive_v1_feature_spec_SSOT.html`.

**1. Version Ladder (new, in the "V1 Scope & State" section + a new sidebar link under Overview).** Three stacked cards — V1, V2, V3 — each stating what it *builds on*, what it *hands up* to the next version, its *entry trigger*, and a link to its completion gate. An intro line states the core mental model plainly: UNI does **not** ship V1 then build V2 then V3 in sequence — V1 ships the visible subset, and most of V2/V3 is already *built-but-hidden* in the codebase, unlocking on **market signals, not dates**. **Why:** the founder's first question — "do versions build on each other?" — had no clear answer in the document. The dependency story (e.g. V2 reuses V1's gating infra and dormant auth code; V3 builds on a proven V2 owner workflow) was real but implicit. The ladder makes it the first thing you see.

**2. V1 — Definition of Done (new, red callout in "V1 Scope & State").** The single shippable gate, consolidating the previously scattered launch blockers into one ✅/⏳/❌ list: 3 done (browse-only product scope; 407 tests + all audits; admin panel + B2B gating infra) and 6 open (database backups, Tailscale/Nginx admin gate, production SMTP, venue seeding & QA, DPA signatures, deploy ops). Cross-links to the At a Glance blockers and the Production Readiness audit. **Why:** there was no one place that answered "is V1 done?" — the answer was spread across At a Glance, the TODOs section, and the Production Readiness audit. This is now the canonical gate, and it's the one that matters most while you're in pre-launch hardening.

**3. V2 — Definition of Done (new callout at the head of the V2 Build Playbook).** States the entry trigger (three owner signals from three separate venues) and a crisp exit gate: *V2 is done when a stranger can register → verify email → submit a NIP-validated claim → get admin-approved → add their first product in under 10 minutes, and Basic analytics is live for owners at 20+ claimed venues.* Links down to the existing "V2 Scope Summary" for the item-level ✅/⏳/❌ rather than duplicating it. **Why:** the V2 section had a good item inventory but no single "done when…" statement; the entry/exit gate was the missing piece.

**4. V3 — Definition of Done (new callout at the head of the V3 Roadmap).** Entry trigger (100+ claimed venues self-updating + measurable retention) and exit gate (a second city launched on its own criteria, Pro/Premium analytics live over V1's gating infra, rarity scoring on a stable 150+ catalog; conditional features ship only on their own demand signals). Links to the existing "Version Progression Summary". **Why:** same gap as V2 — triggers existed but no consolidated completion statement.

**5. Anchors + cross-links.** Gave the existing "V2 Scope Summary" and "Version Progression Summary" cards stable `id`s so the new Definition-of-Done blocks link to them instead of duplicating their content, and added a "Version Ladder" entry to the Overview sidebar group. Verified: all six new anchors are defined exactly once and every internal link resolves; `<div>`/`<section>` tag balance unchanged (delta 0).

### Business Impact

**This turns "where are we?" into a one-glance answer.** The founder asked the exact question a future investor, partner, or developer will ask: do these versions build on each other, and how do we know when one is finished? Previously the document implied an answer but never stated it. Now the first thing a reader meets is a ladder showing that V1 is the foundation, V2 is already built and waiting on real owner demand, and V3 is scale work gated on traction — and each version carries a plain checklist of what "done" means. The V1 gate in particular consolidates the six things standing between you and launch into one unmissable list, so nothing critical hides in the noise. It also reinforces the discipline that makes this plan capital-efficient: you don't build V2 because a calendar says so, you build it when three real venue owners signal they want it.
