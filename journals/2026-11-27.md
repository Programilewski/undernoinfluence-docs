## Vendor terms, read 27.11

**ChoiceQR** (read from the PC; the web-fetch tool was answered with 429 by choiceqr.com). The partner terms (6.4) say the venue owns the menu content and ChoiceQR only holds a licence to show it. The user terms (8.5) forbid "data mining, robots or other similar tools… with the aim of reusing substantial parts of the content" and publishing "databases including substantial parts… (e.g. lists of Products and their prices)". Their "User" is defined as a person buying through the app. UNI reads one page per venue on its refresh interval and publishes only the alcohol-free drinks it lists, never the card or its prices. **eatbu**: its venue pages are the venue's own website built on DISH, link only a privacy notice and no visitor terms, and their menus are ordinary text, so they were never affected.

## Learning Path

- **Structured data (schema.org).** Why a page can show a cookie banner to a reader and a whole menu to Google: sites describe themselves in a hidden, standard format for search engines, and a menu is one of the things it can describe. Search "schema.org Menu MenuSection MenuItem".
- **Single responsibility in admin screens.** The same idea as a class with one job: a page should serve one stage of the work, so its buttons and columns all apply to every row it shows. Search "single responsibility principle".
