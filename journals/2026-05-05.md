## Decision Register

### D-01: Instagram content format — Carousel mini reports from DB data

- **Chosen:** Data-driven carousel posts (e.g., "10 miejsc z piwem 0% w Warszawie — stan na 05.05.2026"). Manual Canva creation for month 1, then automate in /admin if format proves successful.
- **Rejected:** (a) Single-image posts — less engaging, less data density. (b) Story-only content — ephemeral, no permanent discovery. (c) Fully automated from day one — premature investment before format validation.
- **Why:** Carousels allow multiple venues per post (high information density), encourage swipe engagement, and can be templated. Manual-first validates the format before engineering effort. 3x/week balances consistency with not spamming.
- **Status:** Permanent strategy direction. Automation is a Phase 2 admin feature.

### D-02: B2B outreach approach — Data-led, not sales-led

- **Chosen:** Cold emails that lead with demand insights ("82 people searched for NA wine in Mokotow this month — you don't have wine 0% listed"). The data creates curiosity, curiosity drives action.
- **Rejected:** (a) Generic "sign up for our platform" sales pitch — venue owners ignore these. (b) Phone calls — doesn't scale, too aggressive for a free tool. (c) No outreach (rely on organic discovery) — too slow for launch timeline.
- **Why:** Venue owners care about customers, not platforms. Showing them data about unmet demand in their area is immediately valuable — it answers a question they didn't know they could ask. The sign-up is the natural next step, not the pitch.
- **Status:** Template to be created. Requires PostHog data collection to have real search numbers.

### D-03: Target audience positioning — "People who've already decided"

- **Chosen:** Position UNI for people who have already decided to seek NA drinks — not people who need to be convinced. The platform is the answer, not the persuasion.
- **Rejected:** (a) Positioning as a "health" or "wellness" platform — too preachy, alienates casual users. (b) Targeting "sober" people specifically — too narrow, misses the driver/pregnant/health-conscious segments. (c) Being anti-alcohol — contradicts the brand name and philosophy.
- **Why:** The personas (professional after work, designated driver, pregnant woman, athlete, sober-curious) all share one trait: they've already made the choice. They don't need UNI to convince them — they need UNI to show them where to go. This keeps the brand neutral and the product functional.
- **Status:** Permanent positioning.

### D-04: Launch type — Soft launch, no press push

- **Chosen:** Quiet launch with 50-60 seeded venues, Instagram started, SEO indexing. No press announcement until there's proof (claimed venues, real traffic).
- **Rejected:** (a) Big launch event / press push — nothing to show yet, risk of "so what?" response. (b) Waiting for 100+ venues before going live — delays too long, prevents learning. (c) Beta invite system — unnecessary friction for a free platform.
- **Why:** A premature announcement with 50 venues and no claimed owners looks empty. Better to build quietly, prove the model works (owners claim, users find venues), and THEN announce with credibility. The press angle "Poland's first NA venue map with X claimed venues and Y monthly users" is stronger than "we just launched."
- **Status:** Permanent. Public announcement is a future milestone, not the launch.

### D-05: City #2 — Gdansk or Krakow

- **Chosen:** Second city will be Gdansk or Krakow, decided based on NA venue density and founder's ability to verify data.
- **Rejected:** (a) Expanding to all Polish cities at once — impossible to maintain data quality. (b) International expansion — premature, Polish market not yet proven.
- **Why:** Geographic expansion requires venue research, data verification, and district normalization for a new city. The founder needs to be able to physically or remotely verify venue data. Gdansk and Krakow are both large enough markets with active HoReCa scenes.
- **Status:** Decision deferred until Warsaw reaches 100+ venues and 30+ claimed.

### D-06: Phase 1.5 monetization — Small paid features before full premium

- **Chosen:** Introduce low-friction paid features (highlighted listing, priority alerts, custom badges) to test willingness to pay before building a full analytics dashboard.
- **Rejected:** (a) Going straight to Phase 2 premium tier — too much engineering investment without payment validation. (b) Staying free indefinitely — unsustainable.
- **Why:** The gap between "free for everyone" and "200 PLN/month analytics dashboard" is too large. Phase 1.5 bridges it by testing the smallest possible paid feature and seeing if venue owners reach for their wallet. If they won't pay 30 PLN/month for a highlighted listing, they won't pay 200 PLN/month for analytics.
- **Status:** Conceptual. Specific Phase 1.5 feature to be defined after launch.

## Learning Path

### For this session's work

- **Perplexity Spaces:** A Perplexity feature that lets you create a persistent knowledge base (uploaded documents, URLs, instructions) that all conversations within the Space can reference. Think of it as "custom GPTs but for Perplexity." Uploading the context doc means every Perplexity conversation about UNI has full project awareness.

- **Data-led outreach:** The B2B outreach concept (leading with demand data instead of feature lists) is inspired by how successful marketplace businesses acquire their supply side. Airbnb famously showed landlords how much they could earn. UNI shows venue owners how much demand they're missing. The principle: make the signup a natural response to information, not a response to persuasion.

- **Personas vs. demographics:** The 5 personas defined this session are behavioral, not demographic. They describe *situations* (after work, driving, pregnant, training, exploring) not *types of people* (age, income, location). This is more useful for content strategy because the same person can be in different personas on different nights.

### 2. Venue Seeding Infrastructure

**What:** JSON-based seeding pipeline. A template file (`database/data/warsaw-venues.json`) with field reference (`database/data/README.md`) and an idempotent seeder (`RealVenueSeeder.php`) that creates venues, auto-creates missing products, attaches custom drinks, and recalculates breadth scores.

**Where:** `database/data/`, `database/seeders/RealVenueSeeder.php`

**Why:** The #1 launch blocker is real venue data. This infrastructure lets the founder add venues one-at-a-time to the JSON, run the seeder, and incrementally build the database. Products that don't exist in the catalog are created automatically (brand guessed from first word, fixable in admin later).

### 3. Integrity Check Command

**What:** `php artisan uni:check-offer-freshness` — scheduled hourly. Fixes two drift scenarios: (1) `offer_updated_at` behind actual product changes, (2) `breadth_score` not matching fresh calculation.

**Where:** `app/Console/Commands/CheckOfferFreshness.php`, `routes/console.php`

**Why:** Data integrity degrades silently over time. A venue might get products added via admin but `offer_updated_at` doesn't update. This command catches and fixes drift before it affects the user-facing freshness display.

### 4. Freshness Config Extraction

**What:** Extracted the hardcoded 90-day freshness threshold into `config('uni.freshness_days')` across all 7 files that referenced it.

**Where:** `config/uni.php` (new), plus `Venue.php`, `CategoryController.php`, `StatsOverview.php`, `ProductsRelationManager.php`, `VenuesTable.php`, `SwiezoscRelationManager.php`, `VenueStatusWidget.php`

**Why:** Changing the freshness window (e.g., from 90 to 60 days) should be a one-line config change, not a grep-and-replace across the codebase.

### 5. "Warszawie" Removal

**What:** Replaced all hardcoded "w Warszawie" strings with dynamic `$city->name_locative` (with null-safe fallback to "Polsce").

**Where:** `discovery-page.blade.php`, `categories/show.blade.php`, `CategoryController.php`

**Why:** Multi-city readiness. When city #2 (Gdansk/Krakow) launches, these templates will display the correct locative form without code changes.

### 6. District Column Drop

### D-18: SEO pages — `/{city}/{district}/{category}` not `/{city}/{category}`

- **Chosen:** Empty city/district/category combinations return 200 with `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, follow">` and a helpful empty state with cross-links.
- **Rejected:** (a) 404 for empty combos — bad UX (dead link from internal navigation), and it would break the sitemap (which already filters these out). (b) Redirect to parent district page — loses the category context.
- **Why:** A user clicking "Wino" on the Mokotów page expects to see a page, even if it says "no venues yet." The noindex prevents thin content from polluting search results while the page remains navigable. As venues get added, the page naturally transitions to indexed.

- **Chosen:** Lightweight custom auth controllers (~200 lines total) using Laravel's built-in `Auth`, `Hash`, `Password` facades.
- **Rejected:** (a) Laravel Breeze (Blade stack) — would scaffold ~30 files (controllers, views, tests, Tailwind config) that conflict with the existing layout, design tokens, and Polish-language routing. (b) Fortify — headless but still adds a dependency and config complexity for what amounts to 4 simple controllers.

Breeze's scaffolding would need to be entirely rewritten to match. The custom approach is less code total and fits the existing patterns perfectly. No new dependency means one less thing to maintain.
- **Status:** Permanent. If auth complexity grows (OAuth, 2FA), revisit with Fortify.

### D-21: Panel access — keep `role=user` in owner panel for claim flow

- **Chosen:** B2C users (`role=user`) retain access to the owner panel (`/panel`), which they need for the claim flow.
- **Rejected:** Restricting the panel to `role=owner` only — this broke 4 existing tests covering the claim flow where a new user registers, enters the panel, and submits a claim.
- **Why:** The claim flow is: user registers → accesses panel → submits claim → admin approves → role upgraded to owner. If `role=user` can't access the panel, the claim flow breaks. The protection for B2C users comes from routing (public auth redirects to `/profil`) and from the `PanelAuthenticate` middleware (which redirects admins away from the owner panel).

### D-22: `@context` Blade escape — `@@context` workaround

- **Chosen:** Used `@@context` in JSON-LD templates to prevent Blade from interpreting `@context` as a directive.
- **Why:** Laravel 13 / Livewire 4 introduced a `@context` Blade directive. When writing JSON-LD inside a `@push` block, `@context` is parsed as PHP, causing a syntax error. The `@@` prefix escapes it to render as `@context` in the output.
- **Status:** Permanent pattern for all future JSON-LD in Blade templates.

## Learning Path

### For this session's work

- **Programmatic SEO:** The concept of generating many URL-addressable pages from database combinations (city × district × category) is a well-known SEO technique used by Airbnb (city × neighborhood × property type), Yelp (city × category), and Booking.com (city × hotel type). Each page targets a specific long-tail search query. The key to doing it well: only index pages with content (hence the `noindex` on empty combos), use structured data (JSON-LD) so Google understands the page hierarchy, and provide strong internal linking (breadcrumbs, cross-links) so Google can discover all pages efficiently.

- **ShouldQueue on notifications:** Laravel notifications that `implement ShouldQueue` are serialized to the configured queue backend (in this case, the `jobs` database table) instead of being sent inline during the HTTP request. A background worker (`php artisan queue:work`) picks them up and sends them. This means: (1) faster HTTP responses, (2) automatic retries if the mail server is down, (3) the app doesn't crash if SMTP fails. The Supervisor config ensures the worker automatically restarts after crashes or server reboots.

- **ULID vs UUID vs auto-increment:** The `venue_visits` table uses ULID (Universally Unique Lexicographically Sortable Identifier) as its primary key instead of auto-increment. ULIDs are 128-bit identifiers like UUIDs, but they're timestamp-prefixed, which means they sort chronologically. This gives you both: distributed ID generation (no sequence contention) and efficient B-tree index ordering (recent visits are physically adjacent on disk).
