## Decision Register

### 1. No brand names on any public drink label, for every brand — **revisit on measured traction**

**Chosen:** strip every brand, decided 19.10 and confirmed today. **Rejected:** (a) showing every brand as before, because the amendment adds the penalty that makes the existing ban enforceable, and a fine UNI can't pay outweighs brand-name traffic; (b) a switch left off until a lawyer answered (the 18.10 briefing), since there's no lawyer; (c) stripping only brands that share an alcohol name. That was my recommendation at first. You pointed out that a non-alcoholic-only brand can start an alcoholic line, so the flag is a promise to keep checking, and a missed check fails the wrong way. **Why I changed my recommendation:** the code is almost the same either way. The brand searches that mattered were for beer brands, which are stripped under both rules. And it's easier to add names to live pages later than to remove names Google has already indexed.

### 2. Menu lines describe the style, not the brand — **decided in principle**

**Chosen:** a style from a closed list, plus the 0,0/≤0,5 certainty already recorded. **Rejected:** category counts alone (below the threshold); one score in place of the menu (says nothing about what to order); free text (it slides back into the brand's own words). **Rule:** nothing that lets anyone work out the brand, so no origin, no packaging and no brand product words. **Open:** the list, draught vs bottle, and a KCPU follow-up about generic style words.

### 3. No paid legal opinion in V1 — **standing rule until the first paid feature or paying venue**

**Chosen:** no money on a lawyer while UNI earns nothing. **Rejected:** a 1–2k zł opinion after the Senate's text (the 18.10 briefing's Q2). **Why:** stripping brands removes the exposure for free.

### 4. House drinks are text only beyond V1 — **until a decision answers the faking problem**

**Chosen:** structured text, no images, `photo_path` removed. **Rejected:** keeping the column "for later". **Why:** generated images fake a drink photo for free; storage and moderation cost money. **Corrected on filing:** the pasted note called this superseding a "May 2026 recommendation" for drink photos. On 04.05 that option was rejected, not recommended. The example line's price ("22 zł") was left out under the price-is-not-tracked record.
