## The findings, in short

The full list is in the product note's 08.10 section; these are the ones that moved the recommendation.

1. **Classics are worth building.** Of 31 mixed non-alcoholic lines on nine Warsaw menus, **17 are a classic as written (55%)**, 2 a variant, 12 the venue's own. **Mojito is on 4 of the 7 venues** with mixed drinks. Two venues have no mixed drinks at all. Small, non-random sample.
2. **The list of 15 misses what menus carry:** Clover Club 0% and Garibaldi 0% were on menus; Mule 0% is the parent of a "Winter Mule". Paloma 0% wasn't seen.
3. **Poles search drink names for recipes and shops, and search places by category and city.** "Mojito bezalkoholowe" completes to przepis, Lidl, Biedronka, w puszce; "drinki bezalkoholowe" and "bar bezalkoholowy" complete to cities. No drink name came back with a city. A classic's page is an answer for people already on UNI, not the main door.
4. **The Polish form is "{name} bezalkoholowe"**, for every classic tested. "Nojito" isn't used in Poland.
5. **The app has stored classics as products since June**, under a fake brand "Własna receptura" and a name check for "receptura" in three views. Brandless products, confirmed spellings, the `/napoj/` page and its sitemap entry exist already.
6. **Never "0.0%" on a classic's page**: the same 0% ingredient is "LOW ALC" on one menu and inside a "Negroni 0.0%" on another.
7. **Thin classic pages are a spam-policy risk**, fixed the way districts already are: fewer than 3 live venues → kept out of the index and the sitemap.

## Business Impact

**The research says where classics earn their keep.** More than half of what mocktail menus serve is a classic, so recording them as free text throws away the core of UNI's answer to "where can I get this tonight?". But the searches say people find *places* through category and city, so the classic page is the answer once they're on UNI, not the way they arrive. That keeps the build small and the SEO bet on the city and category pages, where it already is.

**Building on products is cheaper and removes a workaround.** The same result with two columns instead of a parallel set of tables, pages and matching, and the "receptura" hack goes with it. Less to build before launch is the whole V1 direction.

**A Polish slug matches how people search.** "Mojito bezalkoholowe" is what Poles type; a URL decided once and right doesn't need a redirect later.

## Learning Path

- **Search intent: informational, transactional, local.** The same words ("mojito bezalkoholowe") can mean "how do I make it", "where do I buy it" or "where do I drink it"; Google ranks for the dominant one. Search "search intent types SEO" and "local intent keywords".
- **Autocomplete as a research tool.** It shows what people type often, never how often. Search "Google autocomplete keyword research limitations".
- **Doorway pages and thin content.** Why many near-identical pages hurt a site, and why an indexing threshold is the standard fix. Read Google's "Spam policies for Google web search".
- **Reusing an existing abstraction versus adding a parallel one.** When a new concept fits an existing table's meaning ("what can be ordered, independent of where"), extending it is usually cheaper than a twin. Search "avoid parallel hierarchies refactoring".
