## Decision Register

### 1. V1 launches as soon as it is stable; everything is sorted Launch / After launch / V2 / V3 — **permanent direction**

**Your reasoning:** search engines need time to index; waiting on V2-quality features delays that clock for nothing.

**Chosen:** the four buckets, with "cheap now, expensive later" as a reason to pull into Launch. Owner CRUD flows are V2: with the switch off, every production menu write is yours, with a source.

**Rejected:** a separate "cheap now" bucket (it's a reason, not a time), and keeping fault 1 first (it can't happen while the switch is off).

### 2. The workspace is organised by lifecycle, with a generated index — **permanent**

**Chosen:** the four new folders; every document described in its frontmatter; the list generated from git.

**Rejected:** keeping the folders and adding only an index (`roadmap/` stays a mix of six kinds of document); listing all 440 files in one index (decisions and journals already have their own); a hand-written list (it drifts, as the 18.08 README did); leaving old journal links untouched (about 880 links would have broken in Obsidian).

### 3. Launch with about 30 venues; add every eligible venue found — **decided**

**Chosen:** go live at ~30 rather than 50–60, and keep adding after launch through the same admin. "Best first" is dropped: you add what you find, in the order you find it. Before launch, each district you want in Google needs 3 live venues.

**Rejected:** the 3-items-across-2-categories threshold (it would push back a venue serving only 0% wines, and finding venues, not reading menus, is the slow part); a district quota (it would force weak venues in; the 3-venue check does the job); a quota by venue type (venue types have no pages of their own, so there's nothing to index).

### 4. Brands are created by hand; the products import refuses unknown ones — **decided, to build**

**Chosen:** the import matches brands ignoring capitals; an unknown brand fails the row.

**Rejected:** creating brands on import, as today ("heineken" and "Hieneken" would each get a public `/marka/` page).

### 5. A drink always needs a web address — **decided**

**Chosen:** option A. A drink seen in the venue but not published online waits until it is. This also covers printed menus and boards inside a venue.

**Rejected:** a dated visit as a source (it needs a "kind of source" field, and nobody else can check it).

### 6. Owners are never asked for a menu link or a justification — **permanent**

**Your reasoning:** they're already asked to keep their menu current; more questions and they bounce. Every owner action is recorded as theirs, and that is the answer in a dispute.

**Rejected:** my suggestion of an optional menu link recorded as the owner's statement.

### 7. What the badges mean, clarified — **direction; records revised in V2**

**Chosen:** no permanent manual admin check; **"Sprawdzona karta" will mean the owner manages the venue**, since UNI trusts the owner; "Zarządza właściciel" means a claim was approved; no source labels on public pages; the FAQ and other public copy are drafts written from the code.

**Conflicts with:** the what-the-badges-claim record (16.09: "a person checked this menu") and the 15.08 split of the two badges. Both are to be revised in the V2 badge step, not now.

**What remains of fault 1:** only the ranking lever, closed by decision 8.

### 8. One ordering for every list of venues — **decided (direction), formula open**

**Your words:** *"In discovery page, district ones and anywhere there are venues, we need one sorting, we try to push the best ones to the top. We want to reward venues that confirm the availability and the ones with the more generous offer, we'd have to combine it somehow."*

**Chosen:** one default rule on every page; confirmation rewarded as a state (within the window), never as recency, so no daily click buys position; generous offer rewarded with diminishing returns; choices like "nearest" stay. Recorded as `decisions/product/one-ordering-for-every-venue-list.md`. It must land before the owner switch.

**Rejected:** a rule per page (pages disagree about the best venue, and the drink page's lever stays); ordering by confirmation date alone (that is the lever).

**Also decided along the way:** unlimited house drinks are not a V1 problem (V2); the "still on the menu" button is needed for house drinks on both sides, the admin's after launch and the owner's with the evidence model; the substitution research is deleted.

## Business Impact

**Launching sooner starts the one clock money can't speed up.** Search engines need months to trust a new site. Every week spent polishing V2 features before launch is a week of indexing lost. Sorting the backlog showed that the launch is gated by about thirty venues and a handful of small fixes, not by the owner side.

**The classics model is where UNI's core promise becomes searchable.** "Where can I get a virgin mojito tonight" is exactly the question UNI exists to answer, and today it can't. With classics as shared pages, every venue that serves one strengthens a page people actually search for, without asking owners for anything more.

**One ordering keeps "can't be bought" true.** Placement that can't be paid for shouldn't be clickable either. A single rule that rewards a generous, confirmed offer is something you can explain to an owner in one sentence and defend in public.

**The reorganised workspace is cheaper to work in.** One backlog, one index, every document with a purpose and a date: less time re-deriving context at the start of each session, and less risk of acting on a stale document.

## Learning Path

- **Canonical entities and instances.** A shared, curated record (the classic) and the many local records that point to it (each venue's version). Untappd's beers and venue menus are the textbook case. Search "canonical record vs instance data model" and "Untappd naming standards".
- **Diminishing returns in ranking.** The fifth variant of the same drink should add less than the first, or the ranking rewards padding. Search "diminishing returns scoring logarithmic weighting".
- **Recency as a signal, and why it's gameable.** Anything a ranked party can refresh at will becomes a lever; review platforms use states and windows instead. Search "ranking manipulation freshness signals".
- **Docs-as-code.** Generated indexes, descriptions in frontmatter, and checks that fail when documentation drifts from what it describes. Search "docs as code" and "frontmatter metadata static site".
