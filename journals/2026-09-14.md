## Learning Path

- **Why a map is heavy.** A vector map has three costs: the library that draws it, the tiles (the map data for what is on screen) and the fonts and icons for labels. Tiles are fetched per view and their size depends almost entirely on zoom: one central-Warsaw tile is 202 KB at zoom 9 and 39 KB at zoom 12. Where a map *starts* is therefore a bigger decision than which library draws it.
- **Why "background loading" is conditional.** Prefetching makes the map instant for people who open it and costs data for people who never do. Chromium browsers report the connection quality, so the prefetch can wait for a good connection; Safari and Firefox do not, so they start on the first touch.
- **Stacking and taps.** Every fixed element has a stack order; a tap goes to the topmost element under the finger. A transparent full-screen sheet above the page makes the whole page untappable even when you can see it.
- **Per-address rate limits.** They stop a single machine, not a person with many addresses — and on IPv6 one customer's block holds 18 quintillion addresses. Serious login protection counts failures per account and adds a second factor.
- **A dead-man's switch.** Instead of a monitor asking "are you up?", the server says "I am fine" every few minutes; silence is the alarm. It catches failures that leave nothing running to raise one.
