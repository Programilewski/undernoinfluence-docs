### Data export — Art 20 right to portability

**Files:** `app/Http/Controllers/Auth/ProfileController.php`, `routes/web.php`,
`resources/views/auth/profile.blade.php`

**Route:** `GET /profil/dane` → `profile.export` (auth middleware)

**What it does:** Returns a streamed JSON download named `uni-moje-dane.json`. Payload:

```json
{
  "exported_at": "2026-06-15T...",
  "account": {
    "name": "...",
    "email": "...",
    "registered_at": "..."
  },
  "saved_venues": [
    { "slug": "...", "name": "...", "city": "Warszawa" }
  ]
}
```

Saved venues are eager-loaded with their city name via `->with('city:id,name')` to avoid the
`city` column ambiguity that caused a `column does not exist` error on the first attempt (the
`user_venue_saves` pivot also has an `id`, so all selected columns must be qualified as
`venues.id`, `venues.slug`, etc.).

**UI:** "Pobierz moje dane" button added to profile page (`profile.blade.php`) above the danger
zone, with a download icon and a description of what the file contains.

**Tests added** (`tests/Feature/Auth/AuthTest.php`):
- `test_user_can_download_their_data` — asserts 200, correct Content-Type, correct JSON shape,
  correct venue slug in payload.
- `test_data_export_requires_authentication` — asserts redirect to login for guests.
- `test_data_export_contains_no_saved_venues_when_none_saved` — asserts empty array.

Total auth tests: **46 passing**.

### Age gate corrected 16 → 18

**File:** `resources/views/auth/register.blade.php`

The checkbox previously read "Mam ukończone 16 lat." The 16 came from GDPR Art 8, which applies
only when the legal basis is consent (Art 6(1)(a)). UNI uses Art 6(1)(b) contract, so Art 8
never applied. Changed to 18.

---

### Privacy policy section 4 rewritten — cookie disclosure

**File:** `resources/views/privacy-policy.blade.php` (section 4)

The original section implied zero cookies. That was inaccurate: `laravel_session` and CSRF token
exist for logged-in users. Strictly necessary cookies are exempt from consent under Art 5(3)
ePrivacy / Art 173 Prawo telekomunikacyjne, but they must still be disclosed. Section 4 now
describes: (a) session cookies for logged-in users (strictly necessary, no consent needed),
(b) localStorage theme preference (functional, not personal data), (c) commitment to add consent
before enabling client-side PostHog.

## Decision Register

| Decision | Chosen | Rejected | Why | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lawful basis for B2C accounts | Art 6(1)(b) contract | Art 6(1)(a) consent | Consent can be withdrawn (Art 7(3)), which would collapse the account. Contract basis is stable and accurate — registration creates a service agreement. | Permanent |
| Portability format | JSON download, streamed response | Email on request | Self-service is faster, requires no manual fulfilment, and is standard UX expectation in 2026. | Permanent |
| Export scope | Name, email, created_at, saved venues | Also inaccuracy reports | Reports reference venues by FK — including them adds no privacy value but complicates the payload. Can expand if UODO asks. | Revisit if UODO audits |
| Owner self-delete | Blocked, redirected to email | Allowed with extra confirmation | `venues.claimed_by` FK nulls on delete, orphaning the venue. Proper offboarding requires human review. | Permanent |
| Age gate threshold | 18 | 16 (GDPR Art 8) | GDPR Art 8 is consent-only and irrelevant to UNI. 18 is correct under civil law (contractual capacity). | Permanent |
| Age gate legal basis | Polish Civil Code (full contractual capacity) | An unrelated statute cited in Session 3 | Civil Code gives a clean, accurate basis tied to the service agreement. | Permanent |
| PostHog in browser | Keep server-side only | Cookie consent banner | Zero UX friction before launch, zero UODO risk. Must add consent mechanism before ever loading posthog-js in the browser bundle. | Permanent until client-side analytics are needed |
| Marketing email basis | Soft opt-in (Art 13(2) ePrivacy) | Explicit consent checkbox at registration | Soft opt-in is lawful for existing users receiving info about similar services, as long as every email has a free unsubscribe link. Simpler for a solo founder. | Permanent for transactional-adjacent emails |

## Learning Path

**If you want to understand the legal landscape:**

- **GDPR Art 6** — lists the six lawful bases. For UNI: Art 6(1)(b) (contract) is the right one
  for user accounts. Art 6(1)(f) (legitimate interest) is the right one for analytics.
- **GDPR Art 13** — what you must tell users at the point of data collection. The privacy policy
  exists to satisfy this.
- **GDPR Art 20** — right to portability: users can request their data in a machine-readable
  format. The JSON download fulfils this.
- **ePrivacy Directive Art 5(3)** — the "cookie law": requires consent before storing/accessing
  anything on a user's device, with an exemption for strictly necessary storage. Session cookies
  fall under the exemption; analytics don't.
- **Kodeks cywilny** — Polish Civil Code. Full legal capacity from 18. This is the actual basis
  for UNI's age gate.

## Session 2 — V1 Browse-Only Strip

### Overview

**Goal:** Strip the app to browse-only for V1 launch. No registration, no accounts, no B2B
self-service. Validate the core hypothesis (do people search for zero-alcohol venues?) before
building user-facing auth infrastructure.

**Outcome:** All auth, saved venues, inaccuracy reports, and venue claim flows removed from
routing, UI, and tests. 403 tests passing. Branch: `v1`.

### Strategic Decision

The compliance burden of full auth (GDPR Art 13/17/20, DPAs, breach runbooks) is significant
for a product with zero validated user demand. Browse-only eliminates all of it: no personal
data collected = GDPR surface near-zero = no DPAs needed = can launch this week.

The auth system, models, migrations, and owner panel code are all preserved on `main` — not
deleted, just not activated in `v1`. Incremental unlock path: browse → localStorage saves →
registered saves → B2B self-service.
