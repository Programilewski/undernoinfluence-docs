## Session Overview

**Goal:** discuss the 01.10 idea — *"What if we actually made UNI community driven in v1/v2?"* — and decide on it; then look at how the brand could grow beyond the app and social media, what a community edition would add to V1, and how to treat a community fairly rather than "capitalise" on it. Along the way: why `@` in Claude Code doesn't list workspace files.

**Outcome:** no code changed. Two decision records were written for what you actually decided: the community direction and the order of brand work. The details stay open **on purpose**. The discussion settled the direction (community-driven, with proposals you review before anything goes live) and the order (traction from the app and social media first, outreach and physical presence after). But the first research answer showed several open questions — selling to producers, how much to trust contributors, what "anonymised" really means — that the records shouldn't fix before the evidence is in. One research round came back, was assessed against the code and records, and turned out to rest partly on a **wrong premise in the prompt**: it said producers can never pay UNI, when the records only forbid them buying placement. A corrected second prompt was too big for one deep-research run and stopped partway, honestly, rather than invent sources. What it finished is saved; the rest is split into three smaller prompts for tomorrow.

**Why `@` didn't list `docs/`:** the application's `.gitignore` has `/docs/` since the workspace became its own repository, and Claude Code's `@` picker skips ignored files. Starting a session from inside `docs/` (its own repository) brings them back; typing the path works too.

## Decision Register

### 1. UNI becomes community-driven, with every contribution reviewed by you before it is published — **direction adopted; scope waits for the research**

Recorded: uni-grows-through-a-community-whose-contributions-are-reviewed.

**Chosen:** people can propose venues and drinks; nothing is added automatically; you review. Contributing without an account. **Rejected for now:** reviews (ADR-006 still holds — one review looks dead), users adding venues directly, and pulling public accounts into V1. **Why:** the bottleneck you named — verifying 70, 100, 200 venues alone — is real, and a motivated niche is the cheapest way to keep data current. But moderation doesn't disappear; it changes shape, so the formats stay narrow. The direction is recorded; the records for scope, timing and sign-in are deliberately not written until runs 1–3 are back.

**Found while checking the code:** V1 already has one community channel. The guest "Zgłoś coś w tym lokalu" report on every venue page works without an account, with an optional e-mail and a honeypot. The 24.08 community-reporting-needs-accounts record still reads as if nothing were live. And `product_proposals` exists but is owner-only.

### 2. The proposed V1 community edition — **proposed, awaiting your answers**

In order: a guest "Zaproponuj lokal" form (with a second intent, "I wish this venue had an NA option"); a one-tap "still here / gone" per drink that only moves a venue up your recheck queue; "Brakuje napoju" in the existing report modal with the drink picked from the catalogue; a "dodane dzięki Wam" e-mail; a public "Nowe na mapie" page that doubles as Instagram/TikTok material; one triage queue in the admin; and a measured trigger, written as a number, for switching accounts on. **Not in V1:** accounts, experience tags, scouts, lists, awards.

### 3. The brand beyond the app comes after traction — **adopted (your call)**

Recorded: brand-outreach-waits-for-traction.

**Chosen:** first get known through the app and Instagram/TikTok; partnerships with groups and a criteria-based window sticker come after. Your reasoning: *nobody trusts a website created yesterday*. **Kept as parked ideas, to be written down:** a twice-yearly data report ("Bezalkoholowa Warszawa") for the press, the criteria-based sticker (never paid), running-club and sports partnerships, a data-generated newsletter; later, awards, venue-hosted "swap one" nights, data for city guides. **Rejected:** influencer collaborations (faceless-brand), producer sponsorship (mission), merch, podcast, festival booths, and targeting recovery communities.

**Proposed framing, not yet decided:** the brand promotes *the person ordering the NA option*, not *NA beverages* — because much of the 0.0% shelf carries an alcohol brand's label, and promoting the category ends up advertising beer brands. Correction made during the session: there is already a permanent `/suchy-styczen` page with its own record; "swap one" stays only an idea.

### 4. The first research answer is used as a framework, not as evidence — **assessment**

It labelled every claim honestly but marked most of what was asked as not researched: half the precedents, Reddit and Stack Overflow, and the whole motivation literature. So most of its advice is the model's own reasoning. **Kept:** count "signals", never "people" (an anonymous tap can't be a headcount — Claude had written "37 people" earlier and was corrected); accountless contribution permanent; a receipt code per anonymous submission; a published review-time target; no points, streaks or leaderboards; its north-star metric, "verified healthier discoveries assisted". **Pushed back on:** open-licensing the venue data now (an open copy is a free lead list, against venue-lists-are-never-sold-as-leads), the 20–30 venue pilot (premature), and recovery signposting beyond a careful minimum.

### 5. The producer premise in the first prompt was wrong, and the corrected position is now the base for all research — **correction, permanent**

Claude's first prompt said brands and producers cannot pay UNI. The records say otherwise: venue-lists-are-never-sold-as-leads expects producer revenue **from aggregates**, and the mission record calls brands instruments "even where they are also customers". What is forbidden is placement, ranking, inclusion and any influence over what the public sees. Every later prompt carries the correct position, and whether producers should buy aggregates at all is now an open question.

**What the partial second run adds:** a higher bar for producers than for venues (they can manufacture demand); **no contributor score at all at launch**; trust later from confirmed accuracy only — WikiTrust-style, scoped and decaying; gamification measurably lowers accuracy (d = −0.68 and −0.40 in a preregistered experiment and its replication); a newcomer reverted on their first try is about half as likely to continue; GUS's own suppression rule (fewer than three entities, or one entity dominating) as a reference for minimum counts; show the KCPU helpline (800 199 990) only on explicit help-related searches, recording nothing.

### 6. Research is run in three smaller prompts, each standalone — **adopted**

**Chosen:** split the remaining 27 items into three runs of 8–10. **Rejected:** loosening the rules so one run could "finish" — the interrupted run refused to cite unread sources, which is the behaviour wanted. **Why:** a deep-research run has a fixed budget of searches and pages; 42 sourced sections doesn't fit in one.

## Business Impact

Nothing shipped, but the session settled **how UNI scales its data without scaling you**. The plan is a community that tells you where to look, not one that edits the map: every contribution lands in your queue, so accuracy — the thing the brand rests on — stays in one pair of hands while the amount of checking you do drops.

It also stopped a mistake before it reached a record. The first research answer would have had UNI promise publicly that producers can never be customers, against what the business model already assumes. Selling aggregates to producers may still turn out to be wrong, but that should be a decision made on evidence, not a sentence written by accident.

And it surfaced three things worth fixing whatever the community plan becomes: the privacy policy's "anonymisation" may claim more than the data does, an old decision would have built health profiles by inference, and nothing yet limits how small a number analytics may show. Each is cheaper to fix now than after the first venue pays.

## Learning Path

- **Two-sided platforms and trust.** Why a marketplace that sells to one side must protect the other. Search: "two-sided market trust neutrality", and Yelp's advertiser-bias history as the cautionary case.
- **Motivation crowding-out.** Why paying or gamifying volunteers can lower quality. Start with Gneezy & Rustichini, "Pay enough or don't pay at all" (2000); run 2 covers the rest.
- **Anonymisation vs pseudonymisation.** The Article 29 Working Party's Opinion 05/2014 — the three tests: singling out, linkability, inference. This is the basis for the `note` debt item.
- **Small-cell suppression.** Why a statistic about two venues identifies them. Search: "statistical disclosure control small cell suppression"; GUS's own rule is the local reference.
- **Getting more from deep research.** Fewer, tightly scoped items per run; demand a coverage table; require proof of searching before "not found".
