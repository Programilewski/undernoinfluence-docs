# Keep OSM beneath UNI, not inside

Three premises change first: **Nominatim left the app on 09.10.2026**, so there is nothing to "keep using"; **GUGiK's misses are mainly UNI's own matching rules**, which reject 51% of a hard address set while GUGiK answers correctly; and **the osm-dropped record misreads ODbL**, because a Produced Work is expressly not share-alike and commercial use is expressly granted. A legal route to OSM venue data therefore exists, but I recommend not taking it now: it would solve discovery, and discovery is not the bottleneck, since OSM lists 3,576 Warsaw venues against his worry about 100 and holds nothing on what any of them pours. The recommended architecture is one rule per data type across Poland: basemap all OSM, venue identity and drinks all non-OSM, candidates from Overture Places rather than OSM, and a repaired GUGiK with at most a per-venue Nominatim fallback. Community work helps only if signals decide which venues get re-read; otherwise it adds a second inbox to a blanket re-read that already sets the limit, somewhere between about 150 and 1,300 venues at 5 hours a week depending on one unmeasured number, the minutes per re-read. Financially, location data is not the moat, UNI pays its cash costs at about 4 paying venues and its social contributions at about 27, and it replaces a Warsaw salary only at about 143, against a Warsaw base case of about 10. No listed-to-paying benchmark exists, so a cheap paid test matters more than any data decision here. This is not legal advice: the guidelines are the licensor's non-binding reading, and no court has ruled on this question for POI data.

**How to read the evidence.** *Measured* means run on 30.10.2026 against live services; the scripts sat in a temporary session folder, so rerun rather than look them up. *Sourced* means a published text I link and did not reproduce. *Assumed* means no source exists and the number should be replaced by measurement. *Derived* means arithmetic on the above. *Record* means UNI's own decision records, journals or code. The measured samples are 26–46 items, with 95% Wilson intervals, so **a gap of ten or fifteen points between two numbers here is not evidence of a real difference**. Licence verdicts are my reading of the texts and "safe" means covered by guideline text, not guaranteed by a court.

## Three premises fall, and two smaller facts move

**Nominatim is already gone.** The 09.10 amendment to osm-dropped removed its four admin buttons and the service for three stated reasons, "none of them a new reading of the licence": GUGiK supplies the point and the district, the reverse lookup wrote OSM's street, number and postcode into the venue, and it sat one mis-click from GUGiK during the launch load. GUGiK is now the only geocoder and there is no fallback (Record, geocoding-comes-from-the-state-register). Any return is a reversal to justify, not a continuation.

**"Every second address fails" is real, and the fault is mostly ours.** On a hand-built hard set of 74 addresses, UNI's rules accept **36 (49%, CI 38–60)**. Of the 38 rejections, **21 come from stripping the type prefix on the register's side only**, so typing "Aleja Krakowska" compares `alejakrakowska` with `krakowska` and fails a perfect 1.0 match; 7 are an accuracy cutoff rejecting a correct top answer; 6 are input format such as `Rondo ONZ 1`; 3 are correct rejections; and 1 may be genuinely missing (Measured, [GUGiK UUG](https://services.gugik.gov.pl/uug/)). Forty real Warsaw permit addresses tell the same story: **0 of 40 pass when typed with "ul."**, 34 of 40 pass in the short form, and a rewritten rule accepts 38 of 40 (95%, CI 83–99). I found no address confirmed to exist and missing from the register. I could not see the addresses he actually tried, because the local database holds only fixtures, so which rate he personally met depends on how he typed them.

**Nominatim is not better once the rule is fixed.** It "finds" 71 of 74, but **only 65 at the exact house number** and 5 as a bare road with no error raised; the rewritten GUGiK rule accepts 64 plus 2 flagged ambiguous (Measured, [Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/)). That is a tie, not a win, on a sample judged by agreement and hand checks rather than surveyed truth. Nominatim does resolve old street names and `Rondo ONZ 1`, and it missed `Piękna 20` entirely. GUGiK answered every request without an error. The earlier belief about Polish government geocoders holds for GUS, whose search the 12.09 record found answering nothing; I did not reconstruct its request, so that stands unconfirmed by me today.

**osm-dropped is wrong on its central claim.** It says ODbL "requires that any 'produced work' derived from the database remain openly licensed". The licence says the opposite: using a database to create a Produced Work "does not create a Derivative Database" (s.4.5b), and rights "explicitly include commercial use" (s.3.1) ([ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)). The OSMF adds that you may license a Produced Work "under any terms you like" ([OSMF Licence FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ)). Its "taint" worry is true only for a Derivative Database, and which side UNI falls on is a design choice. Its practical fear survives in a narrower form, because a website does not escape: a public Produced Work from a Derivative Database counts as public use of that database (s.4.4c) and triggers a free machine-readable offer (s.4.6). Its word "existential" overstates the risk. The enforcement I found is letters and escalation, mostly about attribution, with a cure path in s.9.4 ([LWG 2023-10-16](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licensing_Working_Group/Minutes/2023-10-16), [LWG 2026-02-09](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licensing_Working_Group/Minutes/2026-02-09)). That is no licence to ignore it: Geolytica was caught by leftover tags and Dopper by identical coordinates ([LWG 2025-07-14](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licensing_Working_Group/Minutes/2025-07-14)).

**Two smaller corrections.** The research notes speak of a 60–70 venue launch and a 70-venue target; the backlog has said **about 30 catalogued, 35 done, since 07.10** (Record). And `Venue::activateIfReady()` is an activation gate with no date in it, so a stale venue stays live with a stale date (Record). The rule in osm-dropped that names CARTO tiles is also out of date, since the map moved to OpenFreeMap on 01.09.

## The founder's worry is about re-reading, not finding

**Finding is cheap.** OSM holds 3,576 Warsaw food, drink and hotel venues (fast food could not be fetched, so it is excluded) and about 34,000 venues plus 6,400 hotels in Poland (Measured, [Overpass](https://maps.mail.ru/osm/tools/overpass/api/interpreter); [Geofabrik taginfo](https://taginfo.geofabrik.de/europe:poland/api/4/tag/stats?key=amenity&value=bar)). What OSM cannot do is pass UNI's gate: **2 of 3,576 Warsaw venues have any `drink:*` tag and neither concerns non-alcoholic drinks**. Polish totals for `drink:non_alcoholic` and its variants are 0. Full addresses reach 64% and websites 34%, and 48% of venues were edited in the last 12 months, though a last edit does not prove a check. The closed rate could not be measured: my range is 3–20% of a sample of 30, of which 21 had no website to test.

**The yield is about 140 venues, loosely.** Of 30 random Warsaw bars, pubs and restaurants with websites, 14 had a readable menu and **5 (17%, CI 7–34) named an explicit non-alcoholic item**, or 9 (30%) counting lemonade and kombucha. Derived: 2,417 venues × 35% with a site × 17% gives **about 143 (CI 59–286)**. That is a lower bound, since no JavaScript or images were read and only 6 of the 30 were bars or pubs. It also hangs on a product definition still open: whether a plain juice counts. If it does, most restaurants pass. So 100 Warsaw venues exist to find, and a person with a browser would find more than my script did. Whole-of-Warsaw completeness is not on offer from any source, because the filter is the menu.

**Keeping them current is the real question, and one number decides it.** Taking the community model's capacity of 1,304 minutes a month (5 h/week), and with every input Assumed except the 90-day window from `config/uni.php`:

| Scenario | 100 venues | 500 | 2,000 | Venues at 5 h/week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Founder alone, every venue every 90 days, 3 min per re-read | 0.4 h/wk | 1.9 | 7.7 | **1,304** |
| Same, 8 min | 1.0 | 5.1 | 20 | **489** |
| Same, 26 min (what "stops working at 100–200" implies) | 3.3 | 17 | 66 | **150** |
| Base community added, still 90-day, 3 min | 0.8 | 3.8 | 15 | **652** |
| Base community, signal-led, 3 min | 0.5 | 2.5 | 10 | **1,003** |
| Base community, signal-led, 26 min | 1.4 | 6.9 | 28 | **362** |
| Base community, signal-led, AI closes 50%, 3 min | 0.3 | 1.4 | 5.5 | **1,824** |

**So 100 venues is realistic on every row: at most 3.3 hours a week.** His feeling becomes true somewhere past that, and only timing can say where. Note what the table shows about community: added to a blanket 90-day cycle, it *halves* capacity, from 1,304 to 652. It raises capacity only once signals decide what gets re-read. The churn inputs are not Polish and none measures a non-alcoholic menu line: closure of about 11–18% a year is drawn from a western US restaurant study ([microdata study](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/1410.8603)), one Málaga OSM diary ([diary](https://www.openstreetmap.org/user/alan_gr/diary/405457)) and Washington DC licences ([DC Policy Center](https://www.dcpolicycenter.org/?p=1529)). The 3% monthly offer change has no source at all. **Recommendation: time 20 re-reads before any other work in this report.** It rests on the table above, follows nothing and reverses nothing, and costs an hour or two.

## One architecture resolves the disagreement in the notes

**The disagreement.** The licence notes show a legal route; the community notes recommend no OSM work; the financial notes see no financial case for reversing osm-dropped. They do not actually conflict, because they answer different questions. *May* UNI? Yes. *Would it pay?* Not on current evidence, since no revenue line sells location and the costly work is checking menus, which OSM does not supply. *Would it fit the product?* Poorly, since OSM holds venue facts and UNI holds dated drink evidence. **The resolution: correct the record's reasoning, keep its rule, and replace "never" with a trigger.**

**The legal route, stated fairly.** Under the [Collective Database guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Collective_Database_Guideline_Guideline), UNI could use OSM as the whole venue identity layer, publish that layer under ODbL, and keep its drink tables closed, keyed by `osm_id`. The guideline's phone-number example is nearly UNI's shape, and reference by key is expressly allowed. What gets released is name, address, coordinates, contacts and every correction to them; `venue_products`, `venue_drinks`, `products`, scores, plans and demand tables stay closed. Commercial use survives, and [Overture's own split](https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/) is a structural precedent. I found no named closed venue product publicly documenting this design and no court ruling on it. The record's "untested in Polish courts" remains fair, though the collective route is a defined licence term with a board-endorsed guideline, not the improvised "exemption" the record calls it.

**The gap-filling trap is why this route is all or nothing.** The design UNI would naturally want is OSM plus the venues OSM lacks. That is the guideline's own negative example: a proprietary restaurant list complemented from OSM with duplicates removed "would not be covered" and "may be subject to" share-alike. The [Horizontal Layers guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Horizontal_Map_Layers_-_Guideline) says the same of adding only restaurants absent from OSM: they "must be shared". And it would bite immediately: OSM found **30 of 40 random permit venues by name (75%, CI 60–86), 22 at the address**, so about a quarter of real venues would need adding (Measured, sample of licensed venues only; fast food excluded, so the true figure is likely somewhat higher). The all-OSM route means every missing venue goes into OSM first, under [Contributor Terms](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Contributor_Terms), before UNI can list it. The founder's review could not govern a base anyone can edit, and a correction loop would rest on very few people: **one account is the last editor of 35% of Warsaw venues** (Measured).

**The choice per data type.** The guidelines work per data type within a regional cut, and "[the Regional Cut is at least a country](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Regional_Cuts_-_Guideline)", with smaller areas an open discussion. So UNI cannot run OSM in Łódź and its own data in Warsaw. Each row below is one choice for all of Poland:

| Data type | Choice | Source | Rests on, and the condition to protect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basemap (roads, land, labels) | **All OSM** | OpenFreeMap | Credit already in the style. I checked `uni-dark.json`: it has no `poi` layers, which matches the guideline's safe example of a base map without restaurants beneath your own. Do not add them |
| Name, street, number, postcode, type | **All non-OSM** | Venue's own source, GUGiK | Each field carries a logged non-OSM URL, per the FAQ's "compare but not take" |
| Coordinates | **Non-OSM by default** | GUGiK | The Geocoding guideline, not the all-or-none rule, governs a fallback (below) |
| District | **All non-OSM** | GUGiK cadastre | Never Overture Divisions, which are ODbL |
| Phone, website, e-mail, Instagram | **All non-OSM** | Venue's own site | Overture may point at a site; the value is read at source |
| Drinks, offers, evidence dates | **All non-OSM, no exception** | UNI evidence | One OSM `diet:*` or `drink:*` value breaks the column's all-or-none and pulls it into a release |
| Community contributions | **All non-OSM** | Sender's visit or a public venue source | The sender states it was not copied from a database such as OSM or Google Maps |
| Demand data | First-party | `events`, stats tables | Not a licence question |

**Geocoding sits outside that all-or-none.** Results "may be stored and used together with other proprietary or third party data without having a share-alike impact", the guideline's store example is close to UNI, and a mixed fallback with non-OSM results is its own worked case ([Geocoding guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Geocoding_-_Guideline), endorsed 2017-08-24). So a fallback is licence-permitted, and the record's ground for permitting Nominatim reached the right result for the wrong reason, since the OSMF rejected the Produced Work reading of geocoding. **Recommendation: fix the rule first, and treat the fallback as an option to use only if a residue justifies it.** It rests on the 49% to 86–89% move on the hard set and 95% on permits, at about 6 ms a call, with no provider change. A small alias list covers `Rondo ONZ 1`. If misses remain, add one per-venue, forward-only, admin-triggered Nominatim button that stores only the point and matched label, with no bulk action and no reverse lookup. That removes the record's actual reasons for removal: the reverse write and the one-click launch risk. It would amend both the osm-dropped amendment and "there is no fallback provider". The cost is a site credit and a provider that may withdraw access. The [Nominatim usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/) must be followed by any code written for this, generated code included as the policy states: at most 1 request a second, valid identification, attribution, cached results, no systematic POI queries, no auto-complete, no personal data, and regular tasks go elsewhere. A self-hosted Poland instance or Photon would be a recurring cost against the V1 infrastructure rule for a job measured in single requests, and no published figure confirms a Poland hardware size. Hosted OSM geocoders such as [OpenCage](https://opencagedata.com/pricing) and [LocationIQ](https://locationiq.com/pricing) exist, but their storage statements came from pricing pages and need reading in the contract.

**Overture is the discovery source, and osm-dropped itself invites it.** The record's Option 4 said "no viable alternative with comparable Polish venue coverage" and its revisit line says to evaluate one if it emerges. **That condition is arguably met now.** [Overture Places](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/places/) contains no OSM data per its own guide and is licensed CDLA Permissive 2.0, Apache 2.0 or CC0 by provider ([attribution](https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/)). Measured on release 2026-10-21.0: 8,240 Warsaw food, drink and hotel rows in a box larger than the city, 5,657 at confidence ≥0.7, website on 67% against OSM's 34%. Recall on the same 40 permits was **33 (82%, CI 68–91) against OSM's 30**, which is not a measurable difference, so the case rests on licence and contact data rather than coverage. Honest costs: Overture's docs warn of "duplicates, a high junk rate, and low property completeness"; I measured recall and **not precision**, so the junk rate in Warsaw is unknown; 87,096 of 105,246 Warsaw places originate from Meta, so its website gain may largely be social pages; provider data comes "AS IS" with no warranty of title, and a vendor can carry hidden OSM, as Geolytica did ([LWG 2023-10-16](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licensing_Working_Group/Minutes/2023-10-16)). The hobbyist comparison that favoured OSM (72 against 60 of 100 global restaurants) is small, not Polish and partly unverified. Foursquare OS Places could not be sampled without an account. Take the Places theme only, use it as a pointer and do not copy its values. Never join it to OSM: Overture itself warns the result "may need to carry" ODbL. That rule costs something real, since the two together reached 36 of 40 (90%, CI 77–96), but the union is exactly the grey list this architecture exists to avoid.

**Municipal permit lists complement it, as internal worklists only.** Warsaw's [register](https://kalkulatoralkoholi.um.warszawa.pl/alkohole/cbip_zezwolenia_lst.asp) has 16,397 permits, 2,742 on-premise type A, each with name, address and dates, and a body with a duty to keep it current; an end date gives a natural recheck prompt. But it states no re-use licence and has no bulk export, a full pull is about 1.6 hours of requests, I did not examine the re-use position, and venues with no such permit are absent by construction. Gdańsk publishes a [CC BY 4.0 CSV](https://api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets/32481) with 850 gastronomy rows, whose entity column names sole traders: do not store it. No permit also does not mean closed. UNI never cites these lists publicly, since doing so would read as a position it does not take.

**The launch worklist is grey and should stop being used.** `uni_filtered_venues.csv` (2,378 rows, with address, phone, amenity type and `osm_id`) is OSM-derived and now sits outside the repository as reference only (Record, reference-material). Journals show the launch triage started from it, and coordinates were kept out by the 12.09 decision. Whether the 35 catalogued venues' names, addresses and phones can each be shown as non-OSM is a data question I cannot answer from here. No OSMF text covers "OSM as a discovery list, then independent verification": FAQ s.4 addresses verifying your own data, while Horizontal Layers says adding restaurants "based on comparison with" OSM must be shared. My reading is defensible but uncovered. Risk falls where `osm_id` is not stored, every field has a logged non-OSM URL, the pool has other sources, and selection follows a qualitative test. The [Substantial guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Substantial_-_Guideline) helps a launch of about 30 only partly: under 100 features is fine if "one-off and not repeated", it warns "we regard repeated small extractions as one big extraction", and it calls systematically extracting all eating places in an area systematic. The 07.10 rule "every eligible venue found is added" sits uneasily with that if the list stays the source. **Recommendation: before the production load, check each launch row's identity fields against a logged non-OSM URL, re-source any that cannot show one, and draw nothing further from the CSV.** Production starts empty, so this is cheapest now. About 35 rows at a few minutes each (Assumed).

**Amend osm-dropped, with this content.** Correct the Produced Work sentence and the "existential" claim, change "core asset" to "the enriched offer data", keep "No OSM venue attributes may be stored in the venues table" for its new reason (the gap-filling trap and no payoff, not incompatibility with commercial use), and replace "Never for OSM specifically" with a trigger. I propose reopening only if all three hold: timed work shows discovery rather than re-reading is the binding limit; the Overture precision test fails; and he accepts listing only what OSM holds, feeding missing venues upstream and releasing the identity layer. The release would also end canaries' use in that layer and reveal the list of venues UNI found worth listing, which is the real competitive loss. Asking legal-questions@osmfoundation.org is free but volunteers "may not be able to respond quickly", and the answer would not bind contributors. Trivial Transformations also carries a status conflict: its own page still says "proposal stage" while the index lists it as endorsed.

## Community can lower the load only if signals pick the re-reads

**Already decided, and followed here.** Nothing publishes before the founder's review, no account is needed, there are no points, streaks or leaderboards, no brand can steer, and a signal can only move a venue up the recheck order (Record, uni-grows-through-a-community-whose-contributions-are-reviewed, with its 06.10 amendment). The comparables support the warning behind it: no platform publishes moderation minutes, but a developer at HappyCow calls its summer queue "HUGE" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id435871950)), Foursquare reports "millions" of unprocessed edits under its old system ([Foursquare](https://foursquare.com/?p=44111)), and a small core does most of the work, with 5% of OSM's registered members productive in one 2012 study ([study](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijgi/ijgi-01-00146/article_deploy/ijgi-01-00146.pdf)). [Barnivore](https://www.patreon.com/barnivore/about), where anyone gathers evidence and a small team reviews it before it enters, is the closest model. I found no visitor-to-contributor rate for any place directory, and there is no organised Polish community to borrow (Record, run 2).

**Recommended first form, moved from V2 to After launch.** One tap on a drink line, "Tego już nie ma"; on venue pages and the map, a venue name plus one link. No name, e-mail, reason or free text. It rests on value per review minute: a "gone" tap costs nothing until it triggers one re-read, and a link is 2 minutes against 5 for free text, 4 plus storage for a photo and 10–15 for a new venue (all Assumed; time 20 of each). Photos wait, since they carry faces, copyright and EXIF, and generated menus are now cheap. They would also need their own amendment to no-venue-photos-v1. The backlog put the guest form in V2 because it adds moderation to launch weeks; that reason stands for launch itself, so this moves it to just after launch, not before. Skipping free text also lets UNI avoid most of the pre-publication duty the [Russmedia judgment](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102lx4z/the-ecjs-russmedia-judgment-a-paradigm-shift-for-online-platform-liability/) creates for sensitive data, which I read through commentary only and whose scope is contested.

**Recommended re-read model.** Re-read on a signal, on an unchanged-hash miss, or from a random audit weighted by age and traffic, in place of every venue every 90 days. It rests on the table above (1,003 against 652 venues at 3 minutes), and it follows the 02.10 record rather than reversing it. Hashing a venue's own menu page needs no model and no review, though a page can stay the same after a drink has gone and Instagram or image menus are out of reach. Honest cost: quiet venues show older dates and get checked less, and the 5% audit is an assumption. Modelled staleness is about 6% of venues on a 90-day cycle and 12% on 180 (Derived, from the Assumed 4.2% a month). An honest stale date is acceptable; a quietly false badge is not, so this belongs in the still-open badges-are-rules-over-recorded-evidence decision.

**Abuse control weighs evidence, not people.** Without accounts no identity exists to weigh. The prize is already small because a fake signal publishes nothing, so what remains is wasted founder time. Cap it: one venue moves at most one step per period however many taps arrive, a hundred taps never outrank one link on the venue's own domain, links with campaign identifiers or to a producer's domain do not count, the existing hashed-IP limiter and a global daily cap apply, and the form closes openly past a target. I suggest a 14-day first response and closing new-venue proposals at 30 days (Assumed). This is reasoning from sources, not a controlled result. Evidence on motivation is modest: thanks raised two-week retention about 2 points on Wikipedia with no effect on time contributed ([summary](https://signpost.news/2021-10-31/Recent_research)), and an older MovieLens experiment found uniqueness messages helped where benefit messages lowered contribution ([as reported](https://vcp.isr.uci.edu/content/motivating-participation-displaying-value-contribution.html)). So prompt by need ("Nikt nie sprawdzał tej karty od 7 miesięcy"), give a private status link per contribution, and do not build "your report helped N people" first.

**AI can sort now and publish only after a reversal.** Sorting, closing spam and duplicates, and pre-filling a reading for him to accept reverse nothing, since closing is not publishing. A model changing a public page reverses the 06.10 amendment. Cost is no constraint, but the notes disagree on its size, and they answer different jobs: about 20 PLN a month at 10,000 venues covers text triage, while three-page menu extraction on Sonnet 5.5 is about $166, or roughly 645 PLN (Derived, from token counts Assumed and [Anthropic's list prices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Either is small next to the hours. The risk is an invented drink, and nobody publishes a menu-photo error rate: [DoorDash](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-a-guardrail-system-for-llm-based-menu-transcription) routes failures to people and gives no percentages, and that is via a summary because the original returned 403. **Recommendation: extend the blind test under a-model-is-chosen-by-a-blind-test-not-by-asking-it**, which already names menu photographs as a revisit condition. Use 60 real items with answers written first and traps such as a beer beside its 0.0 version, a cropped page and injected instructions. Proposed pass: zero invented lines in at least 300 returned, which gives a 95% upper bound of about 1%, and recall of at least 90%. Passing earns pre-fill only, and output goes through the one-ingestion-path resolver with no tools and no write rights. Unreviewed publishing would need at least 3 months and 500 items in shadow, plus an amended record. Check the provider's EU data terms before any contributor photo reaches a model.

**Local moderators are not needed soon.** A trusted person who only sends evidence reverses nothing. One who publishes reverses the 06.10 amendment, and a volunteer under UNI's instructions likely falls within UNI's responsibility ([GDPR Art. 29](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-29-gdpr/), [DSA Art. 6(2)](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-6/)). The model reaches 5 h/week at about 1,800 venues before this question arises.

**Pushing upstream is low priority.** `diet:alcohol_free` exists on the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:diet) with no dedicated page and I have no count for it (my drink-tag search would not catch it). It carries only a venue-level claim, while the user's question is which drink, tonight. It would help the map more than a person choosing a drink. Nothing suggests Polish mappers care. Keep the rights open rather than doing the work.

## Financial readiness comes from keeping the right things closed and testing payment early

**The model does not depend on closed location data.** Name, address and coordinates have no moat, since GUGiK, Google, Overture and OSM all have them. The dated record of what each venue serves has a medium one, because a copy goes stale, so the moat is the update process. Demand data is high above a traffic level UNI has not reached and zero below it. Those ratings are judgement. The record's two crown jewels (osm-dropped's enriched data, `schema.md`'s Numbers) can be reconciled in one sentence: *the enriched offer data makes the product work; the demand data could make it pay.* So the architecture above is chosen for simplicity and for the review rule, and it would not ruin the business if a trigger ever fired. Its main financial use is not spending hours on a release obligation that buys nothing.

**What must be true, all Assumed on the payer side because no listed-to-paying rate exists for any comparable:**

| Target | Monthly need, PLN | Payers at 98 PLN | Warsaw base case | Poland base case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash costs | 340 | about 4 | about 10 payers, 941 PLN | about 80 payers, 7,840 PLN |
| Cash plus JDG social and health | 2,540 | about 27 | not reached | reached |
| Replace a Warsaw salary | 13,600 | about 143 | not reached | about 58% of it |

Warsaw's 143 payers would be 24% of a 600-venue listing. Poland's base case means 5,000 listed venues, which on the signal-led row is about **25 hours a week of review, or about 14 with AI closing half** (Derived). So the honest outcome is a self-funding public-interest product with a chance of modest income, and the conservative column, under 500 PLN a month even across Poland, is entirely plausible. Community lifts the listed count and freshness, the biggest swing, but **does nothing for paying share**, so its success is not evidence for the business. Infrastructure is nearly irrelevant, at about 340 PLN at 100 venues and under 1,000 at 10,000. That cost table's review row excludes re-reads and uses a different assumption (1 contribution per venue a month at 2 minutes) from the community model. Both are guesses, and the first 100 items should replace them.

**Recommended revenue order, all without paid ranking, inclusion or brand-funded placement.** Test *tools that do a job* alongside analytics: a hosted, current non-alcoholic menu page, a site embed, printed table cards. It rests on one strong comparable, [BeerMenus](https://www.beermenus.com/pricing), which gives Insights away free and charges $29–99 a month for menus and promotion, and on [Untappd for Business](https://nextglass.substack.com/p/craft-brewers-conference-series-45), whose nearly 20,000 customers feed a data product. One case is not proof. No Polish study compares paying for analytics with paying for outcomes, but independents' stated priorities are payroll and scheduling ([vendor survey](https://www.spoton.com/blog/state-of-restaurant-tech-report-reveal/)). This reverses one phrase: no-pay-to-rank's "B2B analytics only" becomes "B2B analytics and tools for venues, plus aggregates under the producer rules", because that sentence no longer rests on evidence and already conflicts with venue-lists-are-never-sold-as-leads. Rank, placement and badge prohibitions do not change. Keep 79, 129 and 199 PLN for the test: they sit below [UpMenu](https://www.upmenu.com/pl/cennik/) at 169–499, and ARPU is the weakest variable. Delayed producer aggregates stay last, under the 03.10 conditions (category and area only, at least 10 venues and 30 events a cell, 90 days' delay, campaigns excluded), and I would add no buyer above about a quarter of revenue (Assumed). The sizing of 1,250–13,000 PLN a month is a guess with unsourced prices. Rule out paid stickers, affiliate links and consumer paywalls on filters. Hold donations until a community exists, and treat grants as a tension: applications reward the population-level and prevention claims UNI may not make.

**Tell contributors the deal before any sale.** Contributors dislike free work being closed and sold (Record, 03.10 run 1), so state plainly what stays free and that no sender's work ranks anyone, before the first paid test. That costs one paragraph, and it is the cheap defence against the extraction charge.

## Findings raised unprompted, ranked by consequence

**1. UNI has no willingness-to-pay data, and the model's most important variable is unbenchmarked.** A grep of the inventory and journals found no record that the BV-1 pricing conversations happened. Everything financial above rests on an assumed 8%. A further catch is that the proposed gate, 30 claimed venues at ≥8% after 60 days, cannot actually separate outcomes: 3 of 30 has a Wilson interval of about 3.5–26% (Derived). Treat it as a go or no-go on "anyone pays at all", and do not quote the rate.

**2. Paid-prominence lines already sit in the code and the roadmap.** `VenuePlan` grants `StrongestCategoryBadge` to Premium only, and `pricing.md` calls it "shareable social proof", against no-pay-to-rank's "no bought badges, ever". I confirmed it in the enum and report service; whether a public view renders it remains unconfirmed. `roadmap/v3.md` V3-F3 lists promoted discovery placement, its fallback lists sponsored placements and affiliate links, and `pricing.md` keeps "Featured placement: TBD". `TrendingProductAlerts` was flagged 03.10 and remains. Remove these or tie the badge to an earned rule: about an hour, and it closes the easiest route to drift.

**3. The geocoder also accepts wrong points, about 16 km off, silently.** A short form of the Arkadia address, house number 82, passes every rule and lands on a same-named *ulica* in Wesoła, because Warsaw has both an aleja and a ulica under that name and UNI reads only the first record, while the 60 km check cannot catch it. The correct form is rejected by the prefix bug. That sends a person to the wrong place, which is worse than a miss. `callForward` also caches a failure for 30 days and any non-2xx is treated as no result, so one outage strands an address for a month (read in code, not tested live). Tests fake HTTP and the live test uses one address, so nothing could see this.

**4. A community signal has nothing to act on today.** No table for signals or venue proposals exists, `product_proposals` requires a `venue_id` and an owner, `venue_offer_logs.action` has no confirmation value, the `menu_evidence` table does not exist and there is no recheck order (Record, `database/schema.md`). The community is a build, and any migration updates the schema map in the same commit.

**5. Contributor terms cannot be retrofitted on an accountless channel.** Nobody can be asked later. Terms must therefore precede the first submission, granting a non-exclusive, irrevocable, sub-licensable right including under CC0, with the sender stating the source and that nothing was copied from another database. That one line stops OSM entering by the back door, and ODbL s.4.4d would also require compatible rights if the route ever reopened. CC BY would be a poor choice because OSM treats it as incompatible without waivers ([Licence Compatibility](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Licence_Compatibility)). The tension is that broad rights contributed to the finding of control in Russmedia, so a lawyer should draft both at once (cost unpriced), and Polish moral-rights waiver needs checking.

**6. A "still here" tap can do nothing under the record as written.** It cannot move a venue up and an unreviewed signal is not evidence. I recommend logging it only as a counter-weight when a "gone" arrives. That blunts competitor spam at no review cost and needs no amendment, as long as it can never defer a re-read.

**7. Two documents contradict the settled community rule.** community-reporting-needs-accounts says "never before" accounts, and roadmap V2-F4 says "authenticated only" and that trusted reports "auto-apply". The auto-apply line is itself a reversal of the 06.10 amendment hiding in a roadmap. The schema already follows the newer record.

**8. The Polish DSA coordinator law takes effect around 5 November 2026** (Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1296, 30 days from 6 October; [Lege Artis](https://czasopismo.legeartis.org/2026/10/koordynator-spraw-uslug-cyfrowych-urzad-komunikacji-elektronicznej-rozporzadzenie-dsa/)), with a second bill still pending and sources differing on its print number. Micro status removes platform duties ([Art. 19](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-19/)), but not hosting ones: contact point, terms describing moderation including any AI, a notice route ([Art. 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-12/), [14](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-14/), [16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-16/)). A reviewed, rewritten fact is probably UNI's own statement, so the hosting shield likely does not cover it: a lawyer's question, with the same practical list either way. Public copy may not describe any of this before it exists (v1-copy-truth).

**9. Delivery platforms are the riskier research source.** The record lists them, and systematic extraction meets the database right ([Polish act, art. 8(2)](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2021/386/ogl/pol/pdf)) or contract terms where no such right exists (Ryanair, [via commentary](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2015/01/breaking-cjeu-says-that-owner-of-online.html)). Manual per-venue checks are at the low end; any crawler should target only venues' own sites. The same act gives UNI some protection against systematic copying of its closed layer, which is the legal use of its canaries.

**10. Seasonal menus make links decay.** Nine of 39 OSM website links were broken (23%, CI 13–38), and one venue's homepage links seasonal PDFs, the one tried returning 404. Keep the source URL and its check date per fact, as `source_url` and `checked_at` already allow, and expect the 3-minute re-read to be optimistic for such venues.

## Conclusion

The question began as a data-source decision, and the evidence relocates it. The licence stopped being the obstacle once it was read, and the geocoder stopped being one once the rule was tested, so two of the three problems he brought shrink to a record amendment and a session's code. What remains is a pair of numbers nobody has: minutes per re-read and the share of venues who pay. Both can be had within weeks of launch for nearly nothing, and every scale decision here waits on them.

The sharper implication is about what community-first means for UNI. Its value is less in the contribution itself than in the routing: a tap that tells one person where to look can make a directory hold together with one reviewer, while an unrouted community would halve what he can maintain. The open-data question is settled the same way. OSM's strength is the map under the pins, and UNI's only defensible contribution is the dated fact that sits on top of it and that no map has.
