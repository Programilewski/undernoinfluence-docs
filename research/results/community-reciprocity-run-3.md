# You are advising a solo founder on **how a small, community-sourced platform should treat the people who build it** — so that contributors get more out of it than they put in, so that its commercial side serves them rather than feeding on them, and so that nobody who pays it gains power over what the public sees. This part of the research answers **concrete design questions**. Answer each with a recommendation, the evidence behind it, and what changes as UNI grows (hundreds of venues and a few contributions a week at launch; thousands of venues and many per day later).

### How to answer — read this before anything else

1. **Every numbered item gets its own section, in the order given.** No item may be dropped, merged into another or summarised away. If space runs short, shorten the prose of every section; never drop one.
2. **Begin with a coverage table**: one row per numbered item, with its status — *found*, *partly found*, *not found* — and the main primary sources. Write it last, place it first.
3. **"Not found" requires evidence of looking**: at least three search queries you actually ran (in English, and in Polish for anything about Poland) and the sources you checked. A bare "not researched" or "insufficient evidence" is not an acceptable answer.
4. **If your research budget runs out before every item is covered, deliver what is finished** — complete sections for the items you covered, and list the uncovered items by number at the end. Do not withhold finished sections because others are incomplete.
5. **Primary sources first**: the platform's own announcement, help page, licence or terms; the paper itself; the regulator's own guidance. Press and secondary write-ups serve to locate primaries or to document community reaction. Trustpilot, app-store reviews, Reddit threads, LinkedIn posts and marketing listicles are evidence only of individual opinion and must be labelled so.
6. **Do not cite what you have not read.** Papers named below are starting points; if you cannot access one, say so and use what you can verify — an abstract, a citing review — labelled as such.
7. **Label every claim**: **[D]** documented, with a link · **[O]** observed in a current product, help page or terms · **[R]** your own reasoning · **[ND]** not found, with the search record from rule 3.
8. **Date everything**: every announcement, licence change, controversy and study. A practice from 2014 may no longer exist.
9. **Distinguish what a platform says from what it does**, and vendor-commissioned case studies from independent evaluation.

### The product

**Under No Influence (UNI)** is a Polish-language directory of venues — bars, cafés, restaurants, pubs, hotels — catalogued by their **non-alcoholic drink offering**: which drinks a venue serves and when that was last checked. It is pre-launch, Warsaw first, then other Polish cities. The interface and the audience are Polish. One founder runs it, with no staff.

**Mission.** UNI exists to **promote the healthy choice**: to help a person find a non-alcoholic drink they can actually order tonight. It is **harm reduction, not abstinence** — two non-alcoholic drinks alongside two alcoholic ones instead of four alcoholic ones counts as success. Venues, brands and producers are instruments of that, never the point. Every feature is tested against: *does this help somebody find a healthier option they would otherwise have missed?* A feature that helps a brand more than a person is not a UNI feature. UNI takes **no public position on alcohol policy**.

**Brand.** UNI is **faceless**: no founder face or voice, no influencers, no lifestyle content. It earns trust through the accuracy of its data. It runs brand accounts on Instagram and TikTok built on the data itself — the map, counts, screen recordings.

**How the data is kept honest today.** Every menu fact carries a source link and the date it was checked. A drink's alcohol content has a status — verified 0.0%, verified under 0.5%, verified exact, or unknown — and "unknown" is never published as a verified alternative. Changes to a venue's offer are logged with the *kind* of knowledge they represent: first-time cataloguing, a change UNI observed, or something the owner reported. Visitor analytics carry **no identifier** — no IP address, session or user.

### The three sides

**People** use UNI free and without accounts. Today they can send a free-text report on any venue ("something here is wrong"), with an optional e-mail for a reply; a person reads it and nothing changes automatically. Planned for the first public version, all without accounts: a form to **propose a venue or a drink**, reviewed by the founder before anything is published; a **"this venue has no non-alcoholic option, and I wish it did"** signal; a one-tap **"still here / gone"** per drink, which only moves the venue up the founder's re-check queue; an optional e-mail telling a proposer when their suggestion went live; and a public "new on the map" page crediting community additions. **Accounts** come later, switched on when measured contribution shows they are needed; contributing without an account will remain possible permanently.

**Venues** are listed free and can never pay for ranking, inclusion or placement. Owners will be able to manage their own listing — their edits currently publish directly and are logged as owner reports. The plan is to **sell venues analytics**: aggregated counts of how visitors find and view their profile, what people search for in their area, and which drink categories are in demand nearby that they do not stock. Comparisons with nearby venues and alerts about trending products are under consideration.

**Producers and distributors** can **never** buy placement, ranking, inclusion or any influence over what the public sees, and UNI will never sell them a list of venues a sales representative could work through. They **may in future buy aggregates** — counts, trends, category demand, area-level coverage. Whether that should happen at all is an open question.

### The tension behind this research

Contributors are not paid. Their proposals, corrections and demand signals become part of what makes UNI valuable — including to venues and, possibly, producers who pay for analytics. That can be a fair exchange (contributors get an accurate free map; venues get a reason to stock better options) or a quiet extraction (unpaid work packaged and sold). The founder's intuition is that the **two-way** structure benefits both sides. **Test that intuition; do not confirm it.** He does **not** want advice on how to monetise an engaged audience; he wants to know what a fair, durable exchange looks like and how to design it in before launch.

### Constraints

- **A solo founder.** No community manager, no paid moderators, no daily social presence. Say what scales down and what does not.
- **Ranking, inclusion and placement are never for sale**, to venues or producers. Payment never buys faster or more favourable treatment of corrections.
- **No public position on alcohol policy.** Advice that requires campaigning for or against regulation does not fit.
- **The brand is faceless.** No founder-led community, no influencers.
- **Privacy is a design constraint, not a checkbox.** Collecting identity documents or building behavioural profiles would need a very strong case.
- **No long contracts or fixed monthly fees** while the product is being validated; EU-resident services strongly preferred.
- Everything user-facing is in Polish.


### Evidence already established — build on it, do not re-research it

These findings come from earlier research with sources; cite them as background where relevant and spend your budget on the questions below.

- **Commercial adjacency.** Untappd sells sponsored badges that raise check-ins, so a sponsored campaign stimulates the very behaviour later reported as popularity. Vivino states advertising does not alter ratings while selling wineries paid presence. HappyCow sells "Partner" exposure next to free listings. The FTC closed its Yelp inquiry in 2015 without action; that proved no violation, not that none occurred.
- **Gamification and recognition.** A preregistered experiment and replication found a game version of a task more enjoyable but less accurate (d = −0.68, −0.40). Wikipedia barnstars raised productivity only among the top 1% of editors. Precise task framing in Zooniverse raised accuracy (49% vs 35%) while lowering volume.
- **Newcomers and feedback.** Wiki newcomers reverted in their first session had 0.49 times the odds of continuing. Thanks raised retention by about 2%.
- **Trust.** Accuracy-derived reputation (WikiTrust: reputation rises when later editors keep a contribution, falls when they undo it) predicts quality; activity does not measure truth.
- **Privacy.** The Article 29 Working Party (2014) holds that removing direct identifiers is not anonymisation; free text can still identify. EDPB guidance treats an inference about health that a controller makes or uses as special-category data. GUS suppresses business aggregates covering fewer than three entities or dominated by one; health publishers commonly suppress cells below five.
- **Accountless contribution** is compatible with abuse control (Wikimedia's temporary accounts). KCPU runs a free anonymous helpline (800 199 990).

## The questions

**The exchange**

1. **What makes the exchange fair?** State the conditions under which selling aggregated, anonymous analytics built partly on community contributions is a fair use of them, and the conditions under which it becomes extraction — separately for **venues** and for **producers**.
2. **Forms of reciprocity.** Assess recognition, public credit, publication notices, a public log of changes, trusted-contributor status, early access, consultation on rules, open-licensing contributions, revenue sharing, and publicly reporting how revenue is spent. Which are fair, which corrupt the data, which are premature for a solo founder?
3. **Public commitments.** What should UNI promise publicly, and how — a plain "how UNI makes money" page, what is never sold, how contributions are licensed, versioned terms with advance notice of changes? **What happens to contributions if UNI is sold or shuts down** — asset locks, open-data escrow, release on shutdown, exit-to-community models — and which are credible from a one-person company? Draft the key commitments in Polish. Note that UNI's public copy may never promise a feature or control that does not exist yet.
4. **Should the verified factual data be openly licensed?** Weigh the reciprocity argument against the risk that an open copy of the venue data becomes a free lead list for sales representatives — which UNI has committed never to sell — and removes the basis of its analytics. Use Open Food Facts, OpenStreetMap and iNaturalist as reference points.

**Commercial customers**

5. **Should producers and distributors be able to buy aggregates at all?** Examine Untappd's brewery insights, Vivino's winery data, Strava Metro, Google's Popular Times and Places Insights, Foursquare's data products, and on-trade drinks market-data sellers (for example CGA by NielsenIQ). How does each handle a buyer **stimulating the signal it then buys**, and which signal types, embargoes or aggregation levels are excluded from sale in practice? Recommend whether UNI should sell to producers, and if so under what rules.
6. **Comparisons with named competitors.** Is it fair, and lawful in the EU (including competition law on exchanging competitively sensitive information), to show a paying venue how named nearby venues perform or what they stock that it does not? How do Google Business Profile, Yelp for Business, Untappd for Business and TheFork handle competitor benchmarking — named or anonymous, and with what minimum group size?

**Keeping the data honest**

7. **Owner statements against community reports.** When a venue owner's edit and a community report disagree, how do Google Business Profile (owner edits versus user-suggested edits), Tripadvisor, Yelp and OpenStreetMap resolve it, and what do they show the public about the source of a fact? Should UNI's owner edits publish directly, labelled as stated by the venue, or wait for review?
8. **Abuse without identifiers.** With no accounts, no IP storage and no persistent visitor identifier, how can UNI resist repeated taps, competitor sabotage and brand astroturfing of demand signals? Evaluate honeypots, timing checks, privacy-preserving challenges (for example proof-of-work CAPTCHAs, Private Access Tokens), and spike detection by venue and time — cost, privacy impact and EU availability for each — and state what UNI must honestly accept it cannot prevent.
9. **Contributor trust in practice.** An older internal decision proposed weighting each person's reports by their activity on the platform (views, searches, time spent), with the threshold kept secret. Given the evidence above, what should replace it — at launch with no accounts, and once accounts exist? Specify a model one reviewer can operate: what is counted, how it decays, what it unlocks and what it never unlocks.

**Knowing whether it works**

10. **Does the two-way model work, and how would UNI know?** Is there independent evidence — not vendor case studies — that aggregated user demand changes what venues stock, from food-allergy, vegan, accessibility or craft-beer communities, for example? Design a test one person could run in Warsaw. Then: which metrics show a contributor community being served rather than drained, which common growth metrics push a platform toward extraction, and what single measure best tracks UNI's mission without building visitor profiles?

### What a good answer looks like

- **The coverage table first**, honest about every gap, with the search record behind each "not found".
- **Items 1–10 each in their own section**, in order, each ending in a concrete recommendation.
- **A staged plan**: what to commit to and build at an accountless launch, what to add when accounts arrive, and what must be true before any venue — and separately any producer — pays for analytics.
- **A direct answer to the tension**: under which conditions selling aggregates to venues, and separately to producers, is fair, and under which it is not.
- Anything the founder has not asked about but should have, flagged as such.

## 1. What makes exchange fair?

### Venues

[D] The European Commission’s 2023 Horizontal Guidelines recognise that aggregated data pools can produce efficiencies, but recommend necessity, proportionality, aggregation, restricted access and transparency; participants should ordinarily see their own data and final aggregated information from others.[^1_29][^1_1]

[R] Selling venue analytics is a fair exchange only if all of these conditions hold:

- [R] **Public benefit:** revenue materially supports the free map, verification and correction process rather than primarily generating more commercial data.
- [R] **Own data first:** a venue may see how people encountered and viewed its own listing, subject to privacy thresholds.
- [R] **Market context, not competitor surveillance:** neighbourhood demand should be shown by category, area and delayed period—not as another named venue’s private views, searches or inferred sales.
- [R] **No commercial effect on public facts:** paying must never change ranking, inclusion, verification standards, correction speed, badges or how uncertainty is displayed.
- [R] **No disguised personal data:** reports must exclude e-mail addresses, free text, exact timestamps, individual search trails and any derived health or abstinence profile.
- [R] **Minimum publication cells:** suppress reports involving fewer than five venues and use a higher threshold—preferably ten—where one venue or category dominates.
- [R] **Honest provenance:** label community reports, owner declarations, UNI verification and inferred demand separately.
- [R] **Reciprocity:** publish at least an annual account of how commercial revenue supported verification, uptime and free public access.
- [R] **Essential participation remains free:** correcting a listing, responding to a report and maintaining accurate public facts cannot require an analytics subscription.

[R] It becomes extraction when UNI hides the commercial reuse, measures contributors more closely than needed, sells low-volume demand, lets paid venues obtain preferential treatment, or optimises contribution volume while review quality and contributor outcomes deteriorate.

### Producers

[O] Foursquare sells products based on place and visitation data, applies extra review to venue-level delivery, excludes sensitive locations and does not sell raw latitude-longitude trails; its audience product has a 5,000-device minimum. [O] NIQ/CGA explicitly markets brand, manufacturer, channel and regional performance to beverage suppliers and supports outlet targeting and location planning.[^1_30][^1_3][^1_14][^1_31]

[R] The producer exchange is fair only under a stricter standard:

- [R] Producers receive **delayed category-level trends**, not named venue opportunities.
- [R] No report reveals that a particular venue lacks a particular producer’s brand.
- [R] No raw or searchable list of venues, contributors, searches or demand signals is supplied.
- [R] Brand campaigns, samples, promotions and paid activations are excluded from “organic demand” statistics or separately disclosed.
- [R] Buyers cannot purchase a report measuring the same signal they recently stimulated.
- [R] UNI publishes the report’s aggregation, suppression and exclusion rules.
- [R] The producer product has a demonstrable route back to the mission—for example, funding verification or helping venues stock categories that users could not otherwise find.
- [R] UNI periodically tests whether producer access produces more verified availability rather than merely more sales outreach.

[R] It becomes extraction if producers can identify leads, infer venue-level gaps, measure campaigns through community behaviour, monopolise access, or influence the categories and products UNI highlights publicly.

### Growth effect

[R] At launch, no analytics should be sold because sparse Warsaw data cannot support useful privacy thresholds and one founder cannot simultaneously verify facts, investigate anomalies and service commercial clients. [R] Venue pilots become defensible only after thousands of venue-page events and enough venues per area to suppress small cells; producer access should come later, if at all.

**Recommendation:** [R] Treat venue analytics as a possible reciprocal service; treat producer analytics as an exceptional, revocable programme requiring published safeguards and evidence of mission benefit.

| Mechanism | Fairness and data effect | Launch | Later |

| Publication notices | [R] High-value, low-risk reciprocity: the contributor learns that effort produced a result. | [R] Send a one-off notice only where an e-mail was voluntarily supplied; keep the e-mail separate from analytics. | [R] In-account inbox plus e-mail preference. |
| Public change log | [R] Strong reciprocity and accountability if it records the fact, source class and date without exposing reporters or allegations. | [R] Publish additions and material verified changes. | [R] Add structured histories, reversions and reason codes. |
| Trusted-contributor status | [R] Fair only when based on later verification and retained accuracy. It must never be derived from views, searches or time spent. | [R] Premature without accounts and sufficient validation history. | [R] Use transparent accuracy bands; unlock workflow convenience, not truth or ranking power. |

| Consultation on rules | [D] Wikimedia’s current terms promise community comment periods for substantial changes, including at least 30 days for major revisions.[^1_5] | [R] Publish proposals and accept comments asynchronously; do not promise votes or continuous consultation. | [R] Add a quarterly rule-change window or small rotating contributor panel. |
| Open licensing | [R] Potentially powerful reciprocity, but full venue-stock data can become the sales lead list UNI refuses to sell. | [R] License submitted text non-exclusively to UNI; do not open the complete venue-product database yet. | [R] Consider delayed or reduced public-interest extracts after section 4’s conditions are met. |
| Revenue sharing | [R] Premature and administratively disproportionate: attribution is ambiguous, anonymous contributors cannot reliably be paid, and monetary incentives could produce low-quality or strategic submissions. | [R] Do not promise it. | [R] Reconsider only if recurring profit is material and an account-based, tax-compliant scheme can reward verified outcomes rather than volume. |
| Revenue-spend reporting | [R] Fair, cheap and aligned with a faceless brand because it makes institutional behaviour—not founder personality—the trust signal. | [R] Publish only after first revenue. | [R] Annual report covering revenue bands, verification expenditure, infrastructure and reserves. |

[O] Open Food Facts frames volunteer data as a public-interest commons and states that it is independent, non-profit, advertising-free and openly licensed; that structural reciprocity is stronger than a badge alone. [D] Wikimedia and OpenStreetMap also bind contribution reuse to explicit licences and documented change mechanisms rather than relying only on symbolic thanks.[^1_5][^1_6][^1_9]

[R] A solo founder can sustain publication notices, optional credit, a change log and an annual commercial-transparency page. [R] Individual rewards, revenue allocation, contributor voting and a standing council do not scale down safely because they create tax, moderation, identity and governance work before the community exists.

**Recommendation:** [R] Launch with optional credit, publication notices, a source-labelled change log and commercial transparency; postpone trusted status until accounts, and postpone revenue sharing indefinitely unless UNI develops stable profits and auditable contributor attribution.

[R] UNI should promise:

- [R] Payment never affects listing, ranking, placement, verification, correction priority or public uncertainty labels.
- [R] UNI does not sell identifiable contribution records, e-mail addresses, free-text reports or venue prospecting lists.
- [R] Commercial reports use only defined aggregates that pass published suppression rules.
- [R] Owner statements remain distinguishable from independently verified facts.
- [R] Contributions are licensed non-exclusively; contributors do not transfer ownership of their original text or photographs.
- [R] Material terms changes receive 30 days’ notice and apply prospectively; urgent legal or security changes receive prompt notice and an explanation.

- [R] If the business changes owner, the buyer does not gain more rights over old contributions than UNI had when those contributions were made.


### Sale and shutdown

[D] Exit to Community, developed publicly from 2020 by MEDLab and Zebras Unite, describes transitions to ownership by users, workers or other stakeholders through mechanisms such as cooperatives or trusts, but it is a framework rather than a standard legal transaction.[^1_32][^1_8]

[R] Four mechanisms have different credibility for a one-person Polish company:


| Mechanism | Credibility now | Assessment |
| :-- | --: | :-- |
| Contractual successor covenant | High | [R] Put contribution-use and no-paid-influence commitments into the sale agreement and public terms. This is the most realistic launch protection, subject to Polish legal review. |
| Shutdown export | Medium | [R] Technically easy, but a fully open venue-product export immediately becomes a potential sales lead list. Do not promise it until the licence and dataset scope are settled. |
| Independent escrow | Medium later | [R] Deposit encrypted database exports and documentation with a mission-aligned foundation or professional escrow provider once UNI has meaningful community value. |
| Asset lock or golden share | Low now | [R] Requires a company structure, enforceable constitutional documents and an independent holder; it is disproportionate pre-launch but worth legal review before outside investment. |
| Exit to community | Low now | [R] State it as a possible preferred route, not a promise. A community that does not yet have accounts, governance or capital cannot credibly receive the company. |
| Polish family foundation | Poor fit | [D] A Polish family foundation requires at least PLN 100,000 and is designed mainly to hold and manage family assets; official guidance says it is not an instrument for ordinary operating activity.[^1_33] |

[R] Before a sale, UNI should provide at least 60 days’ public notice unless legally prohibited, name the buyer, explain changed data uses and allow deletion of optional contact details. [R] A buyer that refuses the standing contribution covenant should receive software, trademarks and founder-created data—but not contributor free text, contact data or demand-signal history.

[R] On shutdown, UNI should delete optional e-mails and unpublished free text under a published schedule. [R] The verified factual layer should be transferred or released only under a previously documented mechanism; promising “open release” now would be misleading because no licence or escrow currently exists.

## 4. Open licensing

[O] Open Food Facts describes its database as a public-interest commons under the Open Database Licence, available for commercial and non-commercial reuse with attribution and share-alike obligations; it also provides manufacturers with a free product-data platform. [O] OpenStreetMap likewise publishes its database under ODbL, permitting commercial reuse while requiring attribution and share-alike treatment of adapted databases.[^1_10][^1_34][^1_35][^1_9]

[O] iNaturalist uses a different model: as of its 17 March 2025 help update, contributors retain ownership and the default licence is CC BY-NC, while users may select other licences or all-rights-reserved separately for observations, photographs and sounds. [D] This choice creates interoperability costs: some biodiversity systems encourage CC0 or CC BY because non-commercial or other restrictions can prevent downstream reuse.[^1_11][^1_36][^1_37]

### UNI’s trade-off

[R] Full ODbL publication would create strong reciprocity: contributors and the Polish public could preserve, audit and improve the venue map even if UNI failed. [R] It would also allow producers, distributors and data brokers to build exactly the venue-product prospecting dataset UNI has promised not to sell.

[R] ODbL share-alike would require publication of adapted databases distributed publicly, but it would not prohibit a sales representative from using UNI’s data internally to choose venues. [R] A “non-commercial open licence” would reduce this risk but would not meet the conventional definition of open data and would create difficult boundaries around journalists, researchers, apps and commercial public-interest services.

[R] The analytics moat is not a sufficient ethical reason to keep community facts closed forever. [R] The stronger reason at launch is that UNI has not yet proved that bulk venue-product disclosure helps users more than it helps commercial prospecting.

### Layered licensing

[R] Use separate layers:

- [R] **Open now:** schema, field definitions, verification-status vocabulary, documentation and non-proprietary product/ABV facts that can be lawfully republished.
- [R] **Publicly viewable but no bulk licence yet:** the live relationship between named venues and stocked drinks.
- [R] **Never open:** contributor e-mails, free text, moderation records, demand-event logs, abuse indicators and unpublished evidence.
- [R] **Research access later:** delayed, minimised extracts under an agreement prohibiting contact-list construction and re-identification; describe these as controlled research data, not open data.
- [R] **Possible future commons:** a delayed venue-category dataset rather than exact venue-brand pairings, after evaluating scraping, market effects and the commercial model.


### Growth effect

[R] At hundreds of venues, a bulk export would be both easy to copy and relatively easy to convert into Warsaw sales leads, while offering little resilience benefit because contribution volume is still small. [R] At national scale, openness becomes more valuable for continuity and accountability, but also more commercially exploitable.

[R] Full open licensing should be considered only if UNI can survive through freshness, verification, software and analytics rather than database exclusivity—and only after telling contributors that commercial reuse, including sales use, cannot be excluded from a genuinely open licence.

**Recommendation:** [R] Do not openly license the complete venue-product graph at launch; open the schema and safe factual layers, then revisit a delayed venue-category commons after UNI can survive copying and contributors have been consulted.

## 6. Named competitors

### Legal boundary

[D] The EU Horizontal Guidelines published on 21 July 2023 cover indirect exchanges through platforms, online tools and market-research organisations as well as direct exchanges between competitors. [D] They treat attribution-resistant aggregated information as safer and state that individualised data are generally unnecessary for benchmarking when an aggregate ranking can create the claimed efficiency.[^1_1][^1_29]

[D] Current or future prices, quantities, sales, capacities, customer lists and commercial strategies are particularly sensitive; future individualised price or quantity intentions can constitute a restriction by object. [R] UNI therefore cannot assume legality merely because information passes through a dashboard rather than directly between venues.[^1_49][^1_1]

[R] Showing a named venue’s publicly visible, dated drinks list is relatively low risk because every visitor can see the same fact for free. [R] Showing one paying venue another named venue’s private views, searches, demand, conversion proxy or trend is much riskier because UNI would be creating a private information-exchange channel.

### Current practices

| Service | Verified public practice |
| :-- | :-- |
| Google Business Profile | [O] Verified owners receive views, clicks and customer interactions for their own profile and may compare multiple profiles they themselves manage.[^1_15] [R] No current official evidence reviewed showed named competitor private-performance analytics. |
| Yelp for Business | [O] Yelp’s own business materials describe page visits, leads, impressions, clicks and advertising performance for the subscribing business.[^1_50][^1_51] [R] Named competitor ratings and review counts remain publicly observable, but a verified official minimum peer group was not found. |
| Untappd | [O] Location Reports can rank named breweries, brands and retail venues by check-ins in a location.[^1_12] [O] Local analytics also market popular beers and trends to venues.[^1_52] |
| TheFork | [O] The 2023 Revenue Management announcement describes analysis of a restaurant’s promotions, occupancy and margins.[^1_17] [O] Current Manager materials advertise performance analytics and feedback analysis.[^1_53] [R] A verified public rule for named competitor dashboards or minimum peer groups was not found. |

[ND] Exact group-size thresholds for competitor benchmarking were not found. [D] Searches actually run were: “Google Business Profile performance competitor benchmarking named official,” “Yelp business dashboard competitor benchmark page views official analytics,” “Untappd for Business competitor benchmarking official analytics,” “TheFork manager competitor benchmark anonymous official analytics,” “TheFork restaurant benchmark competitors performance official,” and “site:theforkmanager.com benchmark competitors analytics restaurant.” [D] Sources checked included Google Business Profile Help, Yelp Business resources, Untappd Location Reports and analytics pages, and TheFork Manager product and 2023 Revenue Management materials.[^1_15][^1_50][^1_17][^1_12]

### UNI design

[R] A paying venue may receive:

- [R] Its own page views, outbound menu clicks and correction history.
- [R] Its percentile or band among an anonymous cohort.
- [R] Area-level category demand with suppressed counts.
- [R] Public links showing which dated products named venues publicly list, provided exactly the same view is free to everyone.

[R] It should not receive:

- [R] Named competitor views, search appearances, clicks, demand signals or trends.
- [R] Named competitor estimated sales, popularity or conversion.
- [R] Future stocking, pricing, promotion or supplier intentions.
- [R] A “venues most worth targeting” or “gaps versus competitor X” report.
- [R] Cohorts designed so narrowly that members can infer each other.

[R] Use at least five venues per anonymous benchmark at initial scale and preferably ten later; add dominance suppression, a minimum 30-day delay and broader categories where inference remains possible. [R] Before launch of paid benchmarking, obtain Polish/EU competition-law review of the actual fields, cohort construction and dashboard screenshots.

**Recommendation:** [R] Permit named comparisons only for facts already public to everyone; keep all UNI-derived performance anonymous, historical and aggregated.

## 7. Owner versus community

### Platform patterns

[O] Yelp currently receives business updates from owners, users, data partners and platform partners. [O] Its factual attributes may be supplied by businesses or collected through user votes, while subjective attributes are reserved to users and some self-identification attributes can only be set by the business.[^1_18][^1_19][^1_54]

[O] Tripadvisor’s 2018 Management Center guide says verified owners can submit business details, amenities and responses to reviews. [O] Its 2016 category-change guidance says certain material changes require investigation by the Listings team and could take at least 15 business days. [R] These dated materials show differentiated handling, but should not be treated as proof of every current 2026 workflow.[^1_20][^1_21]

[ND] A sufficiently precise current primary-source description of Google’s ordering between owner edits and user-suggested edits, OpenStreetMap’s complete local dispute escalation, and the exact public source labels used by all four platforms was not found in the material reviewed. [D] Searches actually run included “Tripadvisor business listing owner edits user suggested edits official help,” “Tripadvisor owner update business details review official,” “Tripadvisor suggest edits listing owner dispute information official,” “Yelp business owner edit information user suggest edit official help,” “Yelp suggested edits business information source owner official,” and “Yelp business attributes owner community edits official.” [D] Official Tripadvisor and Yelp materials cited above were checked; they did not provide a complete four-platform answer.[^1_21][^1_19][^1_20][^1_18]

### UNI conflict policy

[R] Direct publication should depend on the direction and harm of the change:


| Owner change | Immediate public action | Verification |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| “This drink is gone” | [R] Mark it **„lokal zgłasza brak; w trakcie sprawdzania”** and remove it from confidently verified availability results. | [R] Recheck promptly; false positives can send users to a venue where nothing is available. |
| “We now serve this drink” | [R] Show only as **„zgłoszone przez lokal”**, not as independently verified. | [R] Require a current menu link, dated photo or UNI check before promotion to verified. |
| Opening hours/contact | [R] Publish provisionally if low-risk and supported by the official site. | [R] Queue inconsistent changes. |
| Alcohol-content claim | [R] Never promote to verified solely from an owner statement. | [R] Require manufacturer, label or other authoritative evidence. |
| Request to erase community history | [R] Do not erase accurate provenance or change history merely because the owner objects. | [R] Remove personal data, defamatory text or legally unlawful material where required. |

[R] When a community report contradicts an owner:

1. [R] Preserve both records internally with source class and date.
2. [R] Do not decide by vote count.
3. [R] Downgrade the public confidence where a wrong answer could cause a wasted visit.
4. [R] Seek a current menu, official product evidence or independent check.
5. [R] Publish the resulting fact with source and date, not the dispute narrative.
6. [R] Notify both sides where contact details exist.
7. [R] Keep a private moderation reason and a public factual change log.

[R] At launch, founder review of every owner addition is feasible and safer than direct publication. [R] At scale, low-risk owner removals can publish provisionally, but additions and alcohol verification should remain evidence-gated.

**Recommendation:** [R] Replace direct owner publication with immediate but explicitly provisional owner declarations; only independent evidence should create the strongest verified status.

## 8. Abuse without identifiers

[R] Without accounts, stored IP addresses or persistent browser identifiers, UNI cannot reliably distinguish one person tapping repeatedly from many people behind the same network. [R] Abuse controls must therefore make automation expensive and prevent signals from changing public facts—not pretend to establish one-person-one-vote.

### Controls

| Control | Cost | Privacy | Use at UNI |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Honeypot fields | [R] Negligible. | [R] No additional personal data. | [R] Use at launch against simple form bots. |
| Timing and interaction checks | [R] Negligible, but aggressive limits can penalise password managers, accessibility tools and fast users. | [R] Can operate without persistence. | [R] Reject impossibly fast submissions; do not treat normal timing as identity or trust. |
| Signed, single-use nonce | [R] Low engineering cost. | [R] No persistent visitor profile is required. | [R] Bind a token to venue, action and short expiry; prevent replay of the same page token. Reloading can still obtain another token. |
| Self-hosted proof of work | [O] ALTCHA’s open-source core is free, MIT-licensed, self-hosted and uses no cookies or external service; its EU-hosted cloud service starts at €47/month as observed on 3 October 2026.[^1_22][^1_55][^1_56] | [R] Strongest match for UNI if self-hosted; processing and battery cost shift to the visitor’s device. | [R] Use adaptively on contribution forms or after aggregate spikes, not on ordinary map browsing. |
| Friendly Captcha | [O] German provider using proof-of-work; an EU-only endpoint exists, but official documentation says it requires an Advanced or Enterprise plan.[^1_23][^1_57] | [R] Better EU residency than many US alternatives, but still a processor relationship. | [R] Consider only when managed abuse protection is worth recurring cost. |
| Cloudflare Turnstile | [O] Processes IP address, TLS fingerprint, user agent and site origin; Cloudflare acts as processor for site protection and controller when improving Turnstile.[^1_24] | [R] Better than behavioural advertising CAPTCHA, but incompatible with the claim that no third party processes IP data. | [R] Use only after a documented legitimate-interest assessment and clear privacy notice. |
| Private Access Tokens | [D] Apple’s 7 June 2022 design uses unlinkable blind-signature tokens so a server can verify a legitimate device without learning identity or recognising it over time.[^1_25] | [R] Excellent privacy properties, but platform and issuer support are not universal. | [R] Accept where available as a low-friction positive signal, never as proof of a unique person. |
| Aggregate spike detection | [R] Low cost if implemented as venue/time counters. | [R] No visitor identifier needed. | [R] Detect sudden venue/category bursts, repeated identical text and implausible ratios; quarantine rather than delete automatically. |

### Launch design

[R] Each tap should produce an **event**, not a vote:

- [R] Issue a short-lived signed token for a specific venue and action.
- [R] Allow one redemption per token.
- [R] Keep only venue, action, coarse time bucket, challenge result and moderation state.
- [R] Use a honeypot, CSRF protection, input limits and server-side sanitisation.
- [R] Move unusually dense events into a quarantined aggregate excluded from reports.
- [R] Never display exact demand counts publicly.
- [R] Never let “still here,” “gone” or “wish it did” automatically change a fact.
- [R] Require evidence and human review for proposals and corrections.
- [R] Introduce self-hosted ALTCHA first on free-text submissions, then selectively on suspicious tap bursts.


### What UNI cannot prevent

[R] UNI must state internally—and, where material, publicly—that it cannot prevent all coordinated humans, distributed bots, device rotation, venue staff campaigns, brand astroturfing or repeated taps after page reloads. [R] It can only limit automation, detect anomalies and ensure that weak signals have weak consequences.

**Recommendation:** [R] Combine single-use tokens, honeypots, coarse timing, aggregate anomaly detection and selective self-hosted proof of work; accept that anonymous demand data measures events, not unique people.

## 9. Contributor trust

[R] The proposed secret weighting by views, searches and time spent should be rejected. [R] Those behaviours measure product use and exposure, not whether a factual claim survives independent checking, and secret thresholds prevent contributors from understanding or contesting treatment.

### Accountless launch

[R] Score **claims**, not people:


| Evidence class | Treatment |
| :-- | :-- |
| Current official menu or manufacturer link | [R] Highest review priority; still check that the source supports the exact field. |
| Dated menu or label photograph | [R] Strong evidence after privacy and metadata checks. |
| Detailed report without source | [R] Useful lead, not publication proof. |
| “Still here” / “gone” tap | [R] Queue signal only. |
| Demand tap | [R] Aggregate interest event only; never evidence that a product exists. |
| Repeated or coordinated pattern | [R] Quarantine for review. |

[R] Store the review outcome as **accepted**, **partly accepted**, **rejected with reason**, **duplicate**, **unverifiable** or **superseded**. [R] Send that result when an optional e-mail is present, but do not use e-mail as a hidden identity key.

### Accounts phase

[R] Introduce an accuracy-derived score with a public formula that one reviewer can operate:

- [R] +2 when a factual contribution is independently verified and retained.
- [R] +1 when independently corroborated but not yet fully verified.
- [R] 0 for duplicates, still-pending claims and honest unverifiable reports.
- [R] −2 when the claim is contradicted by stronger contemporaneous evidence.
- [R] −4 for demonstrated fabrication, undisclosed commercial coordination or repeated manipulation.
- [R] Count only the latest 20 resolved factual contributions or apply a 180-day half-life, so old success does not create permanent authority.
- [R] Keep separate scores for venue availability and product/ABV facts; expertise in one does not prove accuracy in the other.
- [R] Publish score bands and reasons, not a hidden exact manipulation threshold.
- [R] Allow a simple appeal reviewed from the underlying evidence.

[R] Trust may unlock batch proposals, fewer explanatory fields, access to a personal contribution history and priority in the human review queue. [R] It must never unlock paid ranking, automatic alcohol verification, power to suppress another person’s report, heavier demand votes, access to contributor identities or commercial data.

[R] At national scale, require periodic revalidation: a trusted status that has no resolved factual contribution for 12 months becomes inactive rather than permanently privileged.

**Recommendation:** [R] At launch, rank submissions by evidence quality; with accounts, use transparent, decaying, domain-specific accuracy based on later verification—never activity, engagement or secret thresholds.

## 10. Does the model work?

### Evidence strength

[D] A 2021 independent scoping review covering 171 records found that perceived customer demand appeared as a motivation in 60% of the reviewed sources concerning independently owned restaurants; reported outcomes often included customer acceptance and increased revenue. [R] That supports the plausibility that demand matters, but it does not establish that a platform aggregate caused a stocking change.[^1_26]

[D] A 2023 cross-sectional study of 51 restaurateurs and 138 customers in Tarragona found large increases in takeaway and digital-menu offerings during the pandemic, reported as responses to changed demand; the pandemic is a major confounder and the intervention was not a community analytics platform.[^1_27]

[D] A survey of 92 independent restaurants in San Juan found that 76% of respondents perceived a trend toward patrons asking for more vegetarian menu items. [D] A 2025 Toronto study found allergen statements on only 16% of 1,000 sampled independent restaurant menus and allergen symbols on 10%, illustrating that unmet informational needs do not automatically produce supply or disclosure changes.[^1_58][^1_28]

[ND] No independent causal study was found in the reviewed material showing that aggregated signals from a community directory caused venues to add products. [D] Searches actually run included “independent study consumer demand restaurant menu change vegan options,” “academic study online reviews demand restaurants menu offerings vegan allergy,” “crowdsourced demand changes restaurant stocking independent study,” “independent evaluation crowdsourced demand platform changed business offerings restaurants HappyCow vegan,” “study restaurant adds vegan options customer demand survey operators independent,” and “craft beer Untappd data brewery product decisions independent academic study.” [D] Sources checked included the independent restaurant scoping review, Tarragona study, San Juan survey, Toronto allergen study, HappyCow-related results and academic work using Untappd data.[^1_59][^1_28][^1_58][^1_27][^1_26]

[R] The founder’s two-way intuition is therefore **plausible but unproven**. [R] UNI should treat “demand data changes supply” as a product hypothesis, not as justification for commercial data sales.

### Warsaw test

[R] Run a small randomised delayed-intervention trial that one founder can operate:

1. [R] Recruit 20–24 Warsaw venues with no verified meaningful non-alcoholic option or a clear category gap.
2. [R] Stratify by venue type and district, then randomly assign half to an early-report group and half to a delayed-report group.
3. [R] Record an eight-week baseline of eligible anonymous demand events, public menu facts and verification freshness.
4. [R] Send the early group one standardised Polish report: broad local category demand, methodology, uncertainty, suppression rules and practical examples; include no named competitors and no producer promotion.
5. [R] Give venues no payment, ranking benefit or public badge for participation.
6. [R] Recheck public menus and availability after eight weeks; then send the same report to the delayed group.
7. [R] Recheck both groups after another eight weeks.
8. [R] Define the primary outcome in advance: a newly verified non-alcoholic category that remains available for at least 30 days.
9. [R] Secondary outcomes may include a published menu update, improved alcohol-content evidence, response rate and time from report to stocking.
10. [R] Record whether the venue independently confirms that the UNI report influenced the decision, but do not substitute self-report for verified availability.

[R] With 20–24 venues, statistical power will be limited; the study should be described as a feasibility experiment. [R] The decision rule should be practical: proceed only if the early group shows materially more sustained verified additions, venues report the aggregate as understandable, and no report reveals or enables individual targeting.

### Community health metrics

[R] Metrics showing contributors are served include:

- [R] Median time from submission to a reasoned outcome.
- [R] Percentage of contributors who receive a publication or rejection notice when requested.
- [R] Acceptance, partial-acceptance and unverifiable rates by contribution type.
- [R] Percentage of community-added facts still correct after 90 and 180 days.
- [R] Correction half-life for wrong or stale information.
- [R] Number of successful-find responses attributable to community-added or corrected facts.
- [R] Percentage of commercial revenue spent on verification, data quality and free public access.
- [R] Contributor survey response to: **„Czy efekt Twojego zgłoszenia był wart poświęconego czasu?”**
- [R] Later, account-based repeat contribution after receiving useful feedback—not after rewards or notification pressure.

[R] Metrics that push toward extraction include raw contributions, taps per person, streak length, time spent, daily active users, notification opens, pages per session, number of demand signals sold, revenue per contributor and conversion from contribution to commercial profiling.

### Mission measure

[R] The best single mission measure is:

> **Odsetek anonimowych odpowiedzi po wizycie: „UNI pomogło mi znaleźć napój bezalkoholowy, który udało mi się zamówić”.**

[R] Generate a short-lived, one-use feedback token only after a person opens directions, a current menu or venue contact from a result containing recently verified availability. [R] Store the answer, coarse venue/time bucket and verification age; delete the token after use or expiry and never connect it to other visits.

[R] Report the response rate beside the outcome rate because responders are self-selected and anonymous events are not unique people. [R] Do not call the result “number of people helped”; call it **„potwierdzone anonimowe wyniki wizyt”**.

**Recommendation:** [R] Validate the two-way model with a randomised delayed-report pilot before monetising demand data, and optimise for verified successful finds rather than engagement or submission volume.

## Missing questions

- [R] **Evidence retention:** UNI needs a retention schedule for source screenshots, photographs, e-mails, free text and moderation records, not only public facts.
