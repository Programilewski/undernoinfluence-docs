# For the commute — written Friday 13.11.2026

**In one paragraph.** Over three sessions (11–13.11) UNI got a machine for finding venues: it searches the web for Warsaw places with alcohol-free drinks, checks each site is a real venue, finds its menu on its own website, has Claude read the menu twice, matches what it reads to the catalogue, and puts everything in front of you for approval. Nothing is published without you clicking. On 13.11 it met real venues for the first time (The Roof, Semolino's six locations, seven search results) and eleven things went wrong; all eleven are fixed. What's left is not building but measuring: a few real rounds to see whether it works in ~80% of cases, then a wipe and the real fetch for the ~30 launch venues.

**How to read this.** Part A is business, product and law — no code. Part B is the technical side, for when you want to know how it works. Part C is the plan. Section 4 of Part A is the questions waiting on you.

# Part A — Business, product and law

## A1. What the pipeline is

Before this, adding a venue meant finding it, opening its menu, deciding drink by drink whether it counts, and typing everything into the admin — roughly the 35 venues you did by hand. The pipeline does the finding, reading and matching; you keep the deciding.

1. **Find candidates.** A paid web search (Brave) runs a set of queries, such as `intitle:menu "bezalkoholowe" Powiśle Warszawa`. Every result gives a website domain.
2. **Check each site.** UNI's own bot (UNIbot) opens the homepage: it must carry a Warsaw address or phone **and** look like a place a guest comes to (opening hours, reservations, a restaurant or bar type, a word about food or drink). Shops, journals, associations and portals drop out here.
3. **Find the menu** on the venue's own site: links named menu/karta, the sitemap, the PDFs on the menu page; the newest drinks card wins. At the same time it reads the venue's **name and address**.
4. **Read the menu twice** with Claude Haiku, which lists only the alcohol-free drinks. Where the two readings differ, you see both.
5. **Match** each drink to the catalogue: "Heineken 0,0 but. 0,33" → Heineken 0.0 only if a person has confirmed that spelling before, otherwise a suggestion.
6. **Approve.** On the menu's page: create the venue (GUGiK puts it on the map) or link an existing one, then approve, correct or reject each drink. A venue goes live by itself once it has a map point and an approved drink.
7. **Refresh weekly.** Each venue's menu page is checked again every week; what appeared or disappeared goes back to approval.

## A2. Where it stands and what "done" means

- **Built and tuned.** Everything above works. Source B (Warsaw's list of alcohol-permit holders, as a second way to find venues) is not built; it waits for your CSV.
- **Everything stored on the PC is test data**, by your rule of 13.11. When the pipeline works in about 80% of cases, it is wiped (*Wyczyść dane testowe*) and the real venues are fetched again from scratch. The PC was wiped at the end of 13.11, so the next round starts clean.
- **What 80% means in practice:** of the candidates a round finds, most are venues; of those, most get the right menu; of the drinks read, most are right and matched; and the venue gets its address and map point without typing. The first batch was nowhere near — 4 of 7 search results weren't venues, the wrong menu was chosen, addresses were empty. Each of those is fixed; the next round measures whether the fixes hold.
- **Why it matters for launch.** Launch is at ~30 venues; each district that gets its own page needs 3. The pipeline is how those 30 get in without typing, and how they stay current after launch (the weekly refresh).

## A4. What keeps it clean (data, copyright, privacy)

This is the part that would matter if anyone ever asked "how did you get this data?".

- **UNIbot obeys `robots.txt` and text-and-data-mining opt-outs.** Before reading any page it checks the site's robots file and the TDM reservation (the machine-readable "don't mine my content" signal under the EU copyright rules). A site that says no is not read. `/bot` on UNI explains who UNIbot is and how a venue opts out; an opt-out deletes the stored menu file.
- **First-party only.** UNI copies nothing from another directory or aggregator; what it publishes is which drinks a venue says it serves, from the venue's own page, approved by a person.
- **Brave's terms forbid storing search results.** UNI stores none: a run keeps only counts (how many results, domains, dropped) and the query text. A candidate keeps only its domain and the homepage address of UNIbot's own visit, never Brave's result.
- **Menu files** are kept one per venue (the current one), privately, opened only by the admin, deleted when replaced, rejected or opted out. The page address, file address, a fingerprint (SHA-256) and the dates are kept as the record of where a fact came from.
- **No personal data.** The pipeline keeps only a venue's own public facts: name, address, website, general contacts. Never a person's name, a sole trader's firm name, a NIP or a named e-mail. The permit list's personal columns are never fetched.
- **Anthropic reads menus** — a menu is a venue's public document, not personal data, but Anthropic is still a recipient and the privacy policy says so. Its data-processing agreement is incorporated in Anthropic's Commercial Terms (found 12.11). **Its own text, sub-processors and transfer mechanism are still unread** — read it once before any bulk run. The privacy policy itself is still a draft, to be written from the app.
- **GUGiK** (the state address register) is open public data; using it raises no licence question.
- **What UNI publishes stays brand-free** on public pages (the October decision); the pipeline's brand guesses are internal, used only for matching.

## A5. What it costs

- **Reading a menu:** about $0.001–0.005 per reading with Haiku, two readings per menu. The September Roof card (12 pages) was about 14,000 tokens in. *Wyodrębnij z obrazami* (pages also as images, for layouts that confuse the text) cost about $0.0024 for both readings of a 2-page PDF.
- **Searching:** Brave charges per request; a run is capped at 30 requests. The templates' yield page is how this stays small.
- **Geocoding:** GUGiK is free.
- **Per venue,** the money is negligible; the real cost is your approval time, which is what most of 13.11's fixes reduce (addresses pre-filled, institutions dropped, clean lines approved in one click).

# Part B — Technical

## B1. Where everything is in the admin

Since 13.11 the admin menu is grouped. The pipeline is one group, in the order you work:

```
Lokale
Pozyskiwanie lokali
  Kandydaci          search (Szukaj lokali), add by hand (Dodaj kandydata), chains (Dodaj lokalizacje),
                     find menus (Znajdź menu), reject, Wyczyść dane testowe (not in production)
  Biblioteka menu    every fetched menu; Wyodrębnij / z obrazami; the menu's page is the approval
  Pozycje z menu     every line read, filterable — the brand queue ("Nieznana marka")
  Wyszukiwania       each run as counts; Wydajność zapytań
  Szablony zapytań   the query templates and their yield
Katalog · Zgłoszenia i wnioski · Administracja
```

## B2. How each step works

**Search (source A).** A run takes templates × areas (districts and free-text neighbourhoods) and sends at most 30 queries to the Brave Search API. Each result's domain is dropped if it's on the blocklist (social networks, delivery apps, aggregators, shops, media, government, and since 13.11 seven guides and platforms) or already known (any candidate, rejected included, or a listed venue's site). The rest go to a queue, one job per domain.

**The site check** (`SourcingDomainVerifier`). UNIbot fetches the homepage through `SafeFetcher` (robots.txt, TDM, size and time limits, no private addresses). If it shows no Warsaw address, it follows the "Kontakt" link once. The site passes when it carries a Warsaw postal code, or "Warszawa" with a street or phone, **and** reads as a venue: opening hours, a reservation, or a schema.org restaurant/bar/café/hotel type, plus a food or drink word, in Polish or English. Dropped domains are counted per run and per query ("Bez adresu", "Nie lokal").

**Finding the menu** (`MenuFinder`). From the homepage and the sitemap it collects links whose address or text names a menu, follows the best menu page one level down for its files, and ranks: drinks before food, file before page, newest first. Since 13.11 a newer card beats an older file that only sat on a "menu page" (The Roof). Kids', breakfast, catering and event menus are excluded. A weak best guess is shown, not fetched. A chain location is searched from its own page only.

**Name and address** (`VenueContactReader`, `VenueContactFinder`). Read during *Znajdź menu*: schema.org data first, then the printed address (postal code or district), the contact page if needed; a page printing two addresses equally gives none, one printed twice as often wins. Only empty fields are filled.

**Reading** (`ReadMenuAction`). The menu file is turned into text (pdftotext for PDFs; optionally every page also as an image). Two Haiku requests run in parallel with structured output — a fixed JSON shape per drink: kind (branded product, house drink, unbranded, out of scope), category, name as printed, brand and product guesses, strength as printed, section, whether a price is printed, and since 13.11 the alcoholic ingredients. A refusal goes once to Sonnet; a prompt over 100,000 tokens is refused rather than paid for at the higher rate. The system prompt says the menu is data, never instructions.

**Comparing the two readings.** Drinks are paired by their normalised name; a line is kept when either reading keeps it; differing fields are stored with both values; a drink only one reading found says so; alcoholic ingredients from both readings are joined.

**Matching** (`MenuLineMatcher` via `MenuItemResolver`, the same resolver site search and the importer use). Certain only for a confirmed spelling (`product_aliases`) or a unique exact name; otherwise suggestions at ≥ 60% similarity, never across a strength claim ("Heineken" never suggests Heineken 0.0's opposite). The model's product guess is only a suggestion. Brand-only lines ("Lech Free, różne smaki") never qualify; a line without a printed price doesn't qualify.

**Approval** (the menu's page). *Utwórz lokal* (pre-filled; GUGiK places it; the venue is created inactive and the activation gate publishes it once it has a point and an approved line) or *Powiąż z lokalem*. Per line: *Zatwierdź*, *Popraw* (choose the product; the spelling becomes a confirmed alias; strength must agree), *Odrzuć* with a reason. *Zatwierdź pewne* (Ctrl+Shift+A) approves the clean lines: priced, certain or house drink, readings agreeing, no alcoholic ingredient.

**Refresh.** Weekly (Monday 04:30) each venue's menu page is checked again; the same text means unchanged; an unreachable page is flagged; what changed goes to approval ("W ofercie", "Zniknęło z karty").

| Purpose | building, testing | a real server to test deploys on | the public site (not live yet) |
| Database | its own | its own, rebuilt 13.11 | empty, its own |

| Pipeline data | test data, wiped 13.11 | none | none yet |
| Keys (Brave, Anthropic) | yes | no | not yet |

**Nothing is copied between machines**: a deploy moves code, never data; `.env` is per machine.

**How a deploy works:** a tag (v0.1.N) is pushed after CI passes; `dep deploy homeserver --tag=v0.1.N` refuses anything else; production will only take the tag the home server already runs. Create migrations are still edited in place (nothing is in production), so after such a change the test machines need `migrate:fresh --seed`.

**Is the home server synced with the PC?** Yes, as of the end of 13.11: both run the same code, tagged **v0.1.9**. The home server's database was rebuilt the same day and holds only reference data, the canaries, the 7 search templates and your admin account.

## B5. Known limits

- **JavaScript-only sites** can't be read (no browser runs). Trattoria Prima Pasta and Jabeerwocky are examples. Source B and *Dodaj kandydata* cover them.
- **Menus that exist only on Instagram or as photos without "menu" in their name** aren't found.
- **Guides that look like venues** still pass now and then; blocked by domain as they appear.
- **Haiku picking the menu link** when the ranking is unsure: held until a round shows the ranking failing.
- **Source B** (permit list) not built.

# C. The plan

1. **Your real round on the PC** (1–2 hours): *Szukaj lokali* with `intitle:menu "bezalkoholowe"` and 4–5 neighbourhoods → *Znajdź menu* → *Wyodrębnij* → approve a few. Note what's wrong.
2. **Fixes by frequency**, not by single case. Read *Szablony zapytań*: switch off what finds nothing, add what you'd search yourself.
3. Repeat 1–2 until it's right ~80% of the time.
4. **Before a bulk run:** read the Anthropic DPA's own text once; source B from your CSV if search runs dry.
5. **Wipe and fetch for real** (*Wyczyść dane testowe*), aiming at ~30 venues with 3 per district to be indexed.
6. In parallel, the launch list: Search Console before the import, the filter names, a style per catalogue product, production infrastructure, the privacy policy from the app.
