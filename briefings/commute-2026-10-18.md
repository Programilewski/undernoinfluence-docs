# For the commute — written Sunday 18.10.2026

**The bill is not a reason to turn UNI into a hobby.** The revenue model in `business/model.md` is analytics subscriptions for venues, with no ads, no paid placement and no brand money. The amendment touches none of that. What it threatens is narrower: public pages and posts that name **non-alcoholic drinks sold under an alcohol brand** (Żywiec 0.0, Lech Free, Heineken 0.0, the 0% gins). Those are the biggest part of the non-alcoholic beer search demand the brand pages were built for.

**So the pivot is a change in how those products are shown, not a new business.** The recommended plan is in §3: build a "brand-safe" switch now and leave it off, get one lawyer's opinion before the law takes effect, and shift the way in through search from brand names to categories and occasions. §4 lists the ways to reach people that don't depend on naming a brand at all.

**One date matters more than the bill: Suchy Styczeń 2027.** `/suchy-styczen` only earns that traffic if it is indexed by mid-December, so the launch has to be live by then. That is the same week the law is expected to take effect.

**Your 90% is reasonable.** The real uncertainty is the final text, not whether it passes.

### The one clause that matters for UNI

The penalty provision makes it a crime, with a fine of 30,000–1,000,000 zł or restriction of liberty, to advertise or promote *products whose name, trademark, graphics or packaging resembles or matches an alcoholic drink's, or any other symbol that clearly refers to an alcoholic drink*. That wording targets brand sharing. Until now, a 0.0 beer sat in the shadow of legal beer advertising. Once beer advertising is banned too, the 0.0 versions are where enforcement has something to bite on, which is why the brewing industry's protest is about them.

### How the state reads "advertising"

The prosecutors' guide published by KCPU (October 2025) reads the definition as widely as it can:
- **Intent doesn't count.** It cites the Supreme Administrative Court (NSA, II GSK 982/17): what matters is whether publishing helps a brand's popularity. Simply publishing information about a drink is enough.
- **It argues against the one court that disagreed.** A Kraków administrative court (WSA, III SA/Kr 19/23) held that a web page with a photo, description and price is information, not advertising. The guide calls that reasoning contradictory.
- **It names "the person responsible for the website" as a possible co-perpetrator,** and treats a food blogger naming a tequila brand in a recipe as a possible offence.

**The courts are split, and nobody comparable to UNI has been tested.** "We're a neutral directory, not advertising" is a reasonable position, but it is not a safe assumption. That is what the lawyer question in §5 Q2 is for.

### What doesn't change

- **Non-alcoholic drinks are not alcoholic drinks.** At 0,5% and below they aren't covered by the ban itself. Selling and listing them stays legal, and so does every category the catalogue carries.
- **Promotion inside venues where drinks are consumed on the spot is still allowed,** so a venue's own menu, table card or blackboard is fine. UNI's website is not inside the venue.
- **Information exchanged between businesses in alcohol production and trade is not advertising.** That matters for §3 D.
- **Independent non-alcoholic brands, kombucha, house drinks and soft drinks** carry no alcohol trademark.

## 2. Findings raised unprompted

### 2.1 The money isn't hit; the way in through search is

The worry was that the "detailed menus" selling point falls apart. The menus are only at risk for the brand-sharing products, and the revenue was never meant to come from brands. The real cost is search. The brand-and-product-pages decision (30.08) found growing search demand for non-alcoholic beer and flat zero for mocktails, and the Polish non-alcoholic beer market is mostly brand-sharing 0.0s. **"Żywiec 0,0 Warszawa" as a way in is what's lost. "piwo bezalkoholowe Warszawa" is not.**

### 2.2 The planned social formats are split down the middle

The launch plan describes carousels such as "10 miejsc z piwem 0% w Warszawie". That one names a category and is fine. A post built around a named 0.0 bottle, or a photo of its label, is exactly what the guide's food-blogger example describes. This also matches the TikTok risk ADR-007 already noted: labels get treated as alcohol by moderation. **Map, counts and screen recordings were already the recommended format; the bill makes that a rule, not a preference.**

### 2.3 "Website operator" is named as a possible co-perpetrator

UNI takes no money from brands, which is the strongest part of a "this is information" argument. But the guide's reading doesn't need payment or intent. If the brand-safe switch is ever needed, it has to cover **everything public**: page titles, meta descriptions, structured data, sitemaps, share images, search results, and social posts. A brand name that leaks through a `<title>` is still published.

### 2.4 Venues lose their own channels too, which makes UNI more useful to them

A venue can't advertise a brand-sharing 0.0 online either, and the bill also bans discount and bundle promotion of alcohol. What a venue *can* still say publicly is "we have four non-alcoholic beers and two non-alcoholic wines". That is a category statement, which is what UNI's tiles already show. **The owner pitch gets stronger, not weaker:** UNI becomes one of the few places a venue's non-alcoholic offer is visible at all.

### 2.5 Producers lose consumer advertising, and aggregate data is exempt

Breweries that can't reach drinkers through advertising will care more about where in bars and restaurants their non-alcoholic lines sell. Information between businesses is outside the definition, and the venue-lists-are-never-sold-as-leads record already allows exactly this kind of product: counts, trends and area-level coverage, never a list of venues. It is a V3 item today. The bill makes it more valuable; it doesn't make it sooner, because it still needs traffic (§5 Q6).

### 2.6 The Suchy Styczeń window and the law's start date are the same weeks

`/suchy-styczen` is a permanent page because ranking takes weeks the season doesn't have. Traffic arrives in the last week of December. To catch January 2027, the site has to be public and indexed in the first half of December. That is when the President's decision will land, and two weeks before the law starts. **If the brand-safe switch is needed, it has to be built before launch, not after,** or the first indexed versions of the brand pages are the ones Google keeps showing.

## 3. Pivot options if the bill passes

Each option is something you could do. They combine: the recommendation is **A + B + C now, D later, and not E or F**.

### A. Brand-safe mode — recommended, build now and leave it off

A `shares_alcohol_brand` flag on **brands**, since the trademark belongs to the brand, set by hand. When the switch is on, every public rendering of a flagged brand's products goes through the category view the tiles already use: "Piwo bezalkoholowe · 3", or a generic style such as "lager 0,0%". `/marka/{slug}` and `/napoj/{slug}` for those brands disappear from the site and the sitemap. Owners and admins keep seeing full product names in the panels, because a closed audience isn't public.

**Not a single 60/100 score instead of the menu.** A number doesn't tell anyone what they can order tonight, nobody can check it, and the ordering formula behind it is still undecided.

**It keeps:** the full catalogue in the data, every independent brand by name, house drinks, shared recipes, and all category and district pages. **It costs:** the brand-name search traffic for those brands, and the visitor who wants Lech Free specifically, not just any 0% beer. **Effort:** a column in the brands create migration (no production database yet), `database/schema.md`, the rendering change, and tests that check no flagged brand name appears anywhere public. Behind a config switch like `UNI_OWNER_ACCESS`: off in `.env.example` until the bill is signed and the lawyer has answered.

### B. Move the way in through search from brands to categories and occasions — recommended

Brand pages were one way in among several. Category × district pages, the Warsaw category pages and `/suchy-styczen` already exist and don't name a brand. What's missing is **style pages that aren't brand pages**: "lager bezalkoholowy", "wino bezalkoholowe musujące", "gin bezalkoholowy", each with the counted link to venues. They answer the generic search, which is the larger one, and none of them depends on the bill's outcome. The one-ordering decision (07.10) still has to land first, because these are venue lists.

### C. Make more of what the bill doesn't touch — recommended

House drinks, shared recipes, non-alcoholic wine, kombucha and independent brands carry no alcohol trademark. Today the catalogue import starts with brands. When a venue is catalogued, house drinks and independent brands should get the same care as the beer fridge, because they would be the visible part of the menu if A is switched on. **One grey area:** a shared recipe named after a cocktail ("Mojito bezalkoholowe") could arguably be "a symbol that clearly refers to an alcoholic drink". It goes to the lawyer with the main question, not into a rule now.

### D. Bring producer aggregates forward — later, not now

See 2.5. It's the one new revenue line the bill strengthens. It stays V3, gated on traffic: an aggregate built on 30 venues and no visitors is worth nothing to a brewery. **What to do now:** nothing, except to keep `producers-stay-internal` as it is. Its "revisit when producers become the customer" condition is the trigger.

### E. Hobby mode — not recommended

That would mean launching, then stopping cataloguing and owner work and leaving the site to the search engines. **The bill isn't a reason for it,** since the revenue model doesn't depend on brands. The question it really asks is the old one: will venues pay for analytics? The bill doesn't answer that, but traffic will. **If you want a hobby/business decision, tie it to measured traction, not to the law** (§5 Q7), for example: six months after launch, with N organic visits a month and M owners claiming venues unasked.

### F. Keep the 07.09 position and show brand names regardless — not recommended

The 07.09 decision rested on nobody comparable ever having been prosecuted. Three things have changed since: the minimum fine triples, the beer exception that made 0.0 advertising ordinary goes, and the political attention is now on 0.0 specifically. The likely first targets are brewery campaigns, not a directory. But being the test case would cost more than the brand-name traffic is worth, and A can be switched off again if the lawyer says it isn't needed.

## 4. Reaching people without brand advertising

**Every channel here is checked against the standing rules:** faceless (no founder face or voice, no lifestyle content), traction before outreach (brand-outreach-waits-for-traction, 02.10), recovery and abstinence organisations are never a recruitment channel, no paid placement, and no public position on alcohol policy. **The bill adds one rule: lead with the category, the map or the count, never with a brand-sharing product.**

| # | Channel | Why it works without brands | Effort | When | Fit with the rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Search on categories, districts and occasions** (§3 B) | The generic query is the bigger one, and it's the whole of the Growth Strategy's primary channel | Building pages from data | Now, before launch | Fully inside |
| 2 | **`/suchy-styczen` for January 2027** | The one outside date of the year; the page is permanent and already live in the code | Launch by early December | **Hard date** | Fully inside |
| 3 | **Table cards and QR codes in venues** | Promotion inside on-premise venues stays allowed, and the card can point to the venue's own UNI page first | One print run, placed once | After the first owners | In the Growth Strategy already |
| 4 | **Owners linking their UNI page** (Instagram bio, Google profile, website) | A venue can't advertise a 0.0 brand, but it can say "see our non-alcoholic menu"; the share card already exists | None for UNI | After launch | Inside; keep the page category-led if A is on |
| 5 | **Faceless Instagram and TikTok** in map, count and screen-recording formats | Already the decided format; the bill removes the brand-led alternative | 3 posts a week | After launch | Inside (ADR-007, faceless-brand) |
| 6 | **Communities that already choose alcohol-free**: running clubs, parkrun, climbing walls, cycling groups, sober-rave nights (SobeRave has run in Warsaw), pregnancy and new-parent forums | They already have the need; UNI gives them a map | One message per group, from the brand | **Parked** until traction | Named in the brand-outreach record; recovery groups excluded |
| 7 | **A data report for the press**, e.g. "how many Warsaw venues serve non-alcoholic wine" | January 2027 coverage of the ban will ask where 0.0 went, and journalists need numbers | One report | Parked; the ban is a hook, not a trigger | **Only data, never comment on the law** (mission rule) |
| 8 | **Local Warsaw newsletters and city guides** | They need lists; "data for city guides" is already a parked idea | Per contact | Parked | Inside |
| 9 | **Event organisers and company HR** (team events, Christmas parties, weddings) | They have to plan for guests who don't drink | Outreach | Parked, needs a decision | New; it's B2B-ish, so check it against the mission first |
| 10 | **Posts on Warsaw subreddits and Facebook groups** | One-off reach to exactly the right city | One post | After launch | **Risky:** a founder's "I built this" post breaks faceless; only a brand-account post |

**What the bill rules out on top of the existing "not doing" list:** co-marketing with producers of brand-sharing 0.0s (it would now be illegal for them as well as against the mission), and any paid ad that shows such a product.

**Grants were checked and don't fit.** Municipal alcohol-prevention programmes (Warsaw's runs 2026–2029, funded from permit fees the bill doubles) fund NGOs through open calls, mostly for addiction treatment, schools and outreach. UNI isn't an NGO, and taking money from a prevention programme would read as a position on alcohol policy.

## 7. Deliberately not doing

- **No public statement about the bill, anywhere.** Not "we're the legal way to find 0.0", not "the ban is wrong". Speak only about what someone can order tonight.
- **No single score in place of the menu** (§3 A).
- **No hiding of independent brands, house drinks or recipes** to be safe. It buys no legal safety and takes information away from the person choosing.
- **No statute reference document, position paper or catalogue rule reasoned from law** in this repository (Q1).
- **No grant applications** to alcohol-prevention programmes (§4).
- **No selling of venue lists** to producers who've lost their advertising. The venue-lists record holds, bill or no bill.
